\begin{abstract}
General relativity describes gravity as the curvature of a single spacetime continuum, yet its reconciliation with quantum mechanics remains open: singularities, the continuum hypothesis, and the missing microscopic origin of curvature stand in the way. This paper develops the quantum sector of the Double Spacetime Theory (DST) introduced in the companion work. There spacetime is not a shared manifold but a pair of compactified Minkowski spacetimes attached to every massive particle and encoded in the 16-real-dimensional biquaternion algebra $\mathbb{H} \oplus \mathbb{H} \cong \mathrm{Cl}(3,1)$. Gravity is the torsional mismatch $J$ between the usual and the dual rotor, and on admissible configurations $|J|\le 3\pi^2/8$.

The dual generators act on $\mathbb{C}^2$ by $-i\sigma_a$, and the dual rotors are exactly the matrices of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$. A unit dual spinor is a point of the unit sphere; a dual rotor turns its spin vector by the full angle, the interference of $u$ with $U(\beta)u$ has intensity $\cos^2(\lVert\beta\rVert/4)$, and on that sphere the Robertson--Schrödinger relation for two spin axes is an equality. The Dirac matrices are the grade-1 generators of $\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)$, and the momentum-space Dirac equation is the first-order factor of the Minkowski quadratic. On the mass shell every dual spinor $u$ determines a solution, the pair $(u,iu)$ at rest. The equation is covariant under every total rotor, and every total rotor is uniquely a usual rotor followed by a dual rotor. The dual rotors are exactly the total rotors that fix the rest momentum, so the dual sector is the little group of a massive particle; usual rotors along $x$ and $z$ compose to a usual rotor followed by a dual rotation about $y$, the Thomas--Wigner rotation. Under $p_\mu\mapsto -i\partial_\mu$ the equation becomes the spacetime Dirac equation. Its plane waves carry the de Broglie phase, its current is conserved, and the time component of that current is the nonnegative density $\Psi^\dagger\Psi$ that Born's rule requires; the Killing self-pairing is indefinite and is not that density. Minimal coupling to an electromagnetic potential is gauge covariant. In the resulting second-order equation the magnetic field acts through the dual generators, identically on both Weyl blocks and with gyromagnetic ratio $2$, and the electric field through the usual generators. Spin precession in a magnetic field is a dual rotation. On Dirac spinors the dual map acts by $i\gamma^5$ and reverses the four-momentum. The dual spinor carries an antilinear quaternionic structure with square $-1$ that commutes with the dual rotors and reverses the spin, and the charge conjugation it defines reverses the charge and the momentum. Their composite, taken with the point reflection of spacetime, carries every solution to a solution of opposite charge in the reflected potential and keeps the momentum of every plane wave: the antiparticle is the charge conjugate read through the time reversal of the dual map. Two dual spinors form a joint state in the tensor product, and no local dual rotor changes its entanglement. The joint states fixed by every common dual rotor are the multiples of the singlet, which pairs every dual spinor with its quaternionic partner. Exchange splits the joint space into that singlet, of total spin $0$, and the symmetric states, of total spin $1$; two dual spinors on one ray cannot occupy the singlet. The orthogonal complement of that ray is the spin-reversed partner, and the two rays exhaust the space. The singlet correlation along two axes is $-a\cdot b$; no local operation on one particle changes the statistics of the other, and the CHSH combination reaches $2\sqrt2$, beyond the bound $2$ that holds whenever each outcome is fixed by variables of its own particle.

The algebra does not fix the dynamics. Equal-scale interference nodes run inward and do not reproduce the Bohr radii. The written opposite-sign particle action frees the mismatch and sources the common rapidity; its Jacobi integral is indefinite, and the harmonic stiffness neither restores a frequency nor selects a misalignment. The value of the mass, measurement, and the path integral remain geometric hypotheses, and the internal duality of the algebra is not ten-dimensional spacetime supersymmetry.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: An Algebraic Approach to Quantum Gravity}

The reconciliation of general relativity with quantum mechanics remains one of the most profound open problems in theoretical physics. Despite decades of effort, no consensus framework has emerged that is simultaneously consistent with the empirical successes of both theories while resolving their conceptual incompatibilities. Loop quantum gravity discretizes geometry but retains a background manifold and curvature as fundamental; string theory introduces extra dimensions and supersymmetry without direct observational support; asymptotic safety and causal dynamical triangulations struggle with the continuum limit and the origin of spacetime itself. All existing approaches share a common foundational assumption: the spacetime continuum hypothesis. Spacetime is presumed to exist as a smooth, differentiable manifold independent of matter, with gravity arising from its curvature sourced by energy-momentum.

This assumption, while extraordinarily successful at macroscopic scales, leads to severe difficulties at the quantum level: ultraviolet divergences on curved backgrounds, the black hole information paradox, cosmological singularities, and---most fundamentally---the absence of any physical mechanism explaining why or how matter should curve spacetime. Mach's principle remains unfulfilled; spacetime behaves as an autonomous entity rather than a relational structure determined by the distribution of matter.

In this paper we develop a different approach. The underlying algebra is the $16$-real-dimensional biquaternion algebra isomorphic to $\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)$; a background manifold and Riemann curvature are not required for the identities recorded below. The physical picture is the pair of rotors $R_\text{usual}$ and $R_\text{dual}$. Dual-sector kinematics is the representation on $\mathbb{C}^2$; gravitational phenomenology is torsional mismatch measured by $J=\frac12\sum_a(\alpha_a^2-\beta_a^2)$. Identification of $J$ with the teleparallel scalar, and Einstein--Hilbert equivalence, are taken from the TEGR literature after a rotor-to-tetrad dictionary is fixed.

The dual rotor at every rapidity is the Rodrigues matrix of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, and every matrix of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ arises in this way (Section~\ref{sec:quantumstates}). A unit dual spinor is a point of the unit sphere, a dual rotor turns its spin vector by the full angle, and the Robertson--Schrödinger relation for two spin axes is an equality on that sphere. The Dirac matrices of signature $(-,+,+,+)$ are the grade-1 generators of $\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)$; they obey $\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=2\eta^{\mu\nu}$ and are not the hyperbolic bivectors used for $P_{\mathrm{spin}}$. The momentum-space Dirac equation follows from the Clifford square of the Minkowski quadratic (Section~\ref{sec:dirac}). It is covariant under every total rotor, and the dual factor of a total rotor is the little group of the rest momentum, so spin is carried by the dual sector and momentum by the usual sector. Replacing $p_\mu$ by $-i\partial_\mu$ gives the Dirac equation on spacetime. Its conserved current has the nonnegative density that Born's rule requires, whereas the Killing self-pairing has signature $(3,3)$ and is not a probability~\cite{dst-d4l}. Minimal coupling to an electromagnetic potential is gauge covariant. In the coupled equation the magnetic field acts on the spinor through the dual generators, so the magnetic moment belongs to the dual sector and spin precession in a magnetic field is a dual rotation. The dual map acts on Dirac spinors by $i\gamma^5$ and reverses the four-momentum; composed with the charge conjugation supplied by the quaternionic structure of the dual spinor, it produces the antiparticle. For two particles the same quaternionic structure builds the singlet, the unique joint state that common dual rotation leaves fixed. Exchange splits the joint space into that singlet and the symmetric states of total spin $1$, and two dual spinors on one ray cannot occupy the singlet. The orthogonal complement of that ray is the spin-reversed partner, and the two rays exhaust the space. The correlations carry no signal, and they exceed every assignment of outcomes by particle-local variables, so entanglement is not a correlation of the dual phases of the two particles. Position operators and mass as torsion are not derived here.

Abandoning the continuum hypothesis also bounds the singularities of classical general relativity. The compactness of the dual spacetime discretizes the rotor angles, and on admissible configurations the algebraic ceiling $|J|\le 3\pi^2/8$ (equivalently $|J_{\rm norm}|\le 1$) forbids the unbounded curvature that would otherwise appear at $r=0$ or at the Big Bang, provided the configuration remains admissible. In the same picture event horizons are replaced by finite torsional layers that light traverses by resonance tunneling, and strong-field deviations from general relativity become observable in principle. Dual-only kinematics on the admissible cone determine when $J$ can be shielded or reversed and allow the dual budget to be routed across silent axes; whether laboratory fields supply that dual force is the experimental question of Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

Section~\ref{sec:spinor} constructs the square-$+1$ projectors of the algebra. Section~\ref{sec:quantumstates} represents the dual sector on $\mathbb{C}^2$, separates the Killing pairing from Born's rule, and records the spin vector, the $4\pi$ interference, and the spin uncertainty equality. Section~\ref{sec:dirac} derives the Dirac equation from the Clifford square: its on-shell solutions, the mass bilinear, the current and the spin along the momentum, the covariance under total rotors with the little group and the Thomas--Wigner rotation, the Gordon identity, the spacetime equation with its Born density, the coupling to an electromagnetic potential, and the antiparticle obtained from the dual map and charge conjugation. \section{Biquaternion Spinors and the Minimal Left Ideal}
\label{sec:spinor}

The biquaternion algebra $\mathbb{H} \oplus \mathbb{H}$, canonically isomorphic to the Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)$ of Minkowski spacetime with signature $(-,+,+,+)$, provides a natural and minimal realization of Dirac spinors. Unlike conventional approaches that introduce spinors as an external representation space, here the spinor emerges directly as an element of the algebra itself.

A primitive idempotent that projects onto an irreducible representation must be built from generators that square to $+1$. If $g$ satisfies $g^2=+1$, then
\[
\frac{1+g}{2}
\]
is idempotent. Write $j:=e_0 e_1$ for the hyperbolic generator of axis~$0$ (so $j^2=+1$). This $j$ is \emph{not} the time vector $\gamma^0=e_0$, which squares to $-1$ in signature $(-,+,+,+)$ (Section~\ref{sec:dirac}). The working projectors are
\[
P_{\mathrm{spin}} = \frac{1+j}{2}=\frac{1+e_0 e_1}{2}
\]
and the complementary spatial chirality pair
\[
P_L = \frac{1-e_1}{2},\qquad P_R = \frac{1+e_1}{2}
\]
(with $e_1^2=+1$). They satisfy $P_L^2=P_L$, $P_R^2=P_R$, and $P_L+P_R=1$. The pair $P_L$, $P_R$ organizes a chiral split in the algebra; it does not by itself yield the weak interaction of the Standard Model.

The product
\[
P = P_{\mathrm{spin}}\, P_R
\]
is not idempotent: $e_0 e_1$ and $e_1$ anticommute, the factors do not commute, and $(P_{\mathrm{spin}}P_R)^2\neq P_{\mathrm{spin}}P_R$. A commuting square-$+1$ pair is $e_1$ with the off-axis boost $e_0 e_2$; their product projector is idempotent and generates a left ideal of real dimension eight. That ideal is the direct sum of the four-dimensional Minkowski core $\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)\,P$ and a copy along the null generator $e_4$; the copy is a proper $G(3,1,1)$-submodule, so the eight-dimensional ideal is reducible as a PGA-module. The match with the real dimension of a Dirac spinor $\mathbb{C}^4$ is this null doubling, not an identification with an irreducible Dirac module. Irreducibility of the Minkowski core as a $\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)$-module is not claimed.

By contrast, the naive formulae built from the $\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)$ pseudoscalar $i$ alone,
\[
\frac{1\pm i}{2}\qquad(i^2=-1),
\]
are not idempotent: $\bigl((1-i)/2\bigr)^2=-i/2$. Composites such as $i\gamma_1\gamma_2$ that square to $+1$ remain admissible building blocks; the bare pseudoscalar does not.

A general element of a candidate spinor space takes the explicit form
\[
\psi = \bigl( a_0 + a_1 i + b_1 kI + b_2 kJ + b_3 kK + c_1 jI + c_2 jJ + c_3 jK \bigr) P,
\]
where $a_0,a_1,b_i,c_i \in \mathbb{R}$ and $P$ is a square-$+1$ projector (not $P_{\mathrm{spin}}P_R$). Identifying the pseudoscalar $i$ with the imaginary unit of quantum mechanics is a dictionary, not a proof that the carrier is the Dirac module. The dual map $X \mapsto X i$ still reverses time orientation, and on Dirac spinors it reverses the four-momentum (Section~\ref{subsec:antiparticle}); the algebraic projectors themselves are $P_L$ and $P_R$ above, not $(1\pm i)/2$.

The action of rotors on algebra elements is left multiplication. Same-axis usual and dual generators commute; off-axis pairs need not. In particular $\Gamma_a$ does not commute with every usual-sector generator $i\Gamma_b$, so the order of the factors in $R_{\mathrm{total}}=R_{\mathrm{usual}}R_{\mathrm{dual}}$ matters off-axis. With that order fixed the factorization is an identity in the $\mathbb{C}^2$ representation: every total rotor is a usual rotor followed by a dual rotor, and both factors are unique (Section~\ref{sec:dirac}).

The bilinear $\bar{\psi}\psi\propto\operatorname{Tr}(\Omega_{\mathrm{biv}})$ and the emergence of the full fermionic Standard Model from the $16$-dimensional algebra are not established here. What follows from the algebra is the projector calculus above.

\section{Quantum States as Dual-Spacetime Rotations}
\label{sec:quantumstates}

The dual rotor
\[
R_\text{dual} = \exp\left( \sum_{a=1}^3 \frac{\phi_a}{2} \Gamma_a \right),
\]
where $\Gamma_1 = I$, $\Gamma_2 = J$, $\Gamma_3 = K$ satisfy the quaternion relations $\Gamma_a^2 = -1$ and $IJ=K$, is represented on $\mathbb{C}^2$ by $-i\sigma_a$. The alternative dictionary $I\leftrightarrow i\sigma$ is incompatible with $IJ=K$.

At every dual rapidity $\beta$ the exponential is the Rodrigues formula
\[
U(\beta)
=\cos(\lVert\beta\rVert/2)\,I
+\sin(\lVert\beta\rVert/2)\,n\cdot(-i\sigma),
\]
with $n=\beta/\lVert\beta\rVert$ when $\beta\neq 0$ and $U(0)=I$. That matrix is unitary of determinant $1$, and conversely every unitary matrix of determinant $1$ is a dual rotor: the dual rotors are exactly the group $\mathrm{SU}(2)$. The inverse is the opposite rapidity, so a relative dual rotor is a product in the group: its character records the alignment of the two dual axes, and the noncommuting residue is a traceless tilt. A $2\pi$ dual rotation about a computational axis is $-I$, and along a fixed axis the dual angle is periodic modulo $4\pi$, as the compactness of the dual spacetime requires. That period is visible in interference: the intensity of $u+U(\beta)u$ is $4\cos^2(\lVert\beta\rVert/4)\,\lVert u\rVert^2$ for every dual spinor, so a $2\pi$ dual rotation, which sends $u$ to $-u$, is not the identity of the superposition.

A unit dual spinor is a point of the unit sphere. Its spin vector $r_k=\langle u,\sigma_k u\rangle$ is real, satisfies $\langle u,\sigma\cdot a\,u\rangle=a\cdot r$, and has $|r|=1$. The outcome $s=\pm1$ along a unit axis $a$ occurs with probability $(1+s\,a\cdot r)/2$. A dual rotor $U(\beta)$ turns $r$ by the full angle $\lVert\beta\rVert$ about $\beta$, and two matrices of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ turn every axis alike exactly when they differ by sign.

The dual generators supply the spin commutators $[\sigma\cdot a,\sigma\cdot b]=2i\,\sigma\cdot(a\times b)$. On a unit dual spinor the Robertson--Schrödinger relation is an equality: $\mathrm{Var}(\sigma\cdot a)\,\mathrm{Var}(\sigma\cdot b)$ equals $(a\cdot b-\langle\sigma\cdot a\rangle\langle\sigma\cdot b\rangle)^2+\langle\sigma\cdot(a\times b)\rangle^2$, so the bound $|\langle[\sigma\cdot a,\sigma\cdot b]\rangle|^2/4\le\mathrm{Var}(\sigma\cdot a)\,\mathrm{Var}(\sigma\cdot b)$ holds with the covariance as the exact excess. Along three perpendicular unit axes the variances sum to $2$. Position operators, the canonical commutation relations of position with momentum, and an uncertainty bound $\Delta\phi\cdot\Delta\theta\ge 1/2$ for orbital angles remain geometric hypotheses. Momentum enters spacetime only through the correspondence $p_\mu\mapsto -i\partial_\mu$ of Section~\ref{sec:dirac}.

The probabilistic interpretation is not the Killing self-pairing. The identification
\[
|\psi|^2 \propto \tfrac12 B(\Omega_{\mathrm{biv}},\Omega_{\mathrm{biv}})
\]
fails: the Killing pairing has signature $(3,3)$ and is negative on pure elliptic configurations. Self-overlap is therefore not a probability. Born's rule belongs to the Hilbert layer and is not a consequence of $J$~\cite{dst-d4l}. The nonnegative conserved density it requires is the time component of the Dirac current, $\Psi^\dagger\Psi$ (Section~\ref{sec:dirac}).

Measurement and collapse as torsional synchronization are likewise left open; the kinematics of two entangled dual spinors is fixed in Section~\ref{sec:entanglement}. Dual-sector kinematics in this paper means the $\mathbb{C}^2$ representation of the cyclic generators.

\section{The Dirac Equation from the Clifford Square}
\label{sec:dirac}

\subsection{Clifford relations and the momentum-space equation}

The Dirac matrices of signature $(-,+,+,+)$ are the grade-1 generators $\gamma^\mu$ of $\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)$. They obey
\[
\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=2\eta^{\mu\nu},\qquad
\eta=\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1).
\]
In particular $(\gamma^0)^2=-1$. The hyperbolic generator $j=e_0 e_1$ used for $P_{\mathrm{spin}}$ squares to $+1$, so $\gamma^0\neq j$, and the dictionary $\gamma^0\leftrightarrow j$, $\gamma^i\leftrightarrow kI,\ldots$ is replaced by this grade-1 basis.

For a four-momentum $p$ the grade-1 element $\gamma(p)=\sum_\mu p_\mu\gamma^\mu$ squares to the Minkowski quadratic $Q(p)=-p_0^2+p_1^2+p_2^2+p_3^2$, both in $\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)$ and as a matrix on $\mathbb{C}^4$. The two chiral slashes of the Weyl representation multiply to the same quadratic. The mass shell $Q(p)=-m^2$ is therefore the vanishing of $(\gamma(p)-im)(\gamma(p)+im)$, and a nonzero spinor with
\[
\gamma(p)\Psi=im\Psi
\]
lies on that shell: this is the Dirac equation in momentum space. In the Weyl representation $\gamma(p)$ is off-diagonal, so vanishing mass decouples the two chiral blocks while a nonzero mass mixes them. Chirality $\gamma^5$ sends a solution of mass $m$ to a solution of mass $-m$.

\subsection{On-shell solutions and the longitudinal boost}

On the mass shell with $m\neq 0$ an arbitrary dual spinor $u$ determines a solution: the upper Weyl block is $u$, and the lower block is fixed by the chiral slash of $p$. At rest momentum $(m,0,0,0)$ this is the pair $\Psi=(u,iu)$. Dual rotation of $u$ remains a solution at the same four-momentum: the dual rotor acts internally on the Weyl parameter.

A longitudinal boost of rest momentum,
\[
p=(m\cosh\alpha,\,0,\,0,\,m\sinh\alpha),
\]
stays on the shell, and at that momentum the Weyl construction is the Dirac-boosted rest pair. The boost acts by the hyperbolic Rodrigues factor
\[
V(\alpha)=\cosh(\alpha/2)\,I+\sinh(\alpha/2)\,\sigma_z
\]
on the upper Weyl block and by $V(-\alpha)$ on the lower: the rest pair $(u,iu)$ is sent to the on-shell solution whose Weyl parameter is $V(\alpha)u$. Dual rotation about the boost axis commutes with $V(\alpha)$, so internal dual rotation and the longitudinal boost may be taken in either order. Dual rotation about a perpendicular axis does not commute with $V(\alpha)$.

\subsection{Overlap, mass bilinear, and current}

The $\mathbb{C}^4$ overlap of two rest-frame solutions that differ by a dual rotation is twice the dual-rotor amplitude. A longitudinal boost of rapidity $\alpha$ multiplies that overlap by $\cosh\alpha$, for every dual rotation, so the overlap agrees with its rest-frame value only at vanishing rapidity. It is a Euclidean identity on $\mathbb{C}^4$, not a spacetime inner product.

The invariant pairing is the mass bilinear $\bar\Psi\Phi=\Psi^\dagger(-i\gamma^0)\Phi$. On two rest-frame solutions it coincides with the Euclidean overlap, and therefore with twice the dual-spinor pairing, and a longitudinal boost leaves it unchanged. The Dirac current built from the same matrix is
\[
j^\mu=\Psi^\dagger(-i\gamma^0)\gamma^\mu\Phi
=i\,\bar\Psi\Phi\,(\cosh\alpha,\,0,\,0,\,-\sinh\alpha)
\]
along the boosted pair. In this representation $(-i\gamma^0)\gamma^0=i$, so the time component is $i$ times the Euclidean overlap and carries the factor $\cosh\alpha$. The transverse components vanish. The Clifford contraction of the current with the boosted momentum equals $im\bar\Psi\Phi$, and $mj^0=i\bar\Psi\Phi\,p^0$, $-mj^3=i\bar\Psi\Phi\,p^3$, so for $m\neq 0$ the combination $(j^0,-j^3)$ is parallel to $p$.

The eigenrays of $\sigma_z$ label the spin of this solution along the momentum. $V(\alpha)$ multiplies the eigenvalue $+1$ by $e^{\alpha/2}$ on the upper block and by $e^{-\alpha/2}$ on the lower, and both blocks remain in that ray. The eigenvalue $-1$ exchanges the two factors, so at large positive rapidity the first spin lies mostly in the upper block and the second mostly in the lower. The mass bilinear between the two rays vanishes at every longitudinal rapidity, and so does every component of the current between them. A dual rotation by $\theta$ about the boost axis multiplies the eigenvalue $+1$ by $e^{-i\theta/2}$ and the eigenvalue $-1$ by $e^{i\theta/2}$; a dual rotation of angle $\pi$ about a perpendicular axis exchanges the two rays.

\subsection{Total-rotor covariance and the little group}

The longitudinal boost is one instance of a covariance under the whole total-rotor group. In the $\mathbb{C}^2$ representation a total rotor is a complex matrix $M$ of determinant one. A four-momentum is the Hermitian matrix $\hat p=p_0I+\sum_a p_a\sigma_a$, whose determinant is $-Q(p)$, and $M$ acts on it by $\hat p\mapsto M\hat pM^\dagger$; this defines the Lorentz action $\Lambda(M)$ on momenta. On a Dirac spinor $M$ acts by $S(M)$: by $M$ on the upper Weyl block and by $(M^\dagger)^{-1}$ on the lower. The action preserves $Q(p)$ and intertwines both chiral slashes, so it sends every solution of mass $m$ at $p$ to a solution of the same mass at $\Lambda(M)p$. The mass bilinear is invariant, and the Clifford contraction of the current with a four-vector is unchanged when the vector is transformed together with the spinors. A dual rotor is unitary and acts by the same matrix on both blocks. A usual rotor $V(\alpha)=\exp(\alpha\cdot\sigma/2)$ is Hermitian and acts on the lower block by its inverse, as $V(-\alpha)$ does for the longitudinal boost.

Every total rotor is a usual rotor followed by a dual rotor, $M=V(\alpha)U(\beta)$, and both factors are unique: $V(\alpha)$ is the positive square root of $MM^\dagger$, and $U(\beta)=V(\alpha)^{-1}M$. A total rotor fixes the rest momentum $(m,0,0,0)$ with $m\neq 0$ exactly when it is a dual rotor. The dual sector is therefore the little group of a massive particle, and its spin degrees of freedom are those of the dual spinor $u$. The momentum is carried by the usual factor: every momentum on the forward mass shell is reached from rest by exactly one usual rotor. Usual rotors along perpendicular axes do not commute, and their product is not a usual rotor: the product of usual rotors along $x$ and $z$ is a usual rotor followed by a nontrivial dual rotation about $y$. This dual factor is the Thomas--Wigner rotation. In double spacetime it is an internal rotation of the dual spinor, produced by composing usual rotors.

\subsection{Gordon identity and the Born density}

The current of two solutions at one momentum is fixed by that momentum. The adjoint matrix $-i\gamma^0$ is Hermitian and every $(-i\gamma^0)\gamma^\mu$ is anti-Hermitian, so the anticommutator $\{\gamma(p),\gamma^\mu\}=2p^\mu$ yields the Gordon identity
\[
m\,j^\mu=-i\,p^\mu\,\bar\Psi\Phi,\qquad p^\mu=\eta^{\mu\nu}p_\nu,
\]
for any two solutions $\Psi$ and $\Phi$ of mass $m$ at $p$. The longitudinal current above is its instance along $z$. The time component reads $m\,\Psi^\dagger\Phi=p_0\,\bar\Psi\Phi$: at every on-shell momentum the Euclidean overlap is $p_0/m$ times the invariant bilinear, and $\cosh\alpha$ is that ratio for the longitudinal boost.

For a single spinor the current is $i$ times a real four-vector $J^\mu$ with $J^0=\Psi^\dagger\Psi$. On a nonzero solution with $m\neq 0$ the component $p_0$ does not vanish, $\bar\Psi\Psi=(m/p_0)\,\Psi^\dagger\Psi$ is real and nonzero, and $J$ has Minkowski square $-(\bar\Psi\Psi)^2$. The current is therefore timelike and parallel to the momentum, and its invariant length is the mass bilinear; with $m>0$ and $p_0>0$ that bilinear is positive and does not exceed $\Psi^\dagger\Psi$. A total rotor that carries the rest momentum to $p$ multiplies the density $\Psi^\dagger\Psi$ of a rest solution by $p_0/m$, while every dual rotor leaves it unchanged. The density is fixed by the little group and changes only with the usual factor.

\subsection{The spacetime equation}

Replacing $p_\mu$ by $-i\partial_\mu$ turns the momentum-space equation into the Dirac equation on spacetime,
\[
\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\Psi=-m\Psi.
\]
A plane wave $\Psi(x)=e^{ip_\mu x^\mu}u$ solves it exactly when its amplitude $u$ solves the momentum-space equation at $p$. The solutions of one mass form a complex vector space, so every finite superposition of on-shell plane waves of mass $m$ is again a solution. The phase $p_\mu x^\mu$ is the de Broglie phase, and the amplitude is the on-shell spinor assembled from a dual spinor. For any two differentiable solutions of the same real mass the current $j^\mu=\bar\Psi\gamma^\mu\Phi$ is conserved,
\[
\partial_\mu j^\mu=0,
\]
and between two plane waves of one momentum it is uniform. For $\Phi=\Psi$ the time component $j^0=i\Psi^\dagger\Psi$ makes $\rho=\Psi^\dagger\Psi$ a density that is nonnegative, vanishes only where $\Psi$ does, and obeys the continuity equation $\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot J=0$ with the real spatial current $J^k=-ij^k$. This is the density that Born's rule requires. It is carried by the Dirac current, not by the Killing self-pairing, which is indefinite.

The total-rotor covariance extends to spacetime. The field
\[
\Psi'(x)=S(M)\,\Psi\bigl(\Lambda(M)^{\mathsf T}x\bigr)
\]
solves $\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\Psi'=-m\Psi'$ whenever $\Psi$ does. The transposed action on the argument is fixed by the phase: $p_\mu x^\mu$ evaluated at $\Lambda(M)^{\mathsf T}x$ is $(\Lambda(M)p)_\mu x^\mu$, so the plane wave $e^{ip_\mu x^\mu}u$ goes to the plane wave of momentum $\Lambda(M)p$ with amplitude $S(M)u$. The de Broglie phase is transported by the usual factor; a dual rotor fixes the rest momentum and leaves the phase of a rest-frame plane wave in place.

\subsection{Electromagnetic coupling}

An electromagnetic potential $A_\mu$ enters through the covariant derivative $D_\mu=\partial_\mu-ieA_\mu$, and the coupled equation is
\[
\gamma^\mu D_\mu\Psi=-m\Psi .
\]
It is gauge covariant: for every differentiable $\chi$, the pair $\Psi\mapsto e^{ie\chi}\Psi$, $A_\mu\mapsto A_\mu+\partial_\mu\chi$ carries solutions to solutions. The gauge factor is a scalar $\mathrm{U}(1)$ phase. It commutes with every total rotor and is neither a dual rotor nor its character. In a constant potential $a$ the plane wave $e^{ip_\mu x^\mu}u$ solves the coupled equation exactly when $u$ solves the momentum-space equation at the kinetic momentum $p-ea$: the canonical momentum $p$ carries the phase, and the kinetic momentum carries the mass shell and the spinor assembled from a dual spinor. The current $\bar\Psi\gamma^\mu\Phi$ is unchanged by a gauge transformation of both spinors, and for real mass, charge, and potential it remains conserved on solutions of the coupled equation. The Born density therefore survives the coupling.

For twice-differentiable spinors the covariant derivatives fail to commute by the field strength,
\[
[D_\mu,D_\nu]\Psi=-ieF_{\mu\nu}\Psi,\qquad F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu .
\]
Applying $\gamma^\nu D_\nu$ to the coupled equation and splitting $\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu$ into its symmetric and antisymmetric parts, every twice-differentiable solution obeys
\[
\eta^{\mu\nu}D_\mu D_\nu\Psi-\frac{ie}{2}\,F_{\mu\nu}\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\Psi=m^2\Psi .
\]
At zero charge this is the Klein--Gordon equation $\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\Psi=m^2\Psi$ for every component of a free solution. The field-strength term is block diagonal in the Weyl representation. With the magnetic field $\mathbf B=(F_{23},F_{31},F_{12})$ and the electric field $E_k=F_{k0}$, it acts as
\[
e\,\sigma\cdot(\mathbf B+i\mathbf E)\ \text{on the upper block},\qquad
e\,\sigma\cdot(\mathbf B-i\mathbf E)\ \text{on the lower block}.
\]
The magnetic part is the same on both blocks. Since $\sigma\cdot\mathbf B=i\sum_a B_a(-i\sigma_a)$ and $-i\sigma_a$ are the dual generators, the magnetic field acts on the spinor through the dual sector, in the way a dual rotor acts by one matrix on both blocks. The electric part changes sign between the blocks, as the generators of a usual rotor do. The magnetic field therefore couples to the spin carried by the little group, and the electric field to the boost carried by the usual sector. With $\Pi_k=-iD_k$ the spatial derivatives and the magnetic term combine into $-(\sigma\cdot\Pi)^2$, because $(\sigma\cdot\Pi)^2=\Pi^2-e\,\sigma\cdot\mathbf B$ follows from the commutator. The magnetic moment thus enters with gyromagnetic ratio $2$, the value fixed by the first-order equation.

\subsection{The dual map, charge conjugation, and the antiparticle}
\label{subsec:antiparticle}

The dual map $X\mapsto Xi$ multiplies by the pseudoscalar $i=e_0e_1e_2e_3$. On Dirac spinors that pseudoscalar is
\[
\omega=\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3=i\gamma^5,
\]
which is $+i$ on the upper Weyl block and $-i$ on the lower, so $\omega^2=-1$. It anticommutes with every $\gamma^\mu$ and commutes with every total rotor $S(M)$. It therefore carries a solution of mass $m$ at $p$ to a solution of the same mass at $-p$. On spinors the reversal of time orientation by the dual map is thus the reversal of the four-momentum, and with it of the sign of the energy. At rest it sends the pair $(u,iu)$ to $i(u,-iu)$, the rest solution at $(-m,0,0,0)$. It leaves the current and the density $\Psi^\dagger\Psi$ unchanged and reverses the sign of the mass bilinear. On spacetime the field $\omega\Psi(-x)$ solves the coupled equation of the same charge in the potential $-A_\mu(-x)$, the image of $A_\mu$ under the point reflection $x\mapsto -x$, and it carries the plane wave of momentum $p$ to the plane wave of momentum $-p$.

The dual spinor carries a second structure. The map
\[
Ju=i\sigma_y\,\bar u
\]
is antilinear with $J^2=-1$, preserves the norm, and makes $Ju$ orthogonal to $u$. It commutes with every dual rotor and reverses the spin along every axis, $J(\sigma\cdot n\,u)=-\sigma\cdot n\,Ju$, so it is the time reversal of a spin-$\tfrac12$ state, and with it $\mathbb{C}^2$ is a quaternionic line on which the dual rotors act. For a total rotor it exchanges the actions on the two Weyl blocks, $J(Mu)=(M^\dagger)^{-1}Ju$. Charge conjugation
\[
\Psi^c=(-Jl,\,Ju)\qquad\text{for }\Psi=(u,l)
\]
is therefore an antilinear involution that commutes with every $\gamma^\mu$ and every total rotor. Because it is antilinear it reverses the sign of $im$, so it is a bijection between the solutions of mass $m$ at $p$ and at $-p$. In a potential it reverses the charge: $\Psi^c$ solves the equation of charge $-e$ in the same potential $A_\mu$, and the plane wave $e^{ip_\mu x^\mu}u$ goes to the negative-frequency wave $e^{-ip_\mu x^\mu}u^c$. It preserves the density and the real current of a single spinor and reverses the sign of the mass bilinear.

Neither operation alone produces a positive-energy solution of opposite charge. Charge conjugation reverses the charge and the energy, and the dual map reverses the energy and keeps the charge. Their composite, the antiparticle
\[
\Psi\mapsto\omega\Psi^c,
\]
commutes with every total rotor and carries a solution of mass $m$ at $p$ to a solution of the same mass at the same $p$. For a single spinor it keeps the density, the current, and the mass bilinear. On spacetime the field $\omega\Psi^c(-x)$ solves the equation of charge $-e$ in the point-reflected potential $-A_\mu(-x)$, and it carries the plane wave of momentum $p$ to the plane wave of the same momentum with amplitude $\omega u^c$. At rest the antiparticle of $(u,iu)$ is $-(Ju,iJu)$, the rest pair of the dual spinor $Ju$, with the spin reversed along every axis. The composite is antilinear and reverses all four coordinates, as the CPT operation of the Dirac equation does.

This is the reading of Stueckelberg and Feynman~\cite{stueckelberg1941,feynman1949}: the charge conjugate is a negative-frequency solution of opposite charge, a particle running backward in time, and reversing the time orientation turns it into a positive-energy solution of opposite charge running forward. In double spacetime both steps are already in the algebra. The charge is reversed by the quaternionic structure of the dual spinor, and the time orientation by the dual map. The antiparticle is the charge conjugate read through the dual spacetime.

\section{Two Dual Spinors and Entanglement}
\label{sec:entanglement}

The spin of a massive particle is carried by its dual spinor, the little-group parameter of Section~\ref{sec:dirac}. Two particles therefore carry two dual spinors, and a joint state is a vector of $\mathbb{C}^2\otimes\mathbb{C}^2$. Written as the array $\Psi_{ij}$ of amplitudes of $e_i\otimes e_j$, it is acted on by a rotor $A$ on the first particle and a rotor $B$ on the second as
\[
\Psi\mapsto A\,\Psi\,B^{\mathsf T},
\]
and the product states $u\otimes v$ are the arrays $u_iv_j$.

The antisymmetric invariant $\det\Psi$ separates the product states from the rest. A pair of rotors multiplies it by $\det A\det B$, so it is unchanged when each particle is turned by its own total rotor, usual or dual. It vanishes exactly on product states, and
\[
2\,|\det\Psi|\le\lVert\Psi\rVert^2 .
\]
Dual rotors are unitary and also keep the norm, so the ratio $2|\det\Psi|/\lVert\Psi\rVert^2$, which is $0$ on product states and at most $1$, is unchanged by independent dual rotations of the two particles. No local dual rotor creates or removes entanglement.

The joint states left fixed by every common dual rotor $U\otimes U$ form a single line, spanned by the singlet
\[
\varepsilon=e_0\otimes e_1-e_1\otimes e_0 .
\]
A common total rotor $M\otimes M$ multiplies $\varepsilon$ by $\det M=1$, so the singlet is fixed by the usual factor as well. It is not a product, since $\det\varepsilon=1$, and it attains the bound above. The singlet is built from the quaternionic structure $J$ of Section~\ref{subsec:antiparticle}: for every unit dual spinor $u$,
\[
Ju\otimes u-u\otimes Ju=\varepsilon .
\]
The right-hand side does not depend on $u$. The singlet pairs each dual spinor with its spin-reversed partner and singles out no axis. Its overlap with a product state $u\otimes v$ is $-\langle Ju,v\rangle$. That overlap vanishes when the two spins are parallel, and for the reversed partner $v=Ju$ it is $-\lVert u\rVert^2$.

On a unit joint state each particle has polarization $\sqrt{1-C^2}$ with $C=2|\det\Psi|$: a product leaves each factor with its own spin vector, and the singlet leaves neither particle a direction of its own. Exchange of the two particles, $\Psi\mapsto\Psi^{\mathsf T}$, commutes with every common rotor. It splits the joint space into the line of the singlet, on which the total spin squared vanishes, and the symmetric states, on which that square is $2$. The orthogonal complement of the ray through a nonzero dual spinor $u$ is the ray through $Ju$. The partner has the same norm and the opposite spin vector, and the two rays exhaust the dual spinor space, so no third ray is orthogonal to both. Two dual spinors on the same ray carry one spin vector. Their antisymmetrized product vanishes, so they occupy only the symmetric state of total spin $1$. The singlet is the joint state of a ray with its reverse. That is the exclusion of a repeated dual spinor. It is not the spin-statistics theorem, and spatial overlap of the repeated ray remains the repulsive torsional layer.

A spin measurement along a unit axis $a$ is the pair of projectors $(1\pm\sigma\cdot a)/2$. In the normalized singlet each particle alone has vanishing spin mean along every axis. The correlation of measurements along $a$ on the first particle and along $b$ on the second is
\[
E(a,b)=\langle\sigma\cdot a\otimes\sigma\cdot b\rangle=-a\cdot b .
\]
The outcomes $s,t=\pm1$ occur with probability $(1-st\,a\cdot b)/4$, so along axes at angle $\theta$ the two outcomes are opposite with probability $\cos^2(\theta/2)$. The angle enters at half its value, as it does in the dual rotor $U(\beta)$ of Section~\ref{sec:quantumstates}.

No local action on one particle is visible at the other. For every joint state, a dual rotor applied to the first particle leaves every expectation of the second unchanged. So does the choice of the axis measured on the first particle: summing over its two outcomes returns the statistics of the second particle alone, whatever the axis. The correlations of an entangled pair carry no signal.

They are nevertheless not correlations of particle-local values. Suppose each outcome is fixed in advance by variables of its own particle, such as its dual phases, and that these variables are distributed over the pair in any way. Then the combination
\[
S=E(a,b)+E(a,b')+E(a',b)-E(a',b')
\]
of Clauser, Horne, Shimony, and Holt obeys $|S|\le 2$~\cite{bell1964,chsh1969}. For the singlet, with $a$ and $a'$ perpendicular and $b$, $b'$ their two bisectors, $S=-2\sqrt2$, and no choice of unit axes gives the singlet a larger $|S|$. The correlation that Einstein, Podolsky, and Rosen took as a sign of incompleteness~\cite{epr1935} is in double spacetime a property of the joint dual state. It is not a pair of correlated dual phases, one carried by each particle. The singlet is the joint state that common dual rotation leaves unchanged, and in it neither particle has a spin direction of its own.

\section{Unified Dynamics of Quantum Mechanics and Gravity}
\label{sec:dynamics}

The rotor Lagrangian is a model for the six rapidities, not a deduction from unitarity of the rotors. Orbital Schr\"odinger evolution, measurement as synchronization, the torsional reading of entanglement, and the path integral below remain geometric hypotheses; the spin part of Schr\"odinger evolution is a dual rotor. In that picture, dual-rotor kinematics is the quantum side and usual--dual misalignment is the gravitational side.

Consider a single massive particle whose complete rotor is $R_\text{total} = R_\text{usual} R_\text{dual}$. The written effective action for the intrinsic rotor angles is
\[
S_\text{particle} = \int \left[ \frac{1}{2} \sum_{a=1}^3 \dot{\phi}_a^2 - \frac{1}{2} \sum_{a=1}^3 \dot{\theta}_a^2 - \frac{m}{2} \sum_{a=1}^3 (\phi_a - \theta_a)^2 \right] dt.
\]
The opposite signs of the kinetic terms are part of the written model. They are intended to reflect the boost-like (hyperbolic) character of the usual sector and the rotation-like (elliptic) character of the dual sector; they are not deduced from unitarity of the rotors. The last term is the oscillator potential of Section~\ref{sec:mass}, not the parameter-space Killing quadratic of the main paper.

On one axis the density is equivalently the mixed form
\[
L=\tfrac12\dot\sigma\dot\delta-\tfrac m2\delta^2,
\]
with common rapidity $\sigma=\phi+\theta$ and mismatch $\delta=\phi-\theta$. The Euler--Lagrange equations are
\[
\ddot{\phi}_a = -m (\phi_a - \theta_a), \qquad \ddot{\theta}_a = -m (\phi_a - \theta_a).
\]
Both angles accelerate together. The mismatch is therefore free, $\ddot\delta_a=0$, and what the potential sources is the common rapidity, $\ddot\sigma_a=-2m\delta_a$. On one axis the general solution is the affine lag $\delta(t)=\delta_0+\nu t$ together with the cubic common rapidity
\[
\sigma(t)=\sigma_0+\dot\sigma_0\,t-m\delta_0\,t^2-\frac{m\nu}{3}\,t^3.
\]
A frozen lag accelerates $\sigma$ uniformly; a nonzero rate makes the acceleration itself linear in time. The mass, written as a stiffness of $\delta$, is a force on $\sigma$ and does not restore a frequency~\cite{dst-dynamic-equation}.

The Jacobi integral
\[
E=\tfrac12\dot\phi^2-\tfrac12\dot\theta^2+\tfrac m2\delta^2
=\tfrac12\dot\sigma\dot\delta+\tfrac m2\delta^2
\]
is conserved along those equations. Along the motion above it keeps its initial value $\tfrac12\nu\dot\sigma_0+\tfrac m2\delta_0^2$: the quadratic growth of $\dot\sigma$ cancels the quadratic growth of the potential, so $E$ stays fixed while the rapidity runs away. The integral is indefinite: a purely dual velocity at vanishing lag already yields $E<0$. Conservation of an unbounded quadratic does not select a frequency.

A neighbouring system sometimes written alongside the action,
\[
\ddot{\phi}_a = m (\phi_a - \theta_a), \qquad -\ddot{\theta}_a = m (\phi_a - \theta_a),
\]
makes the mismatch run away, $\ddot\delta_a=2m\delta_a$. It is not an oscillator.

An oscillator appears only if both kinetic signs are taken positive. The density $\tfrac12\dot\phi^2+\tfrac12\dot\theta^2-\tfrac m2(\phi-\theta)^2$ yields $\ddot\delta+2m\delta=0$, at frequency $\sqrt{2m}$ in units $\hbar=c=1$, while the common rapidity is free. The general solution is $\delta(t)=A\cos(\sqrt{2m}\,t)+B\sin(\sqrt{2m}\,t)$, with $\delta(t)^2\le A^2+B^2$, and its Jacobi integral equals $\tfrac14\dot\sigma_0^2+\tfrac m2(A^2+B^2)$. For $m\ge 0$ that integral is nonnegative, so conservation does bound the motion of $\delta$. The frequency $\sqrt{2m}$ and the Compton frequency $m$ meet only at the isolated values $m=0$ and $m=2$, and the model is not the written action. The neighbouring sign pattern $\ddot\delta=2m\delta$, at the same rate, is instead the unbounded hyperbolic motion $A\cosh(\sqrt{2m}\,t)+B\sinh(\sqrt{2m}\,t)$.

The written action therefore supplies no harmonic bridge to quantum theory. Identifying the rate $\nu$ of free lag with $E/\hbar$ remains a reading; the accumulated lag may then be written
\[
\int \delta_a \, dt \propto \frac{E t - \mathbf{p} \cdot \mathbf{x}}{\hbar},
\]
the de Broglie phase of the matter wave~\cite{dst-debroglie}. The same phase carries the plane-wave solutions of the spacetime Dirac equation of Section~\ref{sec:dirac}; what remains a reading is its identification with the free lag. The scalar $\mathrm{U}(1)$ factor built from $\delta$ is distinct from the real character of the dual rotor and from its spinor matrix element.

The Schr\"odinger equation is readable as the unitary evolution of the dual rotor under an external potential that modulates the dual phases. For the spin this reading is exact. A Hamiltonian $\tfrac12\,\omega\cdot\sigma$, linear in the Pauli matrices, evolves a dual spinor by the dual rotor $U(t\omega)$: the curve $u(t)=U(t\omega)u_0$ solves
\[
i\,\dot u=\tfrac12\,(\omega\cdot\sigma)\,u ,
\]
and its rapidity grows linearly in time. In the nonrelativistic reduction of the coupled Dirac equation of Section~\ref{sec:dirac}, the spin part of the Pauli Hamiltonian in a uniform magnetic field is $-(e/2m)\,\sigma\cdot\mathbf B$, of this form with $\omega=-(e/m)\mathbf B$. The spin precesses as a dual rotation at the angular frequency $|e|B/m$, which equals the cyclotron frequency of the orbit, as gyromagnetic ratio $2$ requires. The orbital part of the Schr\"odinger equation is not linear in the dual generators. Compactness of the dual sector would keep that evolution unitary; it is not derived from the written particle action.

Wave-function collapse is readable as forced synchronization of dual rotors across an entangled many-particle system. A measurement interaction that coupled to the usual spacetime would still have to supply a restoring mechanism: the written action does not drive $\delta_a$ toward zero, and minimizing a global $J$ is a further identification, not an Euler--Lagrange consequence. The apparent non-unitary jump would then be the classical limit of a rapid, collective torsional relaxation. No additional collapse postulate is required in that picture; the picture itself remains a hypothesis.

The kinematics of entanglement is fixed by Section~\ref{sec:entanglement}, and a torsional reading has to respect it. The correlation of an entangled pair cannot be a correlation of dual phases carried separately by the two particles, since those keep the CHSH combination within $2$ and the singlet reaches $2\sqrt2$. It lives in the joint dual state. A local dual rotation of one particle changes neither that entanglement nor any statistics of the partner, so the correlation transmits nothing, and information still propagates at most at the speed of light through the usual sector. What remains a reading is the energy of the pair: a potential for its collective mismatch, which the written action does not supply, and its relaxation as the mechanism of collapse. Compactness of the dual sector makes that collective mismatch available as a geometric option, not as a theorem of the written action.

A path-integral formulation may be written with the same particle action,
\[
\langle R_f | R_i \rangle = \int \mathcal{D}[\phi,\theta] \, \exp\left( \frac{i}{\hbar} S_\text{particle}[\phi,\theta] \right),
\]
the integral running over paths in the dual-rotor phase space. Compactness of the dual sector would keep the integral well-defined. Because the written $S_{\mathrm{particle}}$ makes $\delta$ free, the torsional potential term does not by itself generate Compton-frequency interference.

In this picture quantum mechanics and gravity are not separate theories that must be reconciled. Quantum mechanics is the kinematics of rotations in the compact dual spacetime; gravity is the dynamical cost of breaking the parallelism between the usual and dual rotors. Both readings originate from the same 16-dimensional biquaternion algebra. The kinematics is sharp. The dynamical bridge from the written particle action is not.

\section{Dual Spacetime as Intrinsic Supersymmetry}
\label{sec:susy}

The cosmological constant problem, widely known as the vacuum catastrophe, represents one of the most acute fine-tuning crises in theoretical physics. Quantum field theory predicts that the vacuum energy density arises from the zero-point fluctuations of every field mode up to the ultraviolet cutoff. With the cutoff taken at the Planck scale, this yields an energy density of order \(M_\text{Pl}^4 \approx 10^{74}\,\text{GeV}^4\), which converts in SI units to approximately \(10^{113}\,\text{J/m}^3\). The observed value, however, inferred from the present-day accelerated expansion, is only \(\rho_\text{vac}^\text{obs} \approx 5.4 \times 10^{-10}\,\text{J/m}^3\), corresponding to a cosmological constant \(\Lambda \sim 1.1 \times 10^{-52}\,\text{m}^{-2}\). The ratio between the theoretical prediction and the measured value therefore reaches
\[
\frac{\rho_\text{vac}^\text{theory}}{\rho_\text{vac}^\text{obs}} \sim 10^{120}.
\]
No known renormalization procedure can remove this discrepancy without an extraordinary cancellation between bare parameters and quantum corrections, accurate to one part in \(10^{120}\). The problem is not technical but conceptual: gravity cannot be consistently coupled to the vacuum of quantum field theory on a fixed background without destroying the observed cosmology.

The Double Spacetime Theory addresses the crisis from inside the algebra: supersymmetry is already realized \emph{intrinsically and inseparably} in the 16-real-dimensional biquaternion algebra carried by every massive particle. No new fields or particles are introduced, and no external symmetry is imposed or broken.

The six generators of the underlying Lorentz candidate \(\mathfrak{so}(3,1)\) (not the twelve-dimensional sum \(\mathfrak{so}(3,1)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(3,1)\)) partition into two sectors distinguished by the sign of their quadratic form: the hyperbolic sector, whose generators square to \(+1\), and the cyclic sector, whose generators square to \(-1\). Same-axis generators commute; off-axis commutators need not vanish. The complete rotor \(R_\text{total}\) acts on both sectors simultaneously. The dual map \(X \mapsto Xi\) interchanges the two sectors up to signs while reversing time orientation. This interchange is an internal automorphism of the algebra; it does not map a particle onto a distinct species but exchanges two permanently coupled aspects of one and the same 16-dimensional object. The supersymmetric partner of any bosonic excitation is therefore already present as the dual-sector component, and there is no external superpartner to discover. On Dirac spinors the dual map acts by $\omega=i\gamma^5$ and reverses the four-momentum. Composed with the charge conjugation that the quaternionic structure of the dual spinor supplies, it carries every solution to its antiparticle at the same momentum (Section~\ref{subsec:antiparticle}). The antiparticle is thus internal as well: it is the same Dirac field read through the dual map, not an additional field.

The scalar \(J\) is the parameter-space quadratic \(J=\frac12\sum_a(\alpha_a^2-\beta_a^2)\) built from the mismatch bivector \(\Omega_\text{biv}\). Because the Killing form takes opposite values on the hyperbolic sector (\(+8\)) and the cyclic sector (\(-8\)), bosonic and fermionic contributions enter with opposite signs, and they cancel when the two sectors are aligned. In this reading the residual vacuum value of \(J\) comes from fluctuations of the dual-sector angles. The cyclic generators square to \(-1\), rendering the dual spacetime elliptic: the dual rotors form the compact group \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) (Section~\ref{sec:quantumstates}). The angles are therefore periodic and discretely sampled, \(\theta_a \in (2\pi/N)\mathbb{Z}\) for large integer \(N\). Compactness does \emph{not} force an algebraic upper bound of order \(N^{-2}\). On admissible configurations \(|J|\le 3\pi^2/8\), or \(|J_{\rm norm}|\le 1\) with \(J_{\rm norm}=\frac8{3\pi^2}J\). The discrete quantity \(\varepsilon_N=16/(3N^2)\) is a \emph{lower} bound on the nonzero normalized height, not an upper bound: every non-rest admissible lattice point satisfies \(|J_{\rm norm}|\ge\varepsilon_N\). Vacuum suppression, if it occurs, must come from a dynamical cancellation between sectors, not from writing \(|J|\le J_{\max}\sim N^{-2}\).

The dual sector carries spin-$1/2$ kinematics: it is the little group of a massive particle (Section~\ref{sec:dirac}). A full identification of Standard Model fermions with dual phases is not claimed. The dual map $X\mapsto Xi$ is not spacetime supersymmetry of a ten-dimensional string: $\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)\not\cong\mathrm{Cl}(9,1)$, the dual spinor space is not the Majorana--Weyl module of $\mathrm{Cl}(9,1)$, and the Super-Poincar\'e generator count is not the ten generators of the projective geometric algebra. The cancellation of opposite signatures at alignment does not generate the observed fermion masses, and the admissible bound on \(J\) is not the electroweak scale.

In this framework supersymmetry is not an optional extension that may or may not be realized in nature. It is the geometric consequence of an algebra whose two sectors possess quadratic forms of opposite signature and whose dual sector is compact. The vacuum catastrophe is therefore not met by adding new symmetries. The cancellation that observation requires has to come from the opposite signatures and the compactness already present in the 16-dimensional structure; the written particle action of Section~\ref{sec:dynamics} does not by itself produce it.

The echoes carry a characteristic phase modulation determined by the dual-rotor spectrum. Detection of such echoes by LISA or the Einstein Telescope would constitute direct evidence for the discrete torsional structure of strong gravity.

\section{Comparison with Other Quantum Gravity Approaches}
\label{sec:comparison}

The double spacetime theory occupies a distinctive position among quantum gravity candidates. Most frameworks quantize or discretize an a priori continuum geometry. The results collected here are algebraic identities in $\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)$ and $G(3,1,1)$, not a derivation of quantum mechanics and gravity from that algebra. Below we compare the programme with existing approaches and record why it does not identify with a ten-dimensional string.

\subsection{Loop Quantum Gravity}

Loop quantum gravity (LQG) discretizes spacetime via spin networks and quantizes area and volume operators while retaining a background manifold and Riemannian curvature as fundamental. Singularities are resolved by a minimal eigenvalue of the area operator, yet the continuum limit remains challenging and the dynamics are encoded in complicated Hamiltonian constraints.

In contrast, the double spacetime theory imposes discreteness directly on the dual-rotor angles $\phi_a, \theta_a \in (2\pi/N)\mathbb{Z}$, without introducing a background manifold. Curvature is absent; gravity is the torsional mismatch of the usual and dual rotors, a bivector in the six-dimensional Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(3,1)$. The reconstruction problem of LQG---recovering a smooth spacetime from spin networks---is replaced by the question of how macroscopic spacetime emerges collectively from the torsional alignment of neighbouring particle-intrinsic dual rotors.

\subsection{String Theory}

String theory unifies forces by embedding gravity in a ten- or eleven-dimensional spacetime populated by vibrating strings and requiring supersymmetry. While mathematically elegant, it suffers from a vast landscape of vacua and lacks unique low-energy predictions.

The algebras $\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)$ and $\mathrm{Cl}(9,1)$ are not isomorphic, and the dual spinor space $\mathbb{C}^2$ is not the sixteen-real-dimensional Majorana--Weyl module of ten-dimensional spacetime. Unification inside four-dimensional $\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)$ is a statement about one algebra, not a proof that extra dimensions are physically absent. The dual map is an internal automorphism; it is not spacetime supersymmetry. Gravity, electromagnetism, and the nuclear forces as modes of a single rotor algebra remain hypotheses; within the algebra, the electromagnetic coupling of Section~\ref{sec:dirac} already separates a dual-sector magnetic term from a usual-sector electric term.

No Calabi--Yau compactification is used; compactness of the dual sector at the particle level is a geometric hypothesis, not a string compactification.

\subsection{Canonical and Ashtekar Approaches}

The Ashtekar reformulation casts general relativity in terms of self-dual connections, enabling a Yang--Mills-like quantization. However, it inherits the continuum singularities of classical gravity and must confront the Wheeler--DeWitt equation with its notorious factor-ordering and regularization problems.

In the present framework no connection is introduced. Parallel transport is replaced by rotor exponentiation, and the Killing form on the torsional bivector replaces the curvature scalar. There is therefore no Wheeler--DeWitt constraint to solve; whether the finite rotor image supplies a complete quantization is part of the dynamical question left open in Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}.

\subsection{Emergent Gravity and Causal Sets}

Emergent gravity approaches treat spacetime as a thermodynamic or entropic phenomenon, while causal set theory discretizes Lorentzian order. Both frameworks struggle to recover the exact Einstein equations and face fermion doubling or chirality issues.

The double spacetime theory reaches the Einstein equations through the teleparallel equivalence once a rotor-to-tetrad dictionary is fixed~\cite{maluf2013}. A commuting projector in $G(3,1,1)$ generates an eight-dimensional left ideal that splits by the null generator and is not read as an irreducible fermion. Discreteness is algebraic (Diophantine constraints on rotor angles) rather than set-theoretic, and causality is preserved by the lightlike $J=0$ resonance condition. No thermodynamic analogy is invoked; the emergence of spacetime is read as a collective effect of torsional alignment among particle-intrinsic dual rotors.

In summary, existing quantum gravity programs extend or quantize the continuum manifold while retaining curvature as fundamental. The present paper records algebraic identities in $\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)$ and $G(3,1,1)$ and discards several identifications that do not hold. It does not derive spacetime, all interactions, or a controllable theory of gravity from those identities.

\section{Experimental Testability from the Quantum Perspective}
\label{sec:experiments}

Although the double spacetime theory is fundamentally algebraic, its quantum sector makes concrete experimental predictions that can be tested with current or near-future technology. The mismatch scalar $J=\tfrac12\sum_a(\alpha_a^2-\beta_a^2)$ depends only on the relative rotor. Dual-only motion cannot raise $J$; it conserves $J+M$, so every dual-only shield from a pure usual seed doubles unsigned mass. Shielding is a global budget, $\sum\alpha\le 3\pi/4$, not an isotropic per-axis condition: a single boosted axis can still be dual-only shielded by parking dual rapidity on a silent axis. Repulsion requires a strictly negative wall. The vacuum $J=M=0$ is mixed-reachable and dual-only unreachable except from the origin. The dual-only increment of the dual angles is helicity-even in $J$: a circular Faraday wave does not displace the mean, while a circular potential drops it ponderomotively and leaves the cone from a vanishing dual seed. Whether laboratory fields supply a helicity-odd dual-channel force remains a conjecture.

\subsection{Theoretical Coupling Mechanism}

A circularly polarised electromagnetic wave is null at every phase, with vanishing four-phase mean of every linear Faraday coefficient. Superposing that wave onto the mismatch does not shift mean $J$. Among the six classical Faraday quadratics, the unique helicity-odd combination on that wave is the Poynting component $(E\times B)_z=\sigma E_0^2$. The same dual-only increment, read as $\theta\mapsto\theta+eA$, drops mean $J$ by the helicity-even ponderomotive $\tfrac12 e^2\langle A^2\rangle$ and is not Maxwell-gauge invariant. Helicity drive of the particle mismatch is neither that increment nor a local scalar of $F$. The gauge-invariant electromagnetic coupling that the algebra does supply is the minimal coupling of Section~\ref{sec:dirac}, in which the magnetic field acts on the dual spinor through the dual generators. The working model takes the Poynting bilinear as the candidate source of a dual-phase drive, with right-handed polarisation ($\sigma=+1$) reinforcing attractive mismatch ($J>0$) and left-handed polarisation ($\sigma=-1$) opposing it. The effective interaction is written
\[
H_\text{int} = \kappa \sigma E_0^2 \sum_a \theta_a \Gamma_a,
\]
where $E_0$ is the electric-field amplitude and $\kappa$ is a material-dependent coupling, neither of which is derived from the dual-rotor algebra. The phase-lock
\[

is extra structure. The written particle action, given a dual-channel acceleration $u$, makes the mismatch a double integrator $\ddot\delta=-u$ and supplies no restoring frequency. Dual-only kinematics is a constrained slice of that action; without the constraint, both sectors move.

\subsection{Proposed Near-Term Experiments}

The most accessible platform exploits collective coherence in superconductors or Bose--Einstein condensates, where macroscopic numbers of particles ($N \sim 10^{26}$) can lock their dual rotors into a common phase. A concrete proposal is as follows:

\begin{itemize}

\item Monitor the apparent weight or inertial mass via a high-precision torsion balance or superconducting gravimeter while sweeping the frequency and helicity.

Predicted effect sizes, if the coupling exists, are $\Delta m / m \sim 10^{-2}$--$10^{-5}$ at achievable intensities of $10^{12}$--$10^{15}$ W/m$^2$. The signature is distinctive: the effect must reverse sign when the polarization helicity is flipped and must vanish for linearly polarized or unpolarized radiation.

\subsection{Falsifiability and Caveats}

A null result at the $10^{-6}$ level after a systematic frequency and helicity scan would constrain the dual-rotor coupling strength, not the admissible-cone kinematics of $J$. Positive detection---particularly a helicity-reversible mass shift---would constitute strong evidence that gravity and inertia are torsional phenomena originating in dual spacetime. A resonance peak would further constrain the working oscillator or phase-lock, which the written action does not force.

The theory makes no claim of immediate technological breakthrough. The proposals above are modest, table-top experiments whose primary purpose is to test the hypothesis that dual-spacetime degrees of freedom couple measurably to laboratory electromagnetic fields. Success at this level would open the door to larger-scale gravitational engineering; failure would simply bound the parameters of the coupling model.

\subsection{Broader Implications}

If dual-rotor control proves feasible, the same mechanism offers pathways to nuclear transmutation and radioactivity neutralization by driving torsional layer transitions inside atomic nuclei. These applications, however, lie beyond the scope of the present quantum-gravity test program and will be addressed in future work.

In summary, dual-only kinematics already determine when $J$ can be shielded or reversed and at what unsigned-mass cost, and they allow the dual budget to be routed across silent axes. Whether nature supplies a laboratory dual force---and whether gravity is therefore a controllable torsional degree of freedom---is the experimental question of the coming decade.

\section{The Algebraic Picture}
\label{sec:status}

The quantum sector of double spacetime theory is the kinematics of a compact dual spacetime already present in the $16$-real-dimensional algebra $\mathbb{H}\oplus\mathbb{H}\cong\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)$. No background manifold is quantized, and the Hilbert-layer pairing is treated in~\cite{dst-d4l}.

The dual sector is elliptic. Its generators square to $-1$, satisfy $IJ=K$, and act on $\mathbb{C}^2$ by $-i\sigma_a$, and the dual rotors are exactly the group $\mathrm{SU}(2)$. A unit dual spinor is a point of the unit sphere, a dual rotor turns its spin vector by the full angle, the $4\pi$ period is the intensity of $u+U(\beta)u$, and the Robertson--Schrödinger relation for two spin axes is an equality. Usual and dual generators commute on a common axis and need not commute off-axis. With the usual factor written first, every total rotor factors uniquely as $R_{\mathrm{total}}=R_{\mathrm{usual}}R_{\mathrm{dual}}$, and the dual factors are exactly the total rotors that fix the rest momentum. The little group of a massive particle, which carries its spin, is therefore the dual sector, and the momentum is carried by the usual sector; composing perpendicular usual rotors leaves the Thomas--Wigner rotation in the dual sector.

The Dirac matrices are the grade-1 generators of the same algebra, distinct from the hyperbolic bivectors that build square-$+1$ projectors. The momentum-space Dirac equation is the first-order factor of the Minkowski quadratic, its solutions on the mass shell are assembled from a dual spinor, and the equation is covariant under every total rotor, in momentum space and on spacetime. The mass bilinear is the invariant pairing; the Euclidean overlap is $p_0/m$ times it and, up to the factor $i$, the time component of the conserved current. That component, $\Psi^\dagger\Psi$, is the nonnegative density that Born's rule requires, which the indefinite Killing self-pairing is not. In the electromagnetic coupling the magnetic field enters through the dual generators with gyromagnetic ratio $2$ and the electric field through the usual generators, so spin precession is a dual rotation. The dual map acts on spinors by $\omega=i\gamma^5$ and reverses the four-momentum, which is its reversal of time orientation. The quaternionic structure of the dual spinor defines a charge conjugation that commutes with every total rotor and reverses the charge, and the composite of the two is the antiparticle at the same momentum, the charge conjugate read backward in time. Two dual spinors form a joint state in the tensor product, whose entanglement no local dual rotor changes. The singlet $Ju\otimes u-u\otimes Ju$ is the unique joint state fixed by common dual rotation; exchange splits the joint space into that singlet, of total spin $0$, and the symmetric states, of total spin $1$, and two dual spinors on one ray cannot occupy the singlet. The orthogonal complement of that ray is the spin-reversed partner, and the two rays exhaust the space. The correlation $-a\cdot b$ carries no signal and reaches $|S|=2\sqrt2$, which no assignment of particle-local dual phases attains. None of this derives the value of $m$ from torsion, and equal-scale interference nodes, which run inward, are not the Bohr radii.

Gravity is the mismatch of the two rotors, measured by $J=\frac12\sum_a(\alpha_a^2-\beta_a^2)$ with the admissible ceiling $|J|\le 3\pi^2/8$; the discrete quantity $\varepsilon_N=16/(3N^2)$ is a lower bound on nonzero normalized height, not a ceiling. Vacuum suppression, if it occurs, must come from a dynamical cancellation between the hyperbolic and cyclic sectors. Identification of $J$ with the teleparallel scalar is taken from the literature after a rotor-to-tetrad dictionary is fixed~\cite{maluf2013}, and the dual map $X\mapsto Xi$ is an internal automorphism, not ten-dimensional spacetime supersymmetry.

The written opposite-sign particle action does not close the dynamical bridge from this kinematics to quantum theory. It frees the mismatch, $\ddot\delta=0$, and sources the common rapidity, $\ddot\sigma=-2m\delta$; its Jacobi integral is indefinite, and an oscillator appears only when both kinetic signs are positive, at a frequency $\sqrt{2m}$ that meets the Compton frequency only at isolated masses. Identifying the free lag with the de Broglie phase, measurement as synchronization, the torsional reading of entanglement, and the path integral remain geometric hypotheses placed on this kinematics. The continuum remains a classical approximation, and the $16$-dimensional algebra is the setting of the quantum sector, not a finished theory of quantum gravity.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

The double spacetime theory supplies an algebraic setting for quantum gravity, not a completed derivation. Quantum kinematics is the compact dual sector of $\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)$; gravity is the mismatch of usual and dual rotors. The algebra determines the dual rotors as $\mathrm{SU}(2)$; the spin vector of a unit dual spinor as a point of the unit sphere, turned by the full angle, with the $4\pi$ interference and the Robertson--Schrödinger equality; the Dirac equation as the Clifford factor of the Minkowski quadratic, with on-shell solutions built from a dual spinor; its covariance under every total rotor, with the dual sector as the little group and the Thomas--Wigner rotation as a dual factor; the spacetime equation with its conserved current and Born density; an electromagnetic coupling in which the magnetic field acts through the dual generators and spin precession is a dual rotation; the antiparticle as the charge conjugate read through the dual map, which on spinors reverses the four-momentum; and the singlet of two dual spinors, whose exchange split is spin $0$ against spin $1$, whose repeated ray cannot occupy the singlet, the orthogonal spin-reversed partner exhausting the space with it, and whose correlations carry no signal and exceed the CHSH bound of particle-local variables. It also fixes the admissible ceiling on $J$ and the inward order of equal-scale interference nodes. It does not determine a Compton oscillator from the written action, the value of the mass, or the outward Rydberg radii.

Predictions and laboratory proposals in the preceding sections remain conjectural, bounded by the admissible ceiling. The continuum remains a classical approximation, and the $16$-dimensional algebra is the setting, not a finished theory of quantum gravity.
