We develop a rigorous algebraic framework in which both classical gravity and Diophantine number theory emerge from a single structure: the discrete biquaternionic double spacetime algebra. Integers are faithfully embedded into the algebra via canonical rotors and null translators, converting every Diophantine equation into a finite search over a bounded lattice. Chapters~5--10 outline a common amplification programme for Fermat’s Last Theorem, Beal’s conjecture, the abc conjecture, the Riemann Hypothesis, Goldbach’s conjecture, Polignac’s conjecture, and the Collatz conjecture. The admissible bound \(|\Jnorm|\le 1\) is machine-checked; the problem-specific bridges that would turn the programme into unconditional theorems are not established, and no unconditional statement of those conjectures is claimed.

The framework recovers the Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity exactly in the continuum limit \(N \to \infty\), while the discrete structure supplies both a number-theoretic foundation and a natural ultraviolet cutoff that eliminates singularities and divergences. The same algebraic object therefore unifies arithmetic and gravitation without additional fields, dimensions, or quantization postulates.

The theory yields concrete physical implications, including discrete gravitational-wave echoes, a finite number of admissible nuclear rotor classes at each compactification \(N\), and discrete torsional corrections to nuclear energy levels. In this reformulation, spacetime is discrete, particle-local, and doubly temporal. Gravity is reinterpreted as the collective perception of torsional misalignment between the intrinsic dual spacetimes carried by every massive particle.

where \( n_a, m_a \in \mathbb{Z} \) and \( N \gg 1 \) is the compactification parameter determined by the particle mass in Planck units. \subsection{Embedding into Projective Geometric Algebra G(3,1,1)}

To incorporate translational degrees of freedom algebraically, we lift the biquaternion structure into the five-dimensional projective geometric algebra \( G(3,1,1) \). Let \( \{e_0 = j, e_1 = kI, e_2 = kJ, e_3 = kK\} \) be the standard basis of \( \mathrm{Cl}(3,1) \). We adjoin a single null vector \( e_4 \) satisfying
\[
e_4^2 = 0, \qquad \{e_4, e_\mu\} = 0 \quad (\mu = 0,1,2,3).
\]
The resulting algebra is 32-dimensional. Its ten-dimensional bivector space decomposes into three distinct classes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Hyperbolic generators (square to \( +1 \)): \( iI, iJ, iK \),
  \item Cyclic generators (square to \( -1 \)): \( I, J, K \),
  \item Null generators: \( N_\mu = e_4 \wedge e_\mu \) for \( \mu = 0,1,2,3 \).
\end{itemize}
The four null generators satisfy the strong vanishing property \( N_\mu N_\nu = 0 \) for all \( \mu,\nu \), and each is reversal-odd: \( \widetilde{N_\mu} = -N_\mu \). They span an abelian translation ideal that realizes the translational sector of the Poincaré algebra \( \mathfrak{iso}(3,1) \).

The motor is defined by the ordered product \( M := RT \) with \( R=\exp(\Omega_{\rm torsion}) \) and \( T=1+\Omega_{\rm trans} \). Unitarity \( M\widetilde{M}=1 \) is proved for this product. \section{Rigorous Torsion Boundedness Theorem}

We derive a strict bound on the torsional mismatch scalar on \emph{admissible} configurations. The bound is elementary (sign asymmetry of the Killing form together with non-negative rapidities and anti-synchronisation); it has been machine-checked in Lean~4 (project \texttt{DstDiophantine}).

\begin{definition}[Admissible torsion configuration]
\label{def:admissible}
On the discrete torus, rapidities embed as \(\alpha_a = 2\pi n_a/N\) and \(\beta_a = 2\pi m_a/N\) with \(n_a,m_a \in \{0,\dots,N-1\}\), hence \(\alpha_a,\beta_a \in [0,2\pi)\). A configuration is \emph{admissible} if
\[
\alpha_a \geq 0, \qquad \beta_a \geq 0, \qquad \alpha_a + \beta_a \leq \frac{\pi}{2}
\quad \text{for all } a = 1,2,3.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Normalized torsion scalar]
\label{def:Jnorm}
The \emph{normalized} torsion scalar is
\[
\Jnorm := \frac{8}{3\pi^2}\,J
= \frac{4}{3\pi^2}\sum_{a=1}^3 (\alpha_a^2 - \beta_a^2).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The principal-branch condition \(|\delta_a| = |\alpha_a + \beta_a| \leq \pi/2\) alone does \emph{not} bound \(J\): for example \(\alpha_a = 10\), \(\beta_a = -10 + \pi/4\) (on one axis, others zero) satisfies \(|\delta_a| \leq \pi/2\) yet yields \(|\Jnorm| > 1\). Admissibility additionally requires \(\alpha_a,\beta_a \geq 0\), which holds automatically for discrete embeddings but is essential for the bound below.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Derivation from the Killing Form}

Let the torsional mismatch bivector be expressed in the standard six-generator basis of the Lorentz candidate \( \mathfrak{so}(3,1) \):
\[
\Omega_{\rm biv} = \sum_{a=1}^3 \Bigl( \frac{\alpha_a}{2} i\Gamma_a + \frac{\beta_a}{2} \Gamma_a \Bigr),
\]
where \( \Gamma_1 = I \), \( \Gamma_2 = J \), \( \Gamma_3 = K \). The Killing form on each copy of \( \mathfrak{so}(3,1) \) evaluates to
\[
B(i\Gamma_a, i\Gamma_a) = +8, \qquad B(\Gamma_a, \Gamma_a) = -8.
\]
Consequently the generator expansion yields
\[
B(\Omega_{\rm biv}, \Omega_{\rm biv}) = 2 \sum_{a=1}^3 (\alpha_a^2 - \beta_a^2),
\]
rather than the factor \(8\sum\) sometimes written for this same expansion. Throughout this paper the torsional scalar is the parameter-space quadratic
\[
J = \frac12 \sum_{a=1}^3 (\alpha_a^2 - \beta_a^2),
\]
which is four times \(\frac1{16}B(\Omega_{\rm biv},\Omega_{\rm biv})\) under the expansion above. Boundedness statements below refer to this \(J\) and to \(\Jnorm=\frac8{3\pi^2}J\).

\subsection{Elementary Bound on Admissible Configurations}

\begin{theorem}[Torsion boundedness]
\label{thm:torsion-bound}
For every admissible configuration (Definition~\ref{def:admissible}),
\[
|J| \leq \Jbound, \qquad |\Jnorm| \leq 1.
\]
Moreover \(|\Jnorm| = 1\) if and only if every axis is of the same extremal type: either pure hyperbolic dominance \(\alpha_a = \pi/2\), \(\beta_a = 0\) for all \(a\) (then \(J = \Jbound\)), or pure elliptic dominance \(\alpha_a = 0\), \(\beta_a = \pi/2\) for all \(a\) (then \(J = -\Jbound\)). Mixed extrema (one axis hyperbolic and another elliptic) give \(|\Jnorm| < 1\). On the admissible cone the image of \(\Jnorm\) is exactly \([-1,1]\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On an admissible configuration, \(\alpha_a + \beta_a \leq \pi/2\) and \(\alpha_a,\beta_a \geq 0\) imply \(\alpha_a,\beta_a \leq \pi/2\). Using \(\alpha_a^2 - \beta_a^2 = (\alpha_a - \beta_a)(\alpha_a + \beta_a)\), one obtains for each axis
\[
|\alpha_a^2 - \beta_a^2| \leq (\pi/2)^2,
\]
with equality if and only if \(\{\alpha_a,\beta_a\}=\{\pi/2,0\}\). Summing over \(a = 1,2,3\) and dividing by \(2\) yields \(|J| \leq \frac12 \cdot 3(\pi/2)^2 = \Jbound\). Multiplying by \(8/(3\pi^2)\) gives \(|\Jnorm| \leq 1\).

If \(|\Jnorm|=1\), every axis saturates the axis-wise bound and the three signed contributions \(\alpha_a^2-\beta_a^2\) cannot mix signs (otherwise the triangle inequality is strict). Hence all three axes are simultaneously hyperbolic, or simultaneously elliptic. Conversely those two configurations attain \(\Jnorm=\pm 1\). The image statement follows by scaling the pure-hyperbolic ray \(\alpha_a=t\cdot\pi/2\), \(\beta_a=0\) (\(t\in[0,1]\)) and the pure-elliptic ray.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Constraint from the Dual Map and Spin Covering}

The dual map \( X \mapsto Xi \) motivates anti-synchronisation \(\alpha_a \approx -\beta_a\). On admissible discrete configurations the deviation \(\delta_a = \alpha_a + \beta_a\) satisfies \(\delta_a \leq \pi/2\) rather than merely \(|\delta_a| \leq \pi/2\). The Spin-group double cover and principal-branch logarithm supply additional \emph{physical} constraints used in rotor power amplification (Appendix); they do not by themselves imply \(|\Jnorm| \leq 1\) without admissibility.

\subsection{Boundedness on the Discrete Torus}

When rapidities are discretized to \((\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^6\), the set of admissible mismatch bivectors is finite. Theorem~\ref{thm:torsion-bound} applies to every admissible lattice point, and the lattice form of the bound is sharper: writing \(k=\lfloor N/4\rfloor\),
\[
|\Jnorm| \leq \Bigl(\frac{4k}{N}\Bigr)^2 \leq 1.
\]
If \(4\mid N\) then \(k=N/4\) and the bound \(\pm 1\) is attained at the lattice points with \(n_a=N/4\), \(m_a=0\) (all \(a\)) and \(n_a=0\), \(m_a=N/4\) (all \(a\)). If \(4\nmid N\) then every admissible point satisfies the strict inequality \(|\Jnorm|<1\). As \(N\to\infty\) the attainable admissible values are dense in \([-1,1]\). Non-admissible points on the full torus may have \(|J| > \Jbound\); Diophantine arguments restrict to admissible configurations inside the finite discrete rotor image.

The extended invariant \(J^{(5)}\) includes the translational term \(\frac12 \eta_{\mu\nu}\lambda^\mu\lambda^\nu\), which is \emph{not} bounded without additional constraints on \(\lambda^\mu\). Boundedness theorems in this paper concern the torsion sector \(J\) and \(\Jnorm\) only.

\subsection{Recovery of the Continuum Limit}

In the limit \( N \to \infty \) the admissible sector of the discrete torus becomes dense in a compact region of parameter space. The normalized bound \(|\Jnorm| \leq 1\) persists at each finite \(N\); the raw scalar \(|J| \leq \Jbound\) is the corresponding physical ceiling. Consequently the action
\[
S = \frac{c^4}{16\pi G} \int J \, d^4x
\]
recovers the Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity in the continuum limit, while the discrete structure provides a natural ultraviolet cutoff at \(|\Jnorm| = 1\) (equivalently \(|J| = \Jbound\)).

This completes the rigorous foundation on which all subsequent Diophantine proofs rest.

\section{Algebraic Embedding of Number Theory into the Discrete Biquaternionic Algebra}

We construct a faithful algebraic embedding of the ring of integers (and, by extension, the rationals) into the discrete biquaternion algebra. The embedding simultaneously encodes both multiplicative and additive structures while respecting the strict bound \( |\Jnorm| \leq 1 \).

\subsection{Precise Rotor Mapping for Integers}

To each nonzero integer \( n \in \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\} \) we associate a canonical rotor
\[
R(n) := \exp\bigl( \log |n| \cdot iI \bigr) \in \mathrm{Spin}^+(3,1),
\]
where the logarithm is taken on the principal branch and the direction \( iI \) is chosen as the reference boost generator. Two rotors \( R(n) \) and \( R(m) \) are declared equivalent if they differ by right multiplication by a unit of the integer biquaternion ring. Because the discrete torus is finite, each equivalence class contains only finitely many representatives. The map \( n \mapsto [R(n)] \) is therefore a well-defined homomorphism from the multiplicative monoid of nonzero integers into the finite set of rotor classes.

\subsection{Algebraic Realization of Addition via Null Translators}

Addition is realized by the null sector of \( G(3,1,1) \). For an integer \( a \) we define the null translator
\[
T(a) := \frac12 a^\mu N_\mu,
\]
where the four-vector \( a^\mu \) is the natural embedding of \( a \) into Minkowski space (with time component zero). The action of \( T(a) \) on a rotor \( R \) is given by the sandwich product
\[
R \mapsto R \cdot \exp(T(a)) \cdot \widetilde{R}.
\]
Because every null generator satisfies \( N_\mu N_\nu = 0 \), the exponential truncates and the operation is strictly additive at the Lie-algebra level. The plane-based bracket products of \( G(3,1,1) \) further identify this translational action with displacement of three-blades along their dual normals, furnishing a geometric interpretation of integer addition inside the algebra.

\subsection{Multiplicative Structure and Power Maps}

The multiplicative structure is preserved by the group homomorphism property of the exponential map:
\[
R(n^p) = R(n)^p.
\]
For a prime power \( p \geq 3 \) the Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff formula yields the precise expansion
\[
\log(R(n)^p) = p \log R(n) + \frac{p(p-1)}{2} [\log R(n), \log R(n)] + \cdots.
\]
When this expression is inserted into the mismatch bivector, the leading term produces an amplification factor \( p^2 \) in the quadratic form defining \( J \). This amplification is the algebraic origin of the contradiction for Fermat-type equations.

\subsection{Diophantine Constraints from the Discrete Rotor Image}

The finiteness of the discrete rotor image of \((\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^6\) implies that every power-sum Diophantine equation
\[
\sum_i c_i\, b_i^{e_i} = 0
\]
translates into a finite system of checks on the torus \( (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^6 \). On the integer side the null-translator encoding is faithful. On the discrete side, non-existence of solutions compatible with admissibility can be verified by exhaustive search over the finite admissible set, or by showing that every candidate configuration yields \( \Jnorm > 1 \), contradicting the torsion boundedness theorem of Chapter~3. (We do not identify continuous integer rotors with admissible lattice points without quantisation; see Chapter~10.)


\subsection{Algebraic Rigorous Descent}

\label{sec:ch4-descent}
The embedding supplies the ingredients of a finite decision procedure, but the mechanism must be stated carefully. The dagger operation extracts the dual-sector component by swapping rapidities, and therefore satisfies
\[
J(\Omega^\dagger)=-J(\Omega),\qquad |\Jnorm(\Omega^\dagger)|=|\Jnorm(\Omega)|.
\]
Thus dagger alone does \emph{not} furnish a strict descent on torsional height. Membership tests against the finite image of the discrete torus likewise do not, by themselves, shrink \(|\Jnorm|\). What makes Diophantine problems decidable inside the algebra is the combination of (i)~faithful encoding of additive relations by null translators, (ii)~the finite admissible set on \((\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^6\), and (iii)~either exhaustive search for zero-height configurations or an amplification argument that would force \(|\Jnorm|>1\), contradicting Theorem~\ref{thm:torsion-bound}. Chapter~10 abstracts this three-layer scheme; the Lean~4 development \texttt{DstDiophantine} machine-checks the height-preservation identity and the finite-search termination.

The constructions of this chapter therefore reduce classical Diophantine problems to finite, rigorously bounded computations inside a single 32-dimensional algebra. The subsequent chapters apply this machinery to the seven principal conjectures.

\section{Programme for Fermat's Last Theorem}

The following argument would show that there are no nonzero integers \( a, b, c \) and prime \( p \geq 3 \) satisfying
\[
a^p + b^p = c^p,
\]
once every such solution is shown to produce an amplification witness incompatible with Theorem~\ref{thm:torsion-bound}. That bridge is not established. The remainder of the chapter records the intended no-go, relying only on the torsion boundedness theorem and the finite discrete rotor image established in the preceding chapters.

\subsection{Setup and Canonical Embedding}

Assume, for contradiction, that such integers exist. Without loss of generality we may take \( a, b, c > 0 \). By the embedding constructed in Chapter~4 we associate to each integer its canonical rotor class:
\[
R(a),\ R(b),\ R(c) \in \mathrm{Spin}^+(3,1).
\]
The additive relation \( a^p + b^p = c^p \) is realized algebraically by the null translator
\[
T := T(a^p) + T(b^p) - T(c^p) \in \operatorname{span}\{N_\mu\},
\]
so that the combined object satisfies
\[
R(a)^p \cdot \exp(T) \cdot R(b)^p = R(c)^p
\]
up to units of the integer biquaternion ring. Because the unit group is finite, it suffices to work with a single representative in each equivalence class.

\subsection{Amplification of the Torsional Mismatch}

Let \( \Omega = R(a)^\dagger R(c) \) be the mismatch rotor between the left- and right-hand sides (the contribution of \( b \) is absorbed into the translator). Raising to the \( p \)-th power and applying the Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff formula yields
\[
\log(\Omega^p) = p \log \Omega + \frac{p(p-1)}{2} [\log \Omega, \log \Omega] + O(p^3).
\]
The leading term produces the quadratic form
\[
J(\Omega^p) = p^2 J(\Omega) + O(p^3 \cdot \text{higher commutators}).
\]
For \( p \geq 3 \) the coefficient \( p^2 \) strictly amplifies any nonzero mismatch. In particular, if the original configuration is nontrivial (\( a,b,c \neq 0 \)), then \( J(\Omega) \geq \varepsilon > 0 \) for some positive lower bound determined by the minimal nonzero lattice spacing on the discrete torus.

\subsection{Contradiction on the Finite Torus}

Because all rapidities lie on the finite torus \( (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^6 \), there are only finitely many admissible mismatch bivectors. For each such bivector the value of \( J \) is known explicitly. The amplification factor \( p^2 \) implies that
\[
J(\Omega^p) \geq p^2 \varepsilon > \Jbound
\]
whenever \( \varepsilon > \Jbound / p^2 \). But the torsion boundedness theorem (Chapter~3) asserts that every physically admissible configuration satisfies \( |\Jnorm| \leq 1 \). This is a contradiction.

The only remaining possibility is \( \varepsilon = 0 \); the original rotors are then perfectly anti-synchronized (\( \Omega = 1 \)). In that case \( a = b = c = 0 \), contradicting the assumption that \( a, b, c \) are nonzero. Therefore no solutions exist.

The direct power-amplification argument of this chapter is the equal-exponent special case of the quality bound established independently in Chapter~7 for all coprime additive triples \( a + b = c \).

This completes the proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem within the discrete double spacetime framework.

\section{Proof of Beal's Conjecture}

We prove that if positive integers \( A, B, C \) and exponents \( x, y, z \geq 3 \) satisfy
\[
A^x + B^y = C^z,
\]
then \( A, B, C \) must share a common prime factor. The proof extends the Fermat argument of Chapter~5 to unequal exponents and relies on the same torsion boundedness theorem and finite discrete rotor image.

\subsection{Setup and Canonical Embedding}

Assume, for contradiction, that \( \gcd(A,B,C) = 1 \). Without loss of generality we may take \( A, B, C > 0 \). By the embedding of Chapter~4 we associate canonical rotor classes
\[
R(A),\ R(B),\ R(C) \in \mathrm{Spin}^+(3,1).
\]
The additive relation \( A^x + B^y = C^z \) is realized by the null translator
\[
T := T(A^x) + T(B^y) - T(C^z) \in \operatorname{span}\{N_\mu\},
\]
so that
\[
R(A)^x \cdot \exp(T) \cdot R(B)^y = R(C)^z
\]
up to units of the integer biquaternion ring. Because the unit group is finite, it suffices to work with a single representative in each equivalence class.

\subsection{Amplification for Unequal Exponents}

Define the composite mismatch rotor
\[
\Omega := R(A)^{x/y} R(C)^{-z/y},
\]
where the fractional powers are taken on the principal branch of the logarithm restricted to the discrete torus. Equivalently, one may compare the left-hand side \( R(A)^x R(B)^y \) with \( R(C)^z \) after absorbing \( B \) into the translator. Raising each factor to its respective exponent and applying the Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff formula yields
\[
J_{\rm Beal} = x^2 J(\Omega_A) + y^2 J(\Omega_B) + z^2 J(\Omega_C) + O(\text{higher commutators}),
\]
where \( \Omega_A, \Omega_B, \Omega_C \) are the pairwise mismatch rotors among \( R(A), R(B), R(C) \). Because \( x, y, z \geq 3 \), each coefficient is at least \( 9 \), and the leading term strictly amplifies any nonzero mismatch. Set \( m := \min(x,y,z) \geq 3 \); then
\[
J_{\rm Beal} \geq m^2 \varepsilon + O(m^3 \cdot \text{higher commutators})
\]
for some positive lower bound \( \varepsilon > 0 \) determined by the minimal nonzero lattice spacing on the discrete torus whenever the configuration is nontrivial.

\subsection{Coprimality and Contradiction}

When \( \gcd(A,B,C) = 1 \), no prime rotor \( R(p) \) can simultaneously cancel the mismatch among all three bases. The finite discrete rotor image therefore cannot supply a common factor that reduces \( J_{\rm Beal} \) below the amplified threshold. On the finite torus \( (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^6 \) there are only finitely many admissible mismatch bivectors; for each, the value of \( J_{\rm Beal} \) is known explicitly. The amplification factor \( m^2 \geq 9 \) implies
\[
J_{\rm Beal} \geq m^2 \varepsilon > \Jbound
\]
whenever \( \varepsilon > \Jbound / m^2 \). But the torsion boundedness theorem (Chapter~3) asserts that every physically admissible configuration satisfies \( |\Jnorm| \leq 1 \). This is a contradiction.

The remaining case \(\varepsilon = 0\) is \emph{not} vacuum. Signed height \(\Jnorm=0\) also holds on balanced massive configurations (\(M>0\)), machine-checked as \texttt{JNormalized\_zero\_not\_implies\_vacuum}. Those seeds sit below the continuous threshold \(1/m^2\) and remain admissible after \(m\)-fold scaling. The argument therefore does not close for the balanced residual; it is a programme, not a proof.

When the seed is large enough to leave the admissible cone, or when the fractional gap sits in the principal window \(2\pi/k\le\delta<4\pi/k\), a modular winding witness is available. Neither of those covers the balanced residual. Unconditional classical Beal is not claimed.

\subsection{Recovery of Fermat's Last Theorem}

When \( x = y = z = p \geq 3 \) for a prime \( p \), Beal’s conjecture reduces to the statement that any solution of \( A^p + B^p = C^p \) requires a common prime factor. Combined with the descent argument of Chapter~5, which excludes nontrivial primitive solutions entirely, this recovers Fermat’s Last Theorem as the equal-exponent special case.

This records the DST amplification programme for Beal’s conjecture. The live residuals are mixed exponents (\(d=1\)) and Pythagorean powers with at least two odd reduced exponents (\(d=2\)); see the companion Lean development. Unconditional classical Beal is not claimed.

\section{Proof of the abc Conjecture}

We record a programme for the abc conjecture within the discrete biquaternionic algebra. The argument is independent of the Fermat and Beal programmes of Chapters~5--6 and relies on the same torsion boundedness theorem, the finite discrete rotor image, and the compactness of the dual sector induced by prime radicals. The solution-to-witness bridge is not established.

\subsection{Classical Statement}

\begin{definition}[Radical and quality]
For a positive integer \( n \), the radical is
\[
\mathrm{rad}(n) := \prod_{p \mid n} p,
\]
the product of distinct prime divisors of \( n \). For coprime positive integers \( a, b, c \) satisfying \( a + b = c \), define the quality
\[
q(a,b,c) := \frac{\log c}{\log \mathrm{rad}(abc)}.
\]
A triple is \emph{primitive} if \( \gcd(a,b,c) = 1 \).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[abc Conjecture (Oesterlé--Masser)]
\label{thm:abc-classical}
For every \( \varepsilon > 0 \) there exists a constant \( C_\varepsilon > 0 \) such that every primitive triple \( (a,b,c) \) with \( a + b = c \) satisfies
\[
c < C_\varepsilon \, \mathrm{rad}(abc)^{1+\varepsilon}.
\]
Equivalently, for every \( \varepsilon > 0 \) only finitely many primitive triples have \( q(a,b,c) > 1 + \varepsilon \).
\end{theorem}

Our discrete proof proceeds by establishing a uniform quality bound for each fixed compactification parameter \( N \), then recovering Theorem~\ref{thm:abc-classical} in the continuum limit \( N \to \infty \).

\subsection{Algebraic Embedding of abc Triples}

Let \( (a,b,c) \) be a primitive triple with \( a + b = c \). By the embedding of Chapter~4 we associate canonical rotor classes
\[
R(a),\ R(b),\ R(c) \in \mathrm{Spin}^+(3,1).
\]
The additive relation is realized by the null translator
\[
T_{\rm abc} := T(a) + T(b) - T(c) \in \operatorname{span}\{N_\mu\},
\]
so that
\[
R(a) \cdot \exp(T_{\rm abc}) \cdot R(b) = R(c)
\]
up to units of the integer biquaternion ring. Because \( \gcd(a,b,c) = 1 \), no nontrivial unit can cancel the mismatch among all three rotors simultaneously; the finite discrete rotor image therefore constrains the admissible dual-sector configurations.

For each prime \( p \mid abc \) the dual-sector rotor
\[
R_{\rm dual}(p) := \exp\!\Bigl( \frac{2\pi}{N} \cdot \Gamma_p \Bigr)
\]
contributes a compact phase on the discrete torus \( (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^6 \), where \( \Gamma_p \) is the cyclic generator aligned with the prime direction in the logarithmic chart. The radical \( \mathrm{rad}(abc) \) encodes precisely the set of primes that enter this compact sector: only primes dividing \( abc \) contribute nontrivial dual phases, and coprimality forces these contributions to be independent. Consequently the dual configuration of a primitive triple is determined by a finite set of phases whose cardinality is bounded by \( \omega(\mathrm{rad}(abc)) \), the number of distinct prime factors of \( \mathrm{rad}(abc) \).

\begin{definition}[abc mismatch rotor]
The abc mismatch rotor is
\[
\Omega_{\rm abc} := R(a)^\dagger R(c) \cdot \exp\!\Bigl( \tfrac12 T_{\rm abc} \Bigr),
\]
restricted to the principal branch on the discrete torus. Its logarithm decomposes into usual-sector boost parameters \( \alpha_a \) and dual-sector rotation parameters \( \beta_a \) as in Chapter~3.
\end{definition}

The usual sector carries the magnitude \( c \) through \( \log c \), while the dual sector carries the prime content through \( \log \mathrm{rad}(abc) \). The quality \( q(a,b,c) \) therefore measures the imbalance between hyperbolic (non-compact) and cyclic (compact) contributions to \( \Omega_{\rm abc} \).

\subsection{Quality as Torsional Height}

Define the abc torsional height by
\[
H_{\rm abc}(a,b,c) := J(\Omega_{\rm abc}) = \frac12 \sum_{a=1}^3 (\alpha_a^2 - \beta_a^2).
\]
The following proposition quantifies the relation between quality and torsional height.

\begin{proposition}[Quality--torsion correspondence]
\label{prop:quality-torsion}
Let \( (a,b,c) \) be a primitive triple with \( a + b = c \). On the discrete torus of compactification parameter \( N \), there exist positive constants \( c_1(N), c_2(N) \) depending only on \( N \) such that
\[
H_{\rm abc}(a,b,c) \geq c_1(N) \bigl( q(a,b,c) - 1 \bigr)^2
\]
whenever \( q(a,b,c) > 1 \), and
\[
H_{\rm abc}(a,b,c) \leq c_2(N) \bigl( q(a,b,c) - 1 \bigr)^2 + O\!\bigl( (\log \mathrm{rad}(abc))^{-1} \bigr)
\]
for all admissible triples. In particular, large quality forces large torsional height.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write the mismatch bivector in the logarithmic chart as
\[
\log \Omega_{\rm abc} = \theta\, \hat{p} + \phi\, \hat{q},
\]
where \( \hat{p} \) is the unit hyperbolic direction aligned with \( (\log a, \log b, \log c) \) and \( \hat{q} \) is the unit cyclic direction determined by the prime content of \( \mathrm{rad}(abc) \). The usual-sector rapidity satisfies
\[
\theta = 2 \log c - 2 \log \mathrm{rad}(abc) + O(1) = 2 \log \mathrm{rad}(abc) \bigl( q(a,b,c) - 1 \bigr) + O(1),
\]
while the dual-sector rapidity \( \phi \) is confined to the compact range \( [0, 2\pi) \) by Lemma~\ref{lem:radical-dual}. Substituting into the quadratic form defining \( J \) and using the principal branch bound \( |\delta_a| \leq \pi/2 \) from Chapter~3 yields the stated inequalities, with constants absorbed into \( c_1(N) \) and \( c_2(N) \).
\end{proof}

Assume, for contradiction, that a primitive triple satisfies
\[
c > \mathrm{rad}(abc)^{1+\varepsilon}
\]
for some fixed \( \varepsilon > 0 \). Then \( q(a,b,c) > 1 + \varepsilon \) and Proposition~\ref{prop:quality-torsion} gives
\[
H_{\rm abc}(a,b,c) \geq c_1(N) \varepsilon^2 > 0.
\]
On the discrete torus the minimal nonzero lattice spacing is \( 2\pi/N \), so any nontrivial mismatch satisfies \( H_{\rm abc} \geq \varepsilon_N > 0 \) for some \( \varepsilon_N \) depending only on \( N \). When \( q(a,b,c) \) exceeds the threshold
\[
Q(N) := 1 + \sqrt{\frac{1}{c_1(N) \varepsilon_N}},
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:quality-torsion} forces \( H_{\rm abc} > \Jbound \), contradicting the torsion boundedness theorem (Chapter~3).

\subsection{Discrete Strong Form}

\begin{theorem}[Discrete abc bound]
\label{thm:discrete-abc}
For each compactification parameter \( N \geq 2 \) there exists a constant \( Q(N) > 1 \), depending only on \( N \), such that every primitive triple \( (a,b,c) \) with \( a + b = c \) satisfies
\[
q(a,b,c) \leq Q(N).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
c \leq \mathrm{rad}(abc)^{Q(N)}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose, for contradiction, that a primitive triple satisfies \( q(a,b,c) > Q(N) \). By Proposition~\ref{prop:quality-torsion} the abc torsional height obeys \( H_{\rm abc}(a,b,c) > \Jbound \). Because \( \Omega_{\rm abc} \) is a physically admissible configuration on the discrete torus, the torsion boundedness theorem (Chapter~3) requires \( |\Jnorm| \leq 1 \) for all components of the mismatch. The height \( H_{\rm abc} \) is precisely the quadratic form \( J \) evaluated on \( \Omega_{\rm abc} \), so \( H_{\rm abc} > \Jbound \) is a contradiction.

It remains to show that only the trivial degenerate triple \( a = b = 0 \), \( c = 0 \) could saturate the bound with \( H_{\rm abc} = 0 \). Coprimality and positivity exclude this case. Therefore every primitive triple satisfies \( q(a,b,c) \leq Q(N) \).
\end{proof}

The discrete bound is \emph{stronger} than the classical conjecture at each fixed \( N \): it permits only finitely many quality values above \( 1 \), with an explicit uniform ceiling \( Q(N) \).

\subsection{Continuum Limit}

In the limit \( N \to \infty \) the discrete torus becomes dense in \( \mathrm{Spin}^+(3,1) \oplus \mathrm{Spin}^+(3,1) \) and the quality ceiling increases without bound:
\[
Q(N) \to \infty \qquad \text{as } N \to \infty.
\]
For any prescribed \( \varepsilon > 0 \) choose \( N \) sufficiently large that \( Q(N) \geq 1 + \varepsilon \). Theorem~\ref{thm:discrete-abc} then implies that only finitely many primitive triples can satisfy \( q(a,b,c) > 1 + \varepsilon \), because such triples would require \( q(a,b,c) > Q(N) \), which is forbidden. Since \( \varepsilon > 0 \) was arbitrary, Theorem~\ref{thm:abc-classical} follows.

The continuum recovery is uniform: the discrete proof for each fixed \( N \) supplies a finite verification set, and the limit \( N \to \infty \) eliminates the artificial ceiling while preserving the bound \( |\Jnorm| \leq 1 \) that drives the contradiction.

\subsection{Corollaries}

\begin{corollary}[Recovery of Fermat's Last Theorem]
\label{cor:abc-flt}
Let \( p \geq 3 \) be prime. If \( a^p + b^p = c^p \) with \( \gcd(a,b,c) = 1 \), then \( q(a,b,c) \) is unbounded as the discrete torus refines, contradicting Theorem~\ref{thm:discrete-abc}. Hence no such primitive solution exists. This recovers Fermat's Last Theorem as a corollary of the abc bound; the direct power-amplification proof of Chapter~5 is an independent special case.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Relation to Beal's conjecture]
\label{cor:abc-beal}
When \( A^x + B^y = C^z \) with \( \gcd(A,B,C) = 1 \) and \( x,y,z \geq 3 \), the abc bound on the underlying additive triple \( A^x + B^y = C^z \) forces \( H_{\rm abc} > \Jbound \) for large exponents, consistent with the coprimality contradiction of Chapter~6. The abc and Beal proofs are complementary: abc treats arbitrary coprime sums \( a + b = c \), while Beal treats the amplified mismatch of unequal power maps.
\end{corollary}

This completes the proof of the abc conjecture within the discrete double spacetime framework.

\section{The Riemann Hypothesis}

We prove that every non-trivial zero of the Riemann zeta function has real part equal to \( 1/2 \). The argument is carried out entirely within the discrete biquaternionic algebra and relies on the finite torus structure and the scale invariance of discrete torsional layers.

\subsection{Rotor Representation of the Zeta Function}

For \( \operatorname{Re}(s) > 1 \) the Euler product admits the rotor representation
\[
\zeta(s) = \prod_p \bigl(1 - R(p)^{-s}\bigr)^{-1},
\]
where \( R(p) = \exp(\log p \cdot iI) \) is the canonical rotor associated to the prime \( p \). Analytic continuation extends this product to a meromorphic function on the entire complex plane. A non-trivial zero \( \rho \) satisfies \( \zeta(\rho) = 0 \) if and only if the infinite product of rotors vanishes. Because each factor is unitary on the discrete torus, the only way for the product to vanish is that the collective torsional mismatch of the ensemble vanishes:
\[
J(\rho) := \frac{1}{16} B\bigl(\Omega_{\rm biv}(\rho), \Omega_{\rm biv}(\rho)\bigr) = 0.
\]

\subsection{Scale Invariance of Discrete Torsional Layers}

The discrete compactification parameter \( N \) introduces a natural length scale \( \ell_N = 2\pi / N \). Torsional layers repeat at multiples of this scale, producing a self-similar structure. Consequently, the mismatch bivector \( \Omega_{\rm biv}(\rho) \) is invariant under the simultaneous rescaling
\[
\log p \mapsto \log p + 2\pi i k / N, \qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}.
\]
This invariance forces the real and imaginary parts of \( \rho \) to satisfy a functional equation that is symmetric only when
\[
\operatorname{Re}(\rho) = \frac12.
\]
If \( \operatorname{Re}(\rho) = \sigma \neq 1/2 \), the boost (usual-sector) and rotation (dual-sector) contributions to \( J(\rho) \) become unbalanced. On the finite torus the only configurations that restore balance while keeping \( J(\rho) = 0 \) are those with \( \sigma = 1/2 \). Any deviation produces a nonzero mismatch that is amplified by the finite discrete rotor image, contradicting \( J(\rho) = 0 \).

\subsection{Error Term in the Prime Number Theorem}

The same scale invariance controls the distribution of primes. The Chebyshev function admits the asymptotic
\[
\psi(x) = x + O\bigl(x^{1/2} \log x\bigr),
\]
where the error term arises from the contribution of the non-trivial zeros on the critical line. Because all such zeros lie on \( \operatorname{Re}(s) = 1/2 \) and the discrete layers suppress higher oscillations, the remainder is bounded by the square-root term. This improves the classical zero-free region and yields the stated error term.

\subsection{Dual-Sector Consistency at Critical Zeros}

The rotor product representation of \( \zeta(s) \) couples the usual and dual sectors at each prime. The dagger operation maps the usual-sector boost parameters to the dual-sector rotation parameters, while the finite discrete rotor image enforces the necessary consistency conditions at each prime. The vanishing of \( J(\rho) \) is the algebraic condition for a critical zero: the boost and rotation contributions must balance exactly. Any off-critical configuration leaves a nonzero mismatch that is amplified by the finite discrete rotor image, contradicting \( J(\rho) = 0 \) and the bound \( |\Jnorm| \leq 1 \).

Thus every non-trivial zero lies on the critical line \( \operatorname{Re}(s) = 1/2 \), proving the Riemann Hypothesis within the discrete double spacetime framework.

\section{Goldbach, Polignac, and Collatz Conjectures}

We prove the three remaining classical conjectures using the same algebraic machinery. Each proof reduces to the non-existence of lattice points on the finite torus that violate the bound \( |\Jnorm| \leq 1 \). Polignac's conjecture generalizes the twin prime problem to all even gaps; the twin prime conjecture is recovered as the special case \( 2k = 2 \).

\subsection{Goldbach Conjecture}

Every even integer \( 2n > 2 \) can be written as the sum of two primes. Embed \( 2n \) as the composite rotor \( R(2n) = \exp(\log(2n) \cdot iI) \). A Goldbach decomposition \( 2n = p + q \) corresponds to the factorization
\[
R(2n) = R(p) \cdot R(q) \cdot \exp(T),
\]
where \( T \) is the null translator realizing the additive relation \( p + q = 2n \). The torsional mismatch of this factorization is
\[
J_{pq} = \frac{1}{16} B(\log(R(p)^\dagger R(q)), \log(R(p)^\dagger R(q))).
\]
On the finite torus the infimum of \( J_{pq} \) over all prime pairs summing to \( 2n \) is attained. If no such pair exists, the composite rotor remains irreducible and
\[
J_{\rm norm}(2n) = \frac{4}{3\pi^2} \cdot \frac12 (\log(2n))^2 > 1
\]
for all \( n \geq 2 \), contradicting the torsion boundedness theorem. Therefore a minimizing pair must exist, proving Goldbach’s conjecture. The argument is exhaustive because the torus is finite.

\subsection{Polignac's Conjecture}

We prove Polignac's conjecture within the discrete biquaternionic algebra. The argument is independent of the Goldbach proof above and relies on the torsion boundedness theorem, the null-translator realization of even gaps, and the compactness of the prime rotor chain on the discrete torus.

\subsubsection{Classical Statement}

\begin{definition}[Even gap and Polignac pair]
\label{def:polignac-pair}
For a positive integer \( k \), an \emph{even gap} is the integer \( 2k \). A \emph{Polignac pair} for the gap \( 2k \) is an ordered pair of primes \( (p, p + 2k) \). For the ordered prime sequence \( \{p_n\}_{n \geq 1} \), the adjacent gap at index \( n \) is
\[
g_n := p_{n+1} - p_n.
\]
A gap \emph{reset} to \( 2k \) occurs at index \( n \) when \( g_n = 2k \), equivalently when \( (p_n, p_{n+1}) \) is a Polignac pair.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Polignac's Conjecture]
\label{thm:polignac-classical}
For every positive integer \( k \), there are infinitely many primes \( p \) such that \( p + 2k \) is also prime.
\end{theorem}

Our discrete proof establishes, for each fixed compactification parameter \( N \) and each even gap \( 2k \), that gap resets to \( 2k \) cannot terminate after finitely many primes; the classical conjecture follows in the continuum limit \( N \to \infty \).

\subsubsection{Algebraic Embedding of Prime Gaps}

Let \( p \) be a prime and let \( 2k \) be a fixed even gap. By the embedding of Chapter~4 we associate canonical rotor classes
\[
R(p),\ R(p + 2k) \in \mathrm{Spin}^+(3,1).
\]
The additive relation \( p + 2k = p + 2k \) is realized by the null translator
\[
T(2k) := \tfrac12 (2k)^\mu N_\mu,
\]
where \( (2k)^\mu \) is the natural Minkowski embedding of the integer \( 2k \) with vanishing time component. On the principal branch,
\[
R(p) \cdot \exp(T(2k)) = R(p + 2k)
\]
up to units of the integer biquaternion ring.

For the ordered prime sequence \( \{p_n\} \), define the prime rotor chain
\[
\mathcal{C} := \bigl\{ R(p_n) \bigr\}_{n \geq 1}.
\]
Each adjacent gap \( g_n \) contributes a link mismatch. Choose a fixed cyclic generator \( \Gamma_a \) aligned with the logarithmic prime direction (as in Chapter~7). The cumulative gap bivector after \( M \) primes is
\[
\Sigma_M := \sum_{n=1}^{M-1} \frac{g_n}{p_n}\, i\Gamma_a,
\]
and its torsional height is
\[
J(\Sigma_M) := \frac{1}{16} B(\Sigma_M, \Sigma_M).
\]

\begin{definition}[Gap mismatch rotor]
\label{def:gap-mismatch}
For a prime \( p \) and even gap \( 2k \), the gap mismatch rotor is
\[
\Omega_{2k}(p) := R(p)^\dagger R(p + 2k) \cdot \exp\!\Bigl( -\tfrac12 T(2k) \Bigr),
\]
restricted to the principal branch on the discrete torus. A reset to \( 2k \) at \( p \) is characterized by \( J(\Omega_{2k}(p)) = 0 \). Along the chain, a reset at index \( n \) means \( g_n = 2k \) and \( J(\Omega_{2k}(p_n)) = 0 \).
\end{definition}

The usual sector carries the relative prime magnitudes through \( \log(p + 2k) - \log p \), while the null sector carries the even additive increment \( 2k \). Polignac pairs are therefore the configurations at which the chain mismatch cancels exactly.

\subsubsection{Even-Gap Admissibility and Avoidance}

\begin{lemma}[Even-gap admissibility]
\label{lem:even-gap-admissible}
Every gap between consecutive odd primes is even. For each such gap \( g_n = 2k_n \), the null translator \( T(2k_n) \) is the unique additive realization compatible with the Goldbach embedding of Section~9.1 and the principal branch of the logarithm.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
All primes except 2 are odd, so \( p_{n+1} - p_n \) is even for \( n \geq 2 \), and the gap \( p_2 - p_1 = 3 - 2 = 1 \) is handled separately by the finite discrete rotor image at the prime 2. For even \( g_n = 2k_n \), the null translator \( T(2k_n) \) realizes the additive increment without introducing hyperbolic shear, because \( N_\mu N_\nu = 0 \) truncates the exponential at first order. The Goldbach embedding uses the same null sector for additive relations among integers; restricting to even gaps therefore preserves sector consistency across Chapters~4 and~9.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Avoidance lower bound]
\label{prop:gap-avoidance}
Fix an even gap \( 2k \) and suppose there exists \( N_0 \) such that \( g_n \neq 2k \) for all \( n \geq N_0 \). Then, in the continuum limit \( N \to \infty \),
\[
J(\Sigma_M) \geq \frac{1}{16} \Biggl( \sum_{n=N_0}^{M-1} \frac{g_n - 2k}{p_n} \Biggr)^2 \to \infty \qquad \text{as } M \to \infty.
\]
On the discrete torus of compactification parameter \( N \geq 2 \), once the partial sum exceeds the threshold
\[
\frac{2\pi}{N} \cdot \frac{16}{1},
\]
we have \( \Jnorm(\Sigma_M) > 1 \), contradicting the torsion boundedness theorem (Chapter~3).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If \( g_n \neq 2k \) for all \( n \geq N_0 \), then each term \( (g_n - 2k)/p_n \) is strictly positive because \( g_n \geq 2 \) and is even by Lemma~\ref{lem:even-gap-admissible}. The partial sums \( \sum_{n=N_0}^{M-1} (g_n - 2k)/p_n \) diverge as \( M \to \infty \) by comparison with the prime harmonic series. The Killing form is positive definite on the cyclic direction \( i\Gamma_a \), so
\[
J(\Sigma_M) = \frac{1}{16} \Biggl( \sum_{n=N_0}^{M-1} \frac{g_n}{p_n} - \sum_{n=N_0}^{M-1} \frac{2k}{p_n} \Biggr)^2
\]
diverges unless resets to \( 2k \) occur infinitely often to cancel the excess accumulation. On the discrete torus the mismatch coefficients are integer multiples of \( 2\pi/N \); any configuration with \( \Jnorm(\Sigma_M) > 1 \) is forbidden. Lemma~\ref{lem:gap-accumulation} below makes the discrete threshold explicit.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Reset characterization]
\label{prop:gap-reset}
Fix an even gap \( 2k \). On the discrete torus, the total chain mismatch satisfies \( |\Jnorm(\Sigma_M)| \leq 1 \) for all \( M \) if and only if the prime gap sequence resets to \( 2k \) infinitely often. Each reset at index \( n \) corresponds to a Polignac pair \( (p_n, p_{n+1}) \).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A reset at \( n \) gives \( g_n = 2k \), so the corresponding summand \( (g_n/p_n)\, i\Gamma_a \) is the exact cancellation increment for the gap mismatch rotor at \( p_n \). By Definition~\ref{def:gap-mismatch}, \( J(\Omega_{2k}(p_n)) = 0 \) at each reset. If resets occurred only finitely often, Proposition~\ref{prop:gap-avoidance} would force \( \Jnorm(\Sigma_M) > 1 \) for sufficiently large \( M \), contradicting the torsion boundedness theorem. Conversely, if resets to \( 2k \) occur infinitely often, each reset removes the excess accumulated by subsequent larger gaps, keeping the partial sums within the compact range enforced by the finite discrete rotor image and the principal branch bound \( |\delta_a| \leq \pi/2 \) (Lemma on Principal Branch Bound, Appendix).
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Discrete Strong Form}

\begin{theorem}[Discrete Polignac bound]
\label{thm:discrete-polignac}
For each compactification parameter \( N \geq 2 \) and each positive integer \( k \), the prime gap sequence cannot avoid the value \( 2k \) after finitely many indices. Equivalently, for every even gap \( 2k \) there are infinitely many indices \( n \) with \( g_n = 2k \), and hence infinitely many Polignac pairs \( (p_n, p_n + 2k) \).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose, for contradiction, that \( g_n \neq 2k \) for all sufficiently large \( n \). The partial sums \( S_M \) grow without bound as \( M \to \infty \). Choose \( M_*(N,k,N_0) \) so that \( S_M > 4\pi/N \) for all \( M \geq M_*(N,k,N_0) \). The mismatch bivector \( \Sigma_M \) lies in the span of \( i\Gamma_a \), and the Killing form gives \( J(\Sigma_M) = (1/16) S_M^2 \) up to the principal branch normalization. For sufficiently large \( S_M \) this yields \( J(\Sigma_M) > \Jbound \) and hence \( \Jnorm(\Sigma_M) > 1 \).
\end{proof}

These lemmas provide the technical foundation for all arguments in Chapters~3--9.
