\begin{abstract}
Classical two-valued logic is the logic of a decided world: a
proposition is true or false, and a contradiction entails everything.
Dual Spacetime Theory is not a theory of decided worlds. Every massive
configuration is a pair of frames, usual and dual, and the invariant of
that pair is a single signed height \(\Jnorm\in[-1,1]\). Dual Spacetime
4-Valued Logic (D4L) is the logic of that height.

A proposition is an admissible torsion configuration. Measurement reads
\(\Jnorm\); the four names
\(\lvert T\rangle,\lvert U\rangle,\lvert F\rangle,\lvert B\rangle\)
are the sign and the saturation of the same number, not an external
algebra of labels. Negation is the usual--dual swap of the geometry.
Conjunction and disjunction are \(\min\) and \(\max\) on the height.
A configuration and its swap cannot both sit at the positive wall, so
contradiction does not explode.

The dual sector of the same geometry carries a Hilbert space
\(\mathbb{C}^2\). Amplitude operations and Hilbert operations belong to
one logic, related by an internal dictionary, but they are not
identified: the Killing form is indefinite, self-overlap is not a Born
probability, and the four labels are not a PVM of \(\mathbb{C}^2\).
Two designated predicates---synchrony and non-refutation---turn the
height into a consequence relation. Two-valued restrictions of the
interval fail to host the situations that relation is built to record.
The statements of this paper are machine-checked.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:position}

Two-valued logic is the natural language of a fact that has already
been decided. A proposition is true or it is false. If it is both, then
every other proposition follows: that is the explosion principle, and
it is why classical consequence cannot tolerate a live contradiction.

Dual Spacetime Theory does not describe decided facts. It describes a
pair of Minkowski frames carried by every massive particle---the usual
frame, in which we measure, and a dual frame, time-reversed relative to
the first \cite{DST2025,Diophantine2026}. On each of the three spatial
axes the pair is encoded by two rapidities \((\alpha_a,\beta_a)\): a
boost in the usual sector and a rotation in the dual sector. The
already-proved invariant of that pair is a signed height. When the two
frames are perfectly balanced the height vanishes. When the usual
sector dominates, the height is positive. When the dual sector
dominates, it is negative. At the walls the configuration is saturated:
every axis is a pure boost, or every axis is a pure rotation.

Those four situations are not extra labels glued onto an algebra. They
are the sign and the saturation of a quantity the geometry already
supplies. Dual Spacetime 4-Valued Logic is the decision to take that
quantity as the observable of a proposition, and to let the four names
be what the observable does.

The resulting logic has two layers, and they must not be confused.

The \emph{amplitude layer} takes an admissible configuration as a
proposition, the normalised height \(\Jnorm\) as its observable, and
\(\min/\max/-\) as scalar connectives. Overlap of two configurations is
the parameter Killing form already present in the PGA core. Interference
is the geometric commutator of their torsion bivectors. Nothing is added
to the algebra; the logic reads what the algebra already computes.

The \emph{dual Hilbert layer} is the kinematics of the dual sector: a
Euclidean pairing on \(\mathbb{R}^3\), the quaternion table of the
cyclic generators, and their representation on \(\mathbb{C}^2\). The
lattice of closed subspaces is the Birkhoff--von Neumann logic of a
two-dimensional spinor \cite{BvN1936}. It is a genuine quantum logic.
It is not the amplitude layer in another notation.

An internal dictionary relates the layers. Their operations are not
identified. The Killing form is not the spinor inner product;
\(\min/\max\) is not the meet and join of \(L(\mathbb{C}^2)\); the four
labels are not a projection-valued measure. There is no \(\hbar\), and
there is no derivation of the Born rule from \(J\).

The paper develops this picture in that order. Section~\ref{sec:torsion}
fixes the geometric carrier and the bound that makes four states
possible. Section~\ref{sec:states} names the states and splits the
balanced label by a second observable, mass, without adding a fifth
name. Sections~\ref{sec:conn} and~\ref{sec:geo} give the scalar
connectives, the two orders, and the geometric operations.
Section~\ref{sec:hilbert} extracts the dual Hilbert layer and states
the dictionary that refuses to identify it with the first.
Section~\ref{sec:cons} says which heights hold, and
Section~\ref{sec:examples} shows why two-valued restrictions of the
interval cannot host the situations D4L is built to record.
Section~\ref{sec:regime} reads DST proof artefacts and Diophantine
regimes in the same four names. Section~\ref{sec:claims} states the
reach of the theory, and its limits.

\section{Torsion as the carrier of a proposition}
\label{sec:torsion}

A proposition in D4L is not a sentence. It is a configuration of the
geometry: six rapidities, equivalently a torsion bivector and the rotor
it generates. The observable is a single scalar already proved in the
companion development \cite{Diophantine2026}.

Write \(\Omega=R_{\rm usual}^\dagger R_{\rm dual}\) for a rotor pair,
and let \(\Omega_{\rm biv}\) be a principal logarithm. Throughout this
paper the parameter form of the torsion scalar is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:J-raw}
J = \frac12\sum_{a=1}^3\bigl(\alpha_a^2-\beta_a^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
The combination \(\frac1{16}B(\Omega_{\rm biv},\Omega_{\rm biv})\) that
appears in older drafts differs from \eqref{eq:J-raw} by a constant
factor under the standard generator expansion. Boundedness statements
here always refer to \(J\) and to its normalisation \(\Jnorm\).

Not every six-tuple of rapidities is admitted. Usual and dual
rapidities on one axis are not allowed to add past a right angle: that
is the anti-synchronisation bound of the dual map. Together with
non-negativity it cuts out a compact cone.

\begin{definition}[Admissible configuration]
\label{def:admissible}
A configuration is \emph{admissible} when, on every axis,
\[
\alpha_a\ge 0,\qquad \beta_a\ge 0,\qquad \alpha_a+\beta_a\le \frac{\pi}{2}.
\]
\end{definition}

On that cone the signed height cannot run off to infinity. The
quadratic form \(J\) is bounded by the volume of the cone, and a
constant rescaling puts the bound at \(1\).

\begin{theorem}[Torsion bound]
\label{thm:torsion-bound}
On the admissible cone of Definition~\ref{def:admissible},
\[
|J|\le\Jbound,\qquad |\Jnorm|\le 1,
\]
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Jnorm}
\Jnorm := \frac{8}{3\pi^2}\,J.
\end{equation}
Equality \(|\Jnorm|=1\) holds if and only if every axis is a pure boost,
or every axis is a pure rotation. The image of \(\Jnorm\) on the
admissible cone is the whole interval \([-1,1]\). The bound is false if
one keeps only the principal-branch condition
\(|\alpha_a+\beta_a|\le\pi/2\) and allows negative rapidities.
\end{theorem}

The four names of D4L are therefore not a truncation. Every value in
\([-1,1]\) is attained, and both walls are attained, but only by
configurations in which every axis votes the same way. A mixed
extremum---one axis a pure boost, another a pure rotation---never
reaches \(|\Jnorm|=1\).

On a compactification \(N\) the discrete bound is sharper
\cite{Diophantine2026}:
\[
|\Jnorm|\le\Bigl(\frac{4\lfloor N/4\rfloor}{N}\Bigr)^2.
\]
When \(4\mid N\) the right-hand side is \(1\) and both signs are
attained. When \(4\nmid N\) every admissible lattice point satisfies the
strict inequality \(|\Jnorm|<1\): the positive wall is simply not there.

An \emph{amplitude} is an admissible continuous configuration
\(p=(\alpha_a,\beta_a)\). Its torsion bivector and rotor are
\[
\Omega(p)=\sum_{a=1}^3\Bigl(\frac{\alpha_a}{2}\,B^+_a+\frac{\beta_a}{2}\,B^-_a\Bigr),
\qquad
R(p)=\exp\Omega(p).
\]
Measurement and collapse are
\[
\langle p\rangle:=\Jnorm(p),\qquad
\mathrm{collapse}(p)
\in\{\lvert T\rangle,\lvert U\rangle,\lvert F\rangle,\lvert B\rangle\}.
\]
The adjoint is the usual--dual swap \(p^\dagger\), exchanging
\((\alpha_a)\) with \((\beta_a)\). The swap is an involution and flips
the observable: \((p^\dagger)^\dagger=p\) and
\(\langle p^\dagger\rangle=-\langle p\rangle\). Every label is realised
by some amplitude.

\section{Four states from a signed height}
\label{sec:states}

Once \(\Jnorm\) is known to take every value in \([-1,1]\), the four
names are a partition of that interval by sign and saturation.

\begin{definition}[D4L states]
\label{def:states}
On \(\Jnorm\in[-1,1]\),
\begin{align*}
\lvert T\rangle &\colon \Jnorm = 0
  &&\text{perfect usual--dual balance,}\\
\lvert U\rangle &\colon 0 < \Jnorm < 1
  &&\text{boost-dominant, not saturated,}\\
\lvert F\rangle &\colon \Jnorm = +1
  &&\text{every axis a pure boost,}\\
\lvert B\rangle &\colon -1\le \Jnorm < 0
  &&\text{rotation-dominant, including the}\\
&&& \text{deepest point \(\Jnorm=-1\).}
\end{align*}
\end{definition}

The positive extreme is a singleton named False. The negative extreme
is kept inside Both. That is a logical convention, not a gap in the
cone: both walls exist and are unique. On an admissible configuration,
collapse to \(\lvert F\rangle\) if and only if the configuration is
purely hyperbolic, and \(\Jnorm=-1\) if and only if it is purely
elliptic.

Interior \(\lvert B\rangle\) is a range, not a single fixed point. The
deepest point of that range is the wall \(\Jnorm=-1\). The four names
are therefore not a function of each other under adjoint: deepest
\(\lvert B\rangle\) maps to \(\lvert F\rangle\), while interior
\(\lvert B\rangle\) maps to \(\lvert U\rangle\). Negation, as a map on
labels, does not exist.

The balanced label \(\lvert T\rangle\) is likewise not a single
geometric point. The unsigned torsional mass
\[
M(p)=\frac12\sum_{a=1}^3\bigl(\alpha_a^2+\beta_a^2\bigr),\qquad
M_{\mathrm{norm}}=\frac{8}{3\pi^2}\,M
\]
is a second observable, not a fifth name. On the admissible cone
\(0\le M_{\mathrm{norm}}\le 1\). Usual--dual swap preserves \(M\);
uniform scaling multiplies \(M\) by \(k^2\). Height cannot exceed mass:
\(\lvert J\rvert\le M\), equivalently \(\lvert\Jnorm\rvert\le M_{\mathrm{norm}}\).
Equality holds if and only if one sector vanishes entirely---every
dual rapidity is zero, or every usual rapidity is zero. Saturation of
height relative to mass is purity of sector.

Vanishing height is still not a single geometric condition. The origin
of the cone is vacuum. Axiswise equality \(\alpha_a=\beta_a\) on every
axis is balanced massive. There is a third seat: a boost on one axis
can cancel a rotation on another, so that \(\Jnorm=0\) and \(M>0\)
without axiswise equality. The four-state classifier sees only the
height, and cannot tell these three apart.

Vacuum and balanced massive are predicates on amplitudes:
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{IsVacuum}(p)
  &\iff \mathrm{collapse}(p)=\lvert T\rangle \wedge M(p)=0,\\
\mathrm{IsBalancedMassive}(p)
  &\iff \mathrm{collapse}(p)=\lvert T\rangle \wedge 0<M(p).
\end{align*}
Vacuum is equivalent to all six rapidities vanishing, and implies
\(\Jnorm=0\). The converse is false. Beal's balanced seeds sit in the
axiswise seat; the cross-axis seat is a different geometric reason for
the same label.

\section{Connectives that do not explode}
\label{sec:conn}

The scalar operations on the signed height are the ones the real line
already possesses:
\[
\neg j = -j,\qquad
j\wedge k = \min(j,k),\qquad
j\vee k = \max(j,k).
\]
Negation is not a new connective. It is the usual--dual swap
\((\alpha_a,\beta_a)\mapsto(\beta_a,\alpha_a)\), which sends
\(J\mapsto -J\) and therefore \(\Jnorm\mapsto-\Jnorm\). De~Morgan
duality and distributivity hold as they do for \(\min/\max\) on
\(\mathbb{R}\).

The logical content is in what conjunction of a height with its
negation cannot do.

\begin{theorem}[Non-explosion]
\label{thm:nexplosion}
For every \(j\in[-1,1]\),
\[
j\wedge\neg j = -|j|\le 0.
\]
Hence \(p\wedge\neg p\) is \(\lvert T\rangle\) or \(\lvert B\rangle\),
and never saturates \(\lvert F\rangle\).
\end{theorem}

A configuration and its usual--dual swap cannot both sit at the
positive wall. Contradiction is therefore a rotation-dominant, or
balanced, situation. It is never the unique all-boost world. That is
why D4L can host a live contradiction without forcing every unrelated
atom onto \(\lvert F\rangle\).

The interval \([-1,1]\) carries two preorders, and they must not be
collapsed into one. The \emph{height} order is the ordinary order of
the real line: \(j\preceq_\tau k\) if and only if \(j\le k\). It
compares boost-dominance with rotation-dominance. The
\emph{information} order compares saturation: \(j\preceq_\iota k\) if
and only if \(\lvert j\rvert\le\lvert k\rvert\). In the information
order, \(j=0\) is the unique bottom, and the two tops are exactly the
walls \(j=\pm 1\). Equivalently, the information bottom is the label
\(\lvert T\rangle\), and the two tops are \(\lvert F\rangle\) and
deepest \(\lvert B\rangle\). Interior \(\lvert B\rangle\) is strictly
below deepest \(\lvert B\rangle\) in the information order.

The four labels therefore do not match Belnap's
\(\{\bot,t,f,\top\}\). No bilattice isomorphism is claimed. Height and
saturation are two readings of one number, not two independent
lattices.

\section{Overlap and interference}
\label{sec:geo}

Scalar connectives act after measurement. Before measurement, two
amplitudes already interact in the algebra. The commutative interaction
is overlap; the non-commutative interaction is interference.
\[
\langle p\mid q\rangle := 8\sum_{a=1}^3\bigl(\alpha_a\alpha'_a-\beta_a\beta'_a\bigr),
\qquad
[\Omega(p),\Omega(q)] := \Omega(p)\Omega(q)-\Omega(q)\Omega(p).
\]
Rotor composition \(R(p)R(q)\) is a product in the PGA. It is not
pulled back through the Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff formula to a third
torsion configuration.

The overlap is symmetric, and self-overlap is a constant multiple of
the observable: \(\langle p\mid p\rangle=16\,J(p)\), equivalently a
constant multiple of \(\Jnorm(p)\). Distinct-axis torsion bivectors
need not commute. If \(p\) is a pure boost on axis \(0\) and \(q\) a
pure rotation on axis \(1\), then \([\Omega(p),\Omega(q)]\ne 0\).
Same-axis hyperbolic and cyclic generators do commute, so the
corresponding one-axis interference vanishes. The converse is false:
vanishing interference does not force a common single axis. A two-axis
boost, for instance, has vanishing self-commutator and is not
single-axis.

Because self-overlap is a multiple of \(J\), it inherits the signature
of \(J\). Pure-boost extremals give \(\langle p\mid p\rangle>0\);
pure-rotation extremals give \(\langle q\mid q\rangle<0\). The pairing
is therefore indefinite. A signed quantity cannot be a Born
probability, and the pairing is not the spinor inner product of
Section~\ref{sec:spinor}. Sandwich evaluation of rotors is independent
of the gravity chart; preservation of the degenerate quadratic form is
not claimed. Geometric composition is associative as a PGA product and
is not a lattice operation.

\section{The dual sector as a Hilbert space}
\label{sec:hilbert}

The dual sector already contains a Euclidean three-space, a quaternion
table, and a spinor representation on \(\mathbb{C}^2\). This section
reads that kinematics as the second layer of D4L, and states the
dictionary that relates it to the first. Spatial axes are indexed by
\(a=0,1,2\).

\subsection{Two definite sectors}
\label{sec:sector}

A configuration is dual-sector when \(\alpha=0\), usual-sector when
\(\beta=0\). Write \(p_\beta\) and \(p_\alpha\) for the corresponding
embeddings of Euclidean \(\mathbb{R}^3\). On the dual sector the
Killing overlap restricts to
\[
\langle p_\beta\mid p_\gamma\rangle
=-8\langle\beta,\gamma\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^3},
\]
hence is negative definite. On the usual sector it restricts to
\(+8\langle\alpha,\gamma\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^3}\), hence is positive
definite. The inner-product space is the linear span of each sector,
not the admissible cube \(\alpha_a,\beta_a\ge 0\),
\(\alpha_a+\beta_a\le\pi/2\). Superposition is a statement about that
span.

\subsection{Quaternion table}
\label{sec:quat}

Write \(\Gamma_a\) for the cyclic generators of the dual sector. They
satisfy \(\Gamma_a^2=-1\) and the off-diagonal products of the
quaternion algebra:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_0\Gamma_1&=\Gamma_2,\\
\Gamma_1\Gamma_2&=\Gamma_0,\\
\Gamma_2\Gamma_0&=\Gamma_1,
\end{aligned}
\qquad
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_1\Gamma_0&=-\Gamma_2,\\
\Gamma_2\Gamma_1&=-\Gamma_0,\\
\Gamma_0\Gamma_2&=-\Gamma_1.
\end{aligned}
\]
The identities follow from the vector anticommutators in \(G(3,1,1)\).
For a pure dual configuration
\[
\Omega(p_\beta)=\sum_a(\beta_a/2)\,\Gamma_a,
\]
and on axis \(0\) this collapses to \((\theta/2)\,\Gamma_0\).

\subsection{Spinor Hilbert space}
\label{sec:spinor}

The dual spinor space is \(\mathbb{C}^2\) with the standard Hermitian
product. Pauli matrices \(\sigma_x,\sigma_y,\sigma_z\) are the usual
ones. The representation \(\rho(\Gamma_a):=-i\sigma_a\) is chosen so
that \(IJ=K\) agrees with the cyclic products in \(G(3,1,1)\) and so
that the dual rotor is sent to the standard \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) factor
\(\exp(-i\beta\cdot\sigma/2)\). Each \(\rho(\Gamma_a)\) squares to
\(-1\), the cyclic identity
\(\rho(\Gamma_0)\rho(\Gamma_1)=\rho(\Gamma_2)\) holds, and each
generator is anti-Hermitian.

The dual rotor matrix is therefore
\[
U(\beta)
=\exp\Bigl(\sum_a(\beta_a/2)(-i\sigma_a)\Bigr)
=\exp(-i\beta\cdot\sigma/2).
\]
On a single axis this is the Rodrigues formula
\(\cos(\theta/2)\,I+\sin(\theta/2)\,(-i\sigma_a)\). For a general dual
rapidity the same trigonometric form holds along the unit cyclic
combination determined by \(\beta\).

\begin{theorem}[Dual rotor in \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\)]
\label{thm:su2}
For every dual rapidity \(\beta\in\mathbb{R}^3\), the matrix \(U(\beta)\)
is unitary of determinant \(1\). Thus \(U(\beta)\in\mathrm{SU}(2)\).
On a pure dual configuration the Banach exponential in \(G(3,1,1)\)
collapses to the same Rodrigues formula, so the dual-sector exponential
and the matrix rotor agree on every pure dual rapidity.
\end{theorem}

A general identification of the Banach exponential in \(G(3,1,1)\) with
this matrix exponential for \emph{mixed} usual/dual or translational
generators is not claimed; that would require control of the
Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff formula beyond the pure dual sector.

Because \(\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}^2=2\), a projective measurement
has at most two orthogonal outcomes. A complementary pair of rays does
exist: the coordinate axes of the dual spinor are orthogonal. Four
pairwise orthogonal rays do not. The lattice has infinitely many
atoms---one-dimensional subspaces, one for each point of the complex
projective line---so the Hilbert layer is not a four-valued logic at
all. The four D4L labels cannot even enumerate those atoms.

\subsection[Quantum logic of C2]{Quantum logic of \(\mathbb{C}^2\)}
\label{sec:ql}

A quantum proposition is a linear subspace of \(\mathbb{C}^2\). Meet is
intersection, join is the sum, negation is the orthogonal complement.
Finite dimension makes every subspace closed. The resulting lattice is
orthomodular: if \(A\le B\) then \(A\vee(A^\perp\wedge B)=B\). It is not
distributive. With \(e_0=(1,0)\), \(e_1=(0,1)\), \(d=(1,1)\), and
\(\ell_v=\mathbb{C}v\),
\[
\ell_{e_0}\wedge(\ell_{e_1}\vee\ell_d)
\neq
(\ell_{e_0}\wedge\ell_{e_1})\vee(\ell_{e_0}\wedge\ell_d):
\]
the left side is \(\ell_{e_0}\), the right side is \(\{0\}\).

The scalar connectives of Section~\ref{sec:conn} distribute. A
distributive lattice cannot be isomorphic to the subspace lattice of a
Hilbert space of dimension at least two. There do not exist three
pairwise orthogonal one-dimensional subspaces of \(\mathbb{C}^2\). The
four D4L labels therefore cannot be four orthogonal rays, and the
amplitude connectives cannot present the Birkhoff--von Neumann lattice.

\subsection{Projectors and the Dirac spinor}
\label{sec:proj}

A primitive idempotent in the algebra must be built from generators
that square to \(+1\). If \(g^2=+1\), then \((1+g)/2\) is idempotent.
Write \(j:=e_0e_1\) for the hyperbolic generator of axis \(0\) (so
\(j^2=+1\)). This \(j\) is not the time vector \(\gamma^0=e_0\), which
squares to \(-1\) in signature \((-,+,+,+)\). The working projectors
\[
P_{\mathrm{spin}}=\frac{1+e_0e_1}{2},\qquad
P_L=\frac{1-e_1}{2},\qquad
P_R=\frac{1+e_1}{2}
\]
are each idempotent, and \(P_L+P_R=1\). The formulae
\((1\pm i)/2\) built from the \(\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)\) pseudoscalar \(i\)
with \(i^2=-1\) are not: \(\bigl((1-i)/2\bigr)^2=-i/2\). The product
\(P_{\mathrm{spin}}P_R\) is likewise not idempotent, because \(e_0e_1\)
and \(e_1\) anticommute.

A commuting square-\(+1\) pair does exist: \(e_1\) and \(e_0e_2\). Their
product projector
\[
P=\frac{1+e_1}{2}\cdot\frac{1+e_0e_2}{2}
\]
is idempotent and nonzero. The principal left ideal it generates has
real dimension exactly eight, matching the real dimension of a Dirac
spinor \(\mathbb{C}^4\). Irreducibility of that ideal as a spinor
module, and any identification of the ideal with the Dirac
representation, are not claimed.

The Dirac matrices of signature \((-,+,+,+)\) are the grade-1
generators \(\gamma^\mu\) of \(\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)\). They obey
\[
\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=2\eta^{\mu\nu},\qquad
\eta=\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1).
\]
In particular \((\gamma^0)^2=-1\), so \(\gamma^0\neq j\). The same
Clifford relations are realised by matrices in \(M_4(\mathbb{C})\)
acting on a Dirac spinor space \(\mathbb{C}^4\). That space has complex
dimension four, hence is not the dual spinor \(\mathbb{C}^2\); as real
vector spaces one has a Weyl splitting
\(\mathbb{C}^4\simeq\mathbb{C}^2\oplus\mathbb{C}^2\). The splitting is
a dictionary, not an identification of the dual-sector kinematics with
a chiral Dirac component. The Dirac equation, a mass term, and
covariance under the full rotor group are not derived here.

\subsection{Internal dictionary}
\label{sec:dict}

The two layers share a geometry. They do not share operations.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Amplitude layer & Dual Hilbert layer \\
\midrule
pure dual amplitude & \(\beta\in\mathbb{R}^3\) and \(U(\beta)\in\mathrm{SU}(2)\) \\
usual--dual swap & leaves the dual sector; not a Hilbert adjoint \\
Killing overlap & distinct from the spinor inner product \\
vanishing interference on one axis & commuting generators (necessary only) \\
\(\Jnorm\) four-state collapse & coarse-graining of a continuous scalar \\
\(\min/\max\) & not the meet/join of \(L(\mathbb{C}^2)\) \\
dual \(\mathbb{C}^2\) & not the Dirac \(\mathbb{C}^4\) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

There is no assignment of one-dimensional subspaces of \(\mathbb{C}^2\)
to the four D4L labels that makes distinct labels pairwise orthogonal.
The four names are a partition of an interval, not a projective
measurement of a qubit.

\section{Which heights hold}
\label{sec:cons}

The four labels are not yet a logic until one says which heights
\emph{hold}. D4L uses two designated predicates on \(\Jnorm\in[-1,1]\),
not one.

\begin{definition}[Designated values]
\label{def:desig}
\[
\mathrm{HoldsT}(j)\iff j=0,
\qquad
\mathrm{HoldsNotF}(j)\iff j<1.
\]
The first is synchrony (the label \(\lvert T\rangle\)). The second is
non-refutation (every label except \(\lvert F\rangle\)).
\end{definition}

The two predicates answer different questions. Synchrony asks whether
the usual and dual frames are balanced. Non-refutation asks only
whether the configuration has been driven onto the unique all-boost
world. A rotation-dominant configuration is not synchronous, but it is
not refuted either.

Formulas are generated by atoms, \(\neg\), \(\wedge\), and \(\vee\),
and are evaluated by the scalar operations already fixed on heights.
There is no implication connective on the amplitude heights. The
G\"odel residuum of \(\min\), with lattice top \(1\), is the wrong
operation for this logic. It designates a boost-dominant height
implying synchrony as synchrony itself, and it sends a
rotation-dominant height implying synchrony to the positive wall
\(\lvert F\rangle\). A logic whose designated values sit at balance,
and away from that wall, cannot use that residuum. Implication lives
on the discrete proof-status layer (Section~\ref{sec:regime}).

A valuation assigns each atom a height in \([-1,1]\). Consequence
follows the two predicates:
\(\Gamma\models_T\psi\) when every valuation that synchronises \(\Gamma\)
synchronises \(\psi\); \(\Gamma\models_{\lnot F}\psi\) is the same
clause with non-refutation.

Two-valued \emph{restrictions} of the interval are not unique, because
D4L's names \(\lvert T\rangle,\lvert F\rangle\) are not classical
true/false. The \emph{named} fragment is
\(\{0,1\}=\{\lvert T\rangle,\lvert F\rangle\}\). The \emph{wall}
fragment is \(\{-1,+1\}=\{\text{deepest }\lvert B\rangle,\lvert F\rangle\}\).
They behave differently under negation. On the named fragment,
\(\neg 0=0\): the balanced point is a fixed point of negation, and
\(\neg 1=-1\) leaves \(\{0,1\}\) altogether. The named fragment is
therefore not Boolean and not even closed. On the walls, \(\neg\)
swaps the two points and has no fixed point. The wall fragment is the
faithful classical core. Comparing D4L with ``true and false'' by
cutting to the names \(\lvert T\rangle\) and \(\lvert F\rangle\)
already leaves classical logic.

\section{What two-valued logic cannot host}
\label{sec:examples}

The difference between D4L and two-valued logic is not that D4L is a
stronger proof engine, nor that it decides more arithmetic. It is that
two-valued algebras cannot host certain situations of satisfaction,
consequence, and lattice structure that D4L hosts, and that the
difference can be fixed as a pair: emptiness (or vacuity, or collapse
to a singleton) on the fragment, realisation on D4L.

These are changes of semantics, not new theorems of Peano arithmetic.
Unconditional FLT / Beal and a refutation of G\"odel's theorems are
not claimed.

\subsection{A fixed point of negation}
\label{sec:ex-fp}

Classically, \(P\leftrightarrow\neg P\) has no model. On the wall
fragment the same is true: if \(j\in\{-1,+1\}\) then \(j\neq -j\).
In D4L the condition \(j=\neg j\) is equivalent to \(j=0\). The unique
solution is the label \(\lvert T\rangle\), perfect usual--dual
balance, and it is realised both by a constant valuation and by an
amplitude.

The equation is not the G\"odel sentence. It is the self-referential
\emph{equality} \(P=\neg P\), which an algebra whose negation has no
fixed point cannot host, and which D4L seats uniquely at synchrony.

\subsection{Contradiction without explosion}
\label{sec:ex-efq}

Classical two-valued logic has \emph{ex falso quodlibet}: from
\(\{P,\neg P\}\) every \(Q\) follows. On \(\mathrm{Bool}\) the premise
is already impossible, because \(P\) and \(\neg P\) cannot both be
\(\mathtt{true}\). On the walls, \(j\) and \(\neg j\) cannot both be
\(+1\). Two-valued explosion is therefore vacuous: the premises cannot
be designated together.

In D4L they can. Let \(P\) be atom \(0\) and \(Q\) atom \(1\). Then
\[
\{P,\neg P\}\not\models_T Q,\qquad
\{P,\neg P\}\not\models_{\lnot F} Q.
\]
One witness sends \(P\) to \(0\) and \(Q\) to \(1\): the contradictory
pair is synchronous, the unrelated atom is False. A second witness
sends \(P\) to \(-\tfrac12\) (interior \(\lvert B\rangle\)) and \(Q\)
to \(1\): the pair is non-refuted, the unrelated atom is still False.
The conjunction \(j\wedge\neg j\) is always non-refuted. An
inconsistent pair of observations therefore does not force an unrelated
atom onto the positive wall.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{The constraint \(\Jnorm<1\)}{The constraint Jnorm < 1}}
\label{sec:ex-notF}

A sentence forbidden from sitting at the unique all-boost world is
constrained by \(\mathrm{HoldsNotF}\), that is by \(\Jnorm<1\). On the
named fragment this constraint collapses to \(\mathrm{HoldsT}\): the
only remaining point is \(0\). On D4L the same constraint is inhabited
by three labels, \(\lvert T\rangle\), \(\lvert U\rangle\), and
\(\lvert B\rangle\). Non-refutation is not synchrony. The named
fragment cannot see the difference.

\subsection{Complementarity}
\label{sec:ex-comp}

The subspace lattice of \(\mathbb{C}^2\) is not distributive, hence not
a Boolean algebra. It has at most two pairwise orthogonal atoms. The
four D4L labels are not a PVM. The distributive scalar connectives
cannot present that lattice. Complementary rays in the dual Hilbert
layer cannot be assigned Boolean values, and they cannot be identified
with the four names of the amplitude layer. The two layers remain two
layers.

\section{Proof status and Diophantine regimes}
\label{sec:regime}

A DST proof artefact---a core lemma, a live bridge, a refuted
diagnostic, a bookkeeping identity---is not a torsion configuration.
It carries a discrete status that reuses the four names, but the
observable is not \(\Jnorm\). On this carrier, negation \emph{is} a
function of the four labels, and implication is a table, not the
residuum of \(\min\).

Negation and the lattice operations follow the amplitude
representatives \(\lvert T\rangle\mapsto 0\), \(\lvert U\rangle\mapsto 1/2\),
\(\lvert F\rangle\mapsto 1\), \(\lvert B\rangle\mapsto -1/2\). In
particular \(F\wedge\neg F=B\). A second operation, the establishedness
meet, packages two artefacts along \(F<B<U<T\), so that a proved core
with a live bridge is still live: the meet of \(T\) and \(U\) is \(U\).
Implication is
\[
\begin{aligned}
T\to\phi&=\phi,\\
F\to\phi&=T,\\
U\to T&=U,\\
B\to T&=U.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last two clauses are the point: an open or conflicted programme
does not establish a theorem. They also record why the residuum of
\(\min\) was refused. On the amplitude representatives,
\(U\to T\) would evaluate to synchrony and \(B\to T\) to the positive
wall; the table keeps both at a non-designated status. No Belnap-FOUR
isomorphism is claimed.
Designated predicates on statuses reuse the same two names:
\(\mathrm{HoldsT}_R\) means status \(T\);
\(\mathrm{HoldsNotF}_R\) means status \(\ne F\).

This is the algebra in which DST records what it has proved and what it
has not. Named two-valued logic \(\{T,F\}\) cannot host a live status
\(U\). The wall fragment \(\{B,F\}\) cannot host a proved core \(T\).
D4L realises the assignment core \(T\), diagnostic \(F\), live \(U\),
conjecture \(U\). The core alone does not \(T\)-entail the conjecture.
A window \(T\) together with an ill-posed NoGo \(B\) does not force the
conjecture off \(F\). The implication \(U\to T\) is not designated.

The same algebra hosts a Beal residual atlas: closed slices (including
the Fermat and Darmon--Merel positions, and the signature \((n,n,5)\))
sit at \(T\); refuted diagnostics sit at \(F\); bookkeeping identities
sit at \(B\); live residuals and the classical conjecture sit at \(U\).
The closed slices do not \(T\)-entail classical Beal. A dual-axis
Fermat atlas likewise places the additive core and the real \(L^p\)
dichotomy at \(T\), demoted single-axis modular and balanced-seed
diagnostics at \(F\), and the mixed-motor residual together with
classical FLT at \(U\). Closed Fermat slices do not \(T\)-entail
classical FLT.

This is why DST must not treat a single bound, or a refuted diagnostic,
as entailing the seven classical conjectures. The regime layer records
that prohibition. It does not prove any unproved bridge, and it does
not claim unconditional FLT, Beal, or abc.

Additive motors and multiplicative amplitudes occupy distinct seats at
the same label \(\lvert T\rangle\). Pure translation has vanishing
torsion and is vacuum. A balanced massive configuration is not vacuum.
The mixed product \(R\cdot T\) is not identified with
\(\exp(\Omega_{\mathrm{biv}})\) when the factors fail to commute. The
balanced residual class excludes window seeds: a half-window
configuration is not a balanced residual.

On the finite torus the same four names acquire a discrete reading.
When \(4\nmid N\) the sharp discrete bound is strict, so no admissible
point saturates \(\lvert F\rangle\). When \(4\mid N\) both walls are
attained: the all-boost lattice point realises \(\lvert F\rangle\), and
the all-rotation lattice point realises deepest \(\lvert B\rangle\). Pure-boost scaling multiplies \(\Jnorm\) by \(k^2\).
Synchrony is a fixed point. A label \(\lvert U\rangle\) can be driven
onto \(\lvert F\rangle\), or out of the admissible cone, where collapse
is undefined. That is the logical reading of the shared no-go; it is
not new arithmetic.

Vacuum is a fixed point of every admissible scaling. A small
balanced-massive seed stays at \(\lvert T\rangle\) with mass
\(k^2 M\) and may remain admissible; a large balanced seed leaves the
cone. That cone exit is invisible to the signed-height classifier:
\(\Jnorm\) stays \(0\), so the four-state label is still
\(\lvert T\rangle\). Mass and the cone predicate are the observables
that see it. This is why a Beal balanced seed \(\theta=\log 2/m\) can
survive \(m\)-fold scaling inside the cone.

Height and modular winding are two measurements. Real-scale
admissibility and nonzero winding cannot hold together. That
incompatibility is a separation of observables, not a Both-status on
one observable.

\section{A reading of self-reference}
\label{sec:godel}

Let \(G\) be the G\"odel sentence: ``\(G\) is not provable in the
system.'' In D4L, provability is read as a drive toward synchrony
\(\Jnorm\to 0\), i.e.\ toward \(\lvert T\rangle\). The honest constraint
on a sentence forbidden from sitting at the unique all-boost world is
exactly \(\mathrm{HoldsNotF}\) of Section~\ref{sec:ex-notF}:
\[
\Jnorm(G)<1.
\]
That constraint leaves \(G\) in \(\lvert T\rangle\), \(\lvert U\rangle\),
or \(\lvert B\rangle\) as labels, or at \(\lvert T\rangle\) if one
further demands synchrony. Conjunction does not explode
(Section~\ref{sec:ex-efq}).

This is a change of semantics, not a proof that G\"odel's theorems fail
in classical arithmetic. Completeness talk over the negative-torsion
sector is likewise a reading of admissible rotors, not a completion of
the Hilbert programme inside first-order Peano arithmetic. The content
of Section~\ref{sec:examples} is fixed; the reading in this section is
not a new incompleteness theorem.

\section{Scope}
\label{sec:claims}

D4L takes admissible torsion configurations as the carriers of
propositions and \(\Jnorm\) as the observable. It partitions
\([-1,1]\) into four named states, both walls attained on the
admissible cone, and it makes negation the usual--dual swap of the
geometry. Conjunction of a height with its negation stays out of
\(\lvert F\rangle\). Height and saturation are two preorders on one
interval. Height cannot exceed mass, with equality only when one
sector vanishes. The Killing pairing is indefinite; self-overlap is
not a Born probability; the dual sector yields a Euclidean
three-space, a quaternion table, and an \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) rotor on
\(\mathbb{C}^2\) whose closed-subspace lattice is orthomodular and
non-distributive, with infinitely many atoms and a complementary pair
of rays but not four.

Two designated predicates and two two-valued fragments turn that
picture into a consequence relation. The relation hosts a unique
negation fixed point at \(\lvert T\rangle\), refuses explosion of
\(\{P,\neg P\}\), inhabits \(\Jnorm<1\) by three labels, and records
the DST three-layer split---proved core, refuted diagnostic, open
bridge---without \(T\)-entailing a classical conjecture. Implication
lives on the status algebra, with \(U\to T=U\), because the residuum
of \(\min\) would designate that clause as synchrony and would send
\(B\to T\) to \(\lvert F\rangle\). Label \(\lvert T\rangle\) is not a
single geometric point: vacuum, axiswise balanced massive, and
cross-axis cancellation all sit there, distinguished by mass and by
axiswise equality, without a fifth name. Discrete \(\lvert F\rangle\)
is forbidden when \(4\nmid N\); when \(4\mid N\) both walls are
attained. Amplification is a partial map on labels, including a cone
exit of balanced massive that the height classifier cannot see.

The theory does not identify amplitude operations with Hilbert
operations, four labels with a PVM, vanishing interference with a
common single axis, or raw \(J\) with a value in \([-1,1]\). It does
not derive the Born rule, the Dirac equation, or a solution of the
measurement problem. It does not claim irreducibility of the commuting
left ideal, nor identify the Banach exponential with the matrix
exponential beyond the pure dual sector. It does not refute G\"odel's
theorems in classical arithmetic, and it does not claim unconditional
Beal, FLT, or abc.
