\begin{abstract}
Classical two-valued logic is the logic of a decided world: a
proposition is true or false, and a contradiction entails everything.
Dual Spacetime Theory describes a pair of frames, usual and dual,
carried by every massive configuration. The invariant of that pair is
one signed height \(\Jnorm\in[-1,1]\). Dual Spacetime 4-Valued Logic
(D4L) is the logic of that height.

A proposition is an admissible torsion configuration, and measurement
reads \(\Jnorm\). The four names partition the interval by sign and
saturation. \(\lvert T\rangle\) is balance (\(\Jnorm=0\)),
\(\lvert U\rangle\) is an unsaturated positive height,
\(\lvert F\rangle\) is the positive wall \(\Jnorm=+1\), and
\(\lvert B\rangle\) is every negative height, the negative wall
included. Negation is the usual--dual swap of the geometry.
Conjunction and disjunction are \(\min\) and \(\max\). A height and
its swap cannot both sit at the positive wall, so a contradiction
does not explode.

The dual sector of the same geometry carries a Hilbert space
\(\mathbb{C}^2\). The lattice of its closed subspaces is the
Birkhoff--von Neumann logic of a two-dimensional spinor. An internal
dictionary relates this layer to the amplitude layer. The two layers
stay distinct: the Killing form is indefinite, self-overlap is not a
Born probability, and the four labels are not a projection-valued
measure of \(\mathbb{C}^2\).

Two designated predicates, synchrony and non-refutation, turn the
height into a consequence relation. The names \(\lvert T\rangle\) and
\(\lvert F\rangle\) are a different pair from classical true and false.
On the two walls the positive wall is read as classical truth, and a
classical tautology is a formula that returns that wall under every
assignment of atoms to the two walls. The De~Morgan skeleton of a
classical proof holds at every real height. An equational step holds
at every real height exactly when it holds on the three heights
\(-1\), \(0\), and \(1\). Excluded middle and the syllogism are the
saturation \(|j|=1\). Material implication, modus tollens, disjunctive
syllogism, and the syllogism rule preserve the positive wall at every
real height. Discharge of a premise into a material implication holds
on the walls, and at balance the discharged formula returns synchrony.
For a finite set of premises, that forcing on the interval is decided by
assigning the atoms that occur to the three heights, and it splits into
classical consequence and the case distinction that uses balance.
Discharge of any finite depth agrees with classical consequence on the
walls and returns synchrony at total balance. Synchrony, non-refutation,
and positive-wall forcing are pairwise incomparable. On an island the
classical expansion detaches, and the four rules are the classical ones
on the island's bits. On the diagonal the expansion does not detach.
In the spinor lattice a
classical expansion detaches exactly when the antecedent splits along
the consequent, and each complementary pair determines a four-element
Boolean island. That island is two classical bits of one measurement.
It is a different object from the four amplitude labels. On a circle
divisible by four, four turns of aligned transverse gravitons express
one height, \(-\frac13\), and therefore one label \(\lvert B\rangle\).
Only the half turn and the full turn lie on the island of the
computational ray, and the expansion along that ray does not detach
onto either quarter turn. A separate
status algebra reuses the four names for proof artefacts and
Diophantine regimes, and an open classical conjecture stays off
synchrony. The statements of this paper are machine-checked.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:position}

Two-valued logic is the natural language of a fact that has already
been decided. A proposition is true or it is false. If it is both, then
every other proposition follows: that is the explosion principle, and
it is why classical consequence cannot tolerate a live contradiction.

Dual Spacetime Theory does not describe decided facts. It describes a
pair of Minkowski frames carried by every massive particle---the usual
frame, in which we measure, and a dual frame, time-reversed relative to
the first \cite{DST2025,Diophantine2026}. On each of the three spatial
axes the pair is encoded by two rapidities \((\alpha_a,\beta_a)\): a
boost in the usual sector and a rotation in the dual sector. The
already-proved invariant of that pair is a signed height. When the two
frames are perfectly balanced the height vanishes. When the usual
sector dominates, the height is positive. When the dual sector
dominates, it is negative. At the walls the configuration is saturated:
every axis is a pure boost, or every axis is a pure rotation.

Those four situations are not extra labels glued onto an algebra. They
are the sign and the saturation of a quantity the geometry already
supplies. Dual Spacetime 4-Valued Logic is the decision to take that
quantity as the observable of a proposition, and to let the four names
be what the observable does.

The resulting logic has two layers, and they must not be confused.

The \emph{amplitude layer} takes an admissible configuration as a
proposition, the normalised height \(\Jnorm\) as its observable, and
\(\min/\max/-\) as scalar connectives. Overlap of two configurations is
the parameter Killing form already present in the PGA core. Interference
is the geometric commutator of their torsion bivectors. Nothing is added
to the algebra; the logic reads what the algebra already computes.

The \emph{dual Hilbert layer} is the kinematics of the dual sector: a
Euclidean pairing on \(\mathbb{R}^3\), the quaternion table of the
cyclic generators, and their representation on \(\mathbb{C}^2\). The
lattice of closed subspaces is the Birkhoff--von Neumann logic of a
two-dimensional spinor \cite{BvN1936}. It is a genuine quantum logic.
It is not the amplitude layer in another notation.

An internal dictionary relates the layers. Their operations are not
identified. The Killing form is not the spinor inner product;
\(\min/\max\) is not the meet and join of \(L(\mathbb{C}^2)\); the four
labels are not a projection-valued measure. There is no \(\hbar\), and
there is no derivation of the Born rule from \(J\).

The paper develops this picture in that order. Section~\ref{sec:torsion}
fixes the geometric carrier and the bound that makes four states
possible. Section~\ref{sec:states} names the states and splits the
balanced label by a second observable, mass, without adding a fifth
name. Sections~\ref{sec:conn} and~\ref{sec:geo} give the scalar
connectives, the two orders, and the geometric operations.
Section~\ref{sec:hilbert} extracts the dual Hilbert layer and states
the dictionary that refuses to identify it with the first.
Section~\ref{sec:cons} says which heights hold.
Section~\ref{sec:wall} separates a classical proof into a height
identity, decided by the three heights \(-1\), \(0\), and \(1\), a
saturation on the walls, the three cuts of material implication, and
the positive-wall rules whose discharge is special to the walls. Section~\ref{sec:examples}
shows why two-valued restrictions of the interval cannot host the
situations D4L is built to record. Section~\ref{sec:block} names the
splitting on which a classical expansion detaches in the spinor
lattice, the island of a complementary pair, and the sense in which
that island reads as two classical bits of one measurement.
Section~\ref{sec:turns} applies both layers to aligned gravitons:
four turns express one height, two of them lie on one island, and
the device error carries other labels.
Section~\ref{sec:regime} reads DST proof artefacts and Diophantine
regimes in the same four names. Section~\ref{sec:godel} reads a
self-referential sentence by the same two predicates, and it does not
add an incompleteness theorem. Section~\ref{sec:claims} states the
reach of the theory, and its limits.

\section{Torsion as the carrier of a proposition}
\label{sec:torsion}

A proposition in D4L is not a sentence. It is a configuration of the
geometry: six rapidities, equivalently a torsion bivector and the rotor
it generates. The observable is a single scalar already proved in the
companion development \cite{Diophantine2026}.

Write \(\Omega=R_{\rm usual}^\dagger R_{\rm dual}\) for a rotor pair,
and let \(\Omega_{\rm biv}\) be a principal logarithm. The three
spatial axes are labelled \(a=0,1,2\), in agreement with the Lean
development. The companion paper writes the same three axes as
\(a=1,2,3\). Throughout this paper the parameter form of the torsion
scalar is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:J-raw}
J = \frac12\sum_{a=0}^{2}\bigl(\alpha_a^2-\beta_a^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
Under the generator expansion
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omega-exp}
\Omega=\sum_{a=0}^{2}\Bigl(\frac{\alpha_a}{2}\,B^+_a
+\frac{\beta_a}{2}\,B^-_a\Bigr),
\end{equation}
the norms \(B(B^+_a,B^+_a)=+8\) and \(B(B^-_a,B^-_a)=-8\) give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:killing-omega}
B(\Omega,\Omega)=2\sum_{a=0}^{2}(\alpha_a^2-\beta_a^2).
\end{equation}
Thus \(J\) is four times the older combination
\(\frac1{16}B(\Omega,\Omega)\). The companion writes the same bivector
with \(B^+_a=i\Gamma_a\) and \(B^-_a=\Gamma_a\). The pseudoscalar \(i\)
commutes with every torsional bivector. Boundedness statements here
always refer to \(J\) and to its normalisation \(\Jnorm\).

Not every six-tuple of rapidities is admitted. Usual and dual
rapidities on one axis are not allowed to add past a right angle.
Together with non-negativity, that sum bound cuts out a compact cone.
The parent theory reserves anti-synchronisation for the rotor condition
\(R_{\rm dual}=R_{\rm usual}^\dagger\), which on one axis forces both
bivector coefficients to vanish and is not this inequality.

\begin{definition}[Admissible configuration]
\label{def:admissible}
A configuration is \emph{admissible} when, on every axis,
\[
\alpha_a\ge 0,\qquad \beta_a\ge 0,\qquad \alpha_a+\beta_a\le \frac{\pi}{2}.
\]
\end{definition}

On that cone the signed height is bounded by an axiswise estimate.
Admissibility forces \(0\le\alpha_a,\beta_a\le\pi/2\), and therefore
\(|\alpha_a^2-\beta_a^2|\le(\pi/2)^2\), with equality only when the
axis is a pure boost or a pure rotation. Summing the three axes and
halving yields \(|J|\le\Jbound\). The normalisation below places that
ceiling at \(1\).

\begin{theorem}[Torsion bound]
\label{thm:torsion-bound}
On the admissible cone of Definition~\ref{def:admissible},
\[
|J|\le\Jbound,\qquad |\Jnorm|\le 1,
\]
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Jnorm}
\Jnorm := \frac{8}{3\pi^2}\,J.
\end{equation}
Equality \(|\Jnorm|=1\) holds if and only if every axis is a pure boost,
or every axis is a pure rotation. The image of \(\Jnorm\) on the
admissible cone is the whole interval \([-1,1]\). The bound is false if
one keeps only the principal-branch condition
\(|\alpha_a+\beta_a|\le\pi/2\) and allows negative rapidities.
\end{theorem}

The four names of D4L are therefore not a truncation. Every value in
\([-1,1]\) is attained, and both walls are attained, but only by
configurations in which every axis votes the same way. A mixed
extremum---one axis a pure boost, another a pure rotation---never
reaches \(|\Jnorm|=1\).

On a compactification \(N\) the discrete bound is sharper
\cite{Diophantine2026}:
\[
|\Jnorm|\le\Bigl(\frac{4\lfloor N/4\rfloor}{N}\Bigr)^2.
\]
When \(4\mid N\) the right-hand side is \(1\) and both signs are
attained. When \(4\nmid N\) every admissible lattice point satisfies the
strict inequality \(|\Jnorm|<1\): the positive wall is simply not there.

An \emph{amplitude} is an admissible continuous configuration
\(p=(\alpha_a,\beta_a)\). Its torsion bivector is \eqref{eq:omega-exp},
and its rotor is \(R(p)=\exp\Omega(p)\).
Measurement and collapse are
\[
\langle p\rangle:=\Jnorm(p),\qquad
\mathrm{collapse}(p)
\in\{\lvert T\rangle,\lvert U\rangle,\lvert F\rangle,\lvert B\rangle\}.
\]
The adjoint is the usual--dual swap \(p^\dagger\), exchanging
\((\alpha_a)\) with \((\beta_a)\). The swap is an involution and flips
the observable: \((p^\dagger)^\dagger=p\) and
\(\langle p^\dagger\rangle=-\langle p\rangle\). Every label is realised
by some amplitude.

\section{Four states from a signed height}
\label{sec:states}

Once \(\Jnorm\) is known to take every value in \([-1,1]\), the four
names are a partition of that interval by sign and saturation.

\begin{definition}[D4L states]
\label{def:states}
On \(\Jnorm\in[-1,1]\),
\begin{align*}
\lvert T\rangle &\colon \Jnorm = 0
  &&\text{perfect usual--dual balance,}\\
\lvert U\rangle &\colon 0 < \Jnorm < 1
  &&\text{boost-dominant, not saturated,}\\
\lvert F\rangle &\colon \Jnorm = +1
  &&\text{every axis a pure boost,}\\
\lvert B\rangle &\colon -1\le \Jnorm < 0
  &&\text{rotation-dominant, including the}\\
&&& \text{deepest point \(\Jnorm=-1\).}
\end{align*}
\end{definition}

The positive extreme is a singleton named False. The negative extreme
is kept inside Both. That is a logical convention, not a gap in the
cone: both walls exist and are unique. On an admissible configuration,
collapse to \(\lvert F\rangle\) if and only if the configuration is
purely hyperbolic, and \(\Jnorm=-1\) if and only if it is purely
elliptic.

Interior \(\lvert B\rangle\) is a range, not a single fixed point. The
deepest point of that range is the wall \(\Jnorm=-1\). The four names
are therefore not a function of each other under adjoint: deepest
\(\lvert B\rangle\) maps to \(\lvert F\rangle\), while interior
\(\lvert B\rangle\) maps to \(\lvert U\rangle\). On the amplitude layer
there is no negation of the four names. Section~\ref{sec:regime}
defines one, on the separate carrier of proof status.

The balanced label \(\lvert T\rangle\) is likewise not a single
geometric point. The unsigned torsional mass
\[
M(p)=\frac12\sum_{a=0}^{2}\bigl(\alpha_a^2+\beta_a^2\bigr),\qquad
M_{\mathrm{norm}}=\frac{8}{3\pi^2}\,M
\]
is a second observable, not a fifth name. On the admissible cone
\(0\le M_{\mathrm{norm}}\le 1\). Usual--dual swap preserves \(M\);
uniform scaling multiplies \(M\) by \(k^2\). Height cannot exceed mass:
\(\lvert J\rvert\le M\), equivalently \(\lvert\Jnorm\rvert\le M_{\mathrm{norm}}\).
Equality holds if and only if one sector vanishes entirely---every
dual rapidity is zero, or every usual rapidity is zero. Saturation of
height relative to mass is purity of sector.

Vanishing height is still not a single geometric condition. The origin
of the cone is vacuum. Axiswise equality \(\alpha_a=\beta_a\) on every
axis is balanced massive. There is a third seat: a boost on one axis
can cancel a rotation on another, so that \(\Jnorm=0\) and \(M>0\)
without axiswise equality. The four-state classifier sees only the
height, and cannot tell these three apart.

Vacuum and balanced massive are predicates on amplitudes:
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{IsVacuum}(p)
  &\iff \mathrm{collapse}(p)=\lvert T\rangle \wedge M(p)=0,\\
\mathrm{IsBalancedMassive}(p)
  &\iff \mathrm{collapse}(p)=\lvert T\rangle \wedge 0<M(p).
\end{align*}
Vacuum is equivalent to all six rapidities vanishing, and implies
\(\Jnorm=0\). The converse is false. Beal's balanced seeds sit in the
axiswise seat; the cross-axis seat is a different geometric reason for
the same label.

\section{Connectives that do not explode}
\label{sec:conn}

The scalar operations on the signed height are the ones the real line
already possesses:
\[
\neg j = -j,\qquad
j\wedge k = \min(j,k),\qquad
j\vee k = \max(j,k).
\]
Negation is not a new connective. It is the usual--dual swap
\((\alpha_a,\beta_a)\mapsto(\beta_a,\alpha_a)\), which sends
\(J\mapsto -J\) and therefore \(\Jnorm\mapsto-\Jnorm\). De~Morgan
duality and distributivity hold as they do for \(\min/\max\) on
\(\mathbb{R}\).

The logical content is in what conjunction of a height with its
negation cannot do.

\begin{theorem}[Non-explosion]
\label{thm:nexplosion}
For every \(j\in[-1,1]\),
\[
j\wedge\neg j = -|j|\le 0.
\]
Hence \(p\wedge\neg p\) is \(\lvert T\rangle\) or \(\lvert B\rangle\),
and never saturates \(\lvert F\rangle\).
\end{theorem}

The dual identity is \(j\vee\neg j=\lvert j\rvert\). Excluded middle
is synchronous only at balance, and on the interval it is refuted
exactly at the two walls.

A configuration and its usual--dual swap cannot both sit at the
positive wall. Contradiction is therefore a rotation-dominant, or
balanced, situation. It is never the unique all-boost world. That is
why D4L can host a live contradiction without forcing every unrelated
atom onto \(\lvert F\rangle\). Refusal of explosion is the feature
isolated in paraconsistent logic \cite{Priest2006}. Here the carrier
is the signed height, and the designated predicates are synchrony and
non-refutation (Section~\ref{sec:cons}).

The interval \([-1,1]\) carries two preorders, and they must not be
collapsed into one. The \emph{height} order is the ordinary order of
the real line: \(j\preceq_\tau k\) if and only if \(j\le k\). It
compares boost-dominance with rotation-dominance. The
\emph{information} order compares saturation: \(j\preceq_\iota k\) if
and only if \(\lvert j\rvert\le\lvert k\rvert\). In the information
order, \(j=0\) is the unique bottom, and the two tops are exactly the
walls \(j=\pm 1\). Equivalently, the information bottom is the label
\(\lvert T\rangle\), and the two tops are \(\lvert F\rangle\) and
deepest \(\lvert B\rangle\). Interior \(\lvert B\rangle\) is strictly
below deepest \(\lvert B\rangle\) in the information order.

The four labels therefore do not match Belnap's
\(\{\bot,t,f,\top\}\). No bilattice isomorphism is claimed. Height and
saturation are two readings of one number, not two independent
lattices.

\section{Overlap and interference}
\label{sec:geo}

Scalar connectives act after measurement. Before measurement, two
amplitudes already interact in the algebra. The commutative interaction
is overlap; the non-commutative interaction is interference.
\[
\langle p\mid q\rangle := 8\sum_{a=0}^{2}\bigl(\alpha_a\alpha'_a-\beta_a\beta'_a\bigr),
\qquad
[\Omega(p),\Omega(q)] := \Omega(p)\Omega(q)-\Omega(q)\Omega(p).
\]
Rotor composition \(R(p)R(q)\) is a product in the PGA. It is not
pulled back through the Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff formula to a third
torsion configuration.

The overlap is the parameter-space pairing with generator norms
\(\pm 8\). It is symmetric. On one configuration it equals
\(16\,J(p)\), and therefore \(6\pi^2\,\Jnorm(p)\). That value is four
times the bivector Killing form \(B(\Omega(p),\Omega(p))\) of
Section~\ref{sec:torsion}. Distinct-axis torsion bivectors
need not commute. If \(p\) is a pure boost on axis \(0\) and \(q\) a
pure rotation on axis \(1\), then \([\Omega(p),\Omega(q)]\ne 0\).
Same-axis hyperbolic and cyclic generators do commute, so the
corresponding one-axis interference vanishes. The converse is false:
vanishing interference does not force a common single axis. A two-axis
boost, for instance, has vanishing self-commutator and is not
single-axis.

Because self-overlap is a multiple of \(J\), it inherits the signature
of \(J\). Pure-boost extremals give \(\langle p\mid p\rangle>0\);
pure-rotation extremals give \(\langle q\mid q\rangle<0\). The pairing
is therefore indefinite. A signed quantity cannot be a Born
probability, and the pairing is not the spinor inner product of
Section~\ref{sec:spinor}. Sandwich evaluation of rotors is independent
of the gravity chart; preservation of the degenerate quadratic form is
not claimed. Geometric composition is associative as a PGA product and
is not a lattice operation.

\section{The dual sector as a Hilbert space}
\label{sec:hilbert}

The dual sector already contains a Euclidean three-space, a quaternion
table, and a spinor representation on \(\mathbb{C}^2\). This section
reads that kinematics as the second layer of D4L, and states the
dictionary that relates it to the first. Axes stay labelled
\(a=0,1,2\), as fixed in Section~\ref{sec:torsion}.

\subsection{Two definite sectors}
\label{sec:sector}

A configuration is dual-sector when \(\alpha=0\), usual-sector when
\(\beta=0\). Write \(p_\beta\) and \(p_\alpha\) for the corresponding
embeddings of Euclidean \(\mathbb{R}^3\). On the dual sector the
Killing overlap restricts to
\[
\langle p_\beta\mid p_\gamma\rangle
=-8\langle\beta,\gamma\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^3},
\]
hence is negative definite. On the usual sector it restricts to
\(+8\langle\alpha,\gamma\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^3}\), hence is positive
definite. The inner-product space is the linear span of each sector,
not the admissible cone \(\alpha_a,\beta_a\ge 0\),
\(\alpha_a+\beta_a\le\pi/2\). Superposition in that span is the internal
sum, which has no ceiling. The expressed mismatch is the point of the
cone closest to the sum.

\subsection{Quaternion table}
\label{sec:quat}

Write \(\Gamma_a\) for the cyclic generators of the dual sector. They
satisfy \(\Gamma_a^2=-1\) and the off-diagonal products of the
quaternion algebra:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_0\Gamma_1&=\Gamma_2,\\
\Gamma_1\Gamma_2&=\Gamma_0,\\
\Gamma_2\Gamma_0&=\Gamma_1,
\end{aligned}
\qquad
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_1\Gamma_0&=-\Gamma_2,\\
\Gamma_2\Gamma_1&=-\Gamma_0,\\
\Gamma_0\Gamma_2&=-\Gamma_1.
\end{aligned}
\]
The identities follow from the vector anticommutators in \(G(3,1,1)\).
For a pure dual configuration
\[
\Omega(p_\beta)=\sum_a(\beta_a/2)\,\Gamma_a,
\]
and on axis \(0\) this collapses to \((\theta/2)\,\Gamma_0\).

\subsection{Spinor Hilbert space}
\label{sec:spinor}

The dual spinor space is \(\mathbb{C}^2\) with the standard Hermitian
product. Pauli matrices \(\sigma_x,\sigma_y,\sigma_z\) are the usual
ones. The representation \(\rho(\Gamma_a):=-i\sigma_a\) is chosen so
that \(IJ=K\) agrees with the cyclic products in \(G(3,1,1)\) and so
that the dual rotor is sent to the standard \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) factor
\(\exp(-i\beta\cdot\sigma/2)\). Each \(\rho(\Gamma_a)\) squares to
\(-1\), the cyclic identity
\(\rho(\Gamma_0)\rho(\Gamma_1)=\rho(\Gamma_2)\) holds, and each
generator is anti-Hermitian.

The dual rotor matrix is therefore
\[
U(\beta)
=\exp\Bigl(\sum_a(\beta_a/2)(-i\sigma_a)\Bigr)
=\exp(-i\beta\cdot\sigma/2).
\]
On a single axis this is the Rodrigues formula
\(\cos(\theta/2)\,I+\sin(\theta/2)\,(-i\sigma_a)\). For a general dual
rapidity the same trigonometric form holds along the unit cyclic
combination determined by \(\beta\).

\begin{theorem}[Dual rotor in \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\)]
\label{thm:su2}
For every dual rapidity \(\beta\in\mathbb{R}^3\), the matrix \(U(\beta)\)
is unitary of determinant \(1\). Thus \(U(\beta)\in\mathrm{SU}(2)\).
On a pure dual configuration the Banach exponential in \(G(3,1,1)\)
collapses to the same Rodrigues formula, so the dual-sector exponential
and the matrix rotor agree on every pure dual rapidity.
\end{theorem}

The usual sector is the twin, with opposite signature. Distinct
hyperbolic generators anticommute and each squares to \(+1\), so the
combination \(\Gamma^+(\alpha)=\sum_a\alpha_a B^+_a\) satisfies
\(\Gamma^+(\alpha)^2=\lVert\alpha\rVert^2\). The Banach exponential on a
pure usual rapidity is therefore the hyperbolic Rodrigues formula
\[
\exp\bigl(\Omega(p_\alpha)\bigr)
=\cosh(\lVert\alpha\rVert/2)\,I
+\sinh(\lVert\alpha\rVert/2)\,U^+(\alpha),
\]
and on axis \(0\) this is
\(\cosh(\theta/2)\,I+\sinh(\theta/2)\,B^+_0\). Representing the same
generators by Pauli matrices rather than \(-i\sigma_a\) sends the usual
rotor to
\[
V(\alpha)=\exp(\alpha\cdot\sigma/2).
\]
For every usual rapidity, \(V(\alpha)\) is Hermitian of determinant
\(1\), and the matrix exponential agrees with the Banach exponential on
every pure usual configuration. A nonzero boost is not unitary: the
usual rotor is not an element of \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\). The set of
Hermitian determinant-\(1\) matrices is not claimed to be a group under
multiplication.

This is the kinematics of the positive wall. Height equals mass if and
only if one sector vanishes; the usual exponential saturates
\(\lvert F\rangle\) as the dual exponential saturates deepest
\(\lvert B\rangle\).

A general identification of the Banach exponential in \(G(3,1,1)\) with
this matrix exponential for \emph{mixed} usual/dual or translational
generators is not claimed; that would require control of the
Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff formula beyond a single pure sector. The
product of a usual boost with a dual rotation is not identified with an
\(\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\) factorisation.

Because \(\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}^2=2\), a projective measurement
has at most two orthogonal outcomes. A complementary pair of rays does
exist: the coordinate axes of the dual spinor are orthogonal. Four
pairwise orthogonal rays do not. The lattice has infinitely many
atoms---one-dimensional subspaces, one for each point of the complex
projective line---so the Hilbert layer is not a four-valued logic at
all. The four D4L labels cannot even enumerate those atoms.

\subsection[Quantum logic of C2]{Quantum logic of \(\mathbb{C}^2\)}
\label{sec:ql}

A quantum proposition is a linear subspace of \(\mathbb{C}^2\). Meet is
intersection, join is the sum, negation is the orthogonal complement.
Finite dimension makes every subspace closed. The resulting lattice is
orthomodular: if \(A\le B\) then \(A\vee(A^\perp\wedge B)=B\). It is not
distributive. With \(e_0=(1,0)\), \(e_1=(0,1)\), \(d=(1,1)\), and
\(\ell_v=\mathbb{C}v\),
\[
\ell_{e_0}\wedge(\ell_{e_1}\vee\ell_d)
\neq
(\ell_{e_0}\wedge\ell_{e_1})\vee(\ell_{e_0}\wedge\ell_d):
\]
the left side is \(\ell_{e_0}\), the right side is \(\{0\}\).

The scalar connectives of Section~\ref{sec:conn} distribute. A
distributive lattice cannot be isomorphic to the subspace lattice of a
Hilbert space of dimension at least two. There do not exist three
pairwise orthogonal one-dimensional subspaces of \(\mathbb{C}^2\). The
four D4L labels therefore cannot be four orthogonal rays, and the
amplitude connectives cannot present the Birkhoff--von Neumann lattice.

\subsection{Projectors and the Dirac spinor}
\label{sec:proj}

A primitive idempotent in the algebra must be built from generators
that square to \(+1\). If \(g^2=+1\), then \((1+g)/2\) is idempotent.
Write \(j:=e_0e_1\) for the hyperbolic generator of axis \(0\) (so
\(j^2=+1\)). This \(j\) is not the time vector \(\gamma^0=e_0\), which
squares to \(-1\) in signature \((-,+,+,+)\). The working projectors
\[
P_{\mathrm{spin}}=\frac{1+e_0e_1}{2},\qquad
P_L=\frac{1-e_1}{2},\qquad
P_R=\frac{1+e_1}{2}
\]
are each idempotent, and \(P_L+P_R=1\). The formulae
\((1\pm i)/2\) built from the \(\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)\) pseudoscalar \(i\)
with \(i^2=-1\) are not: \(\bigl((1-i)/2\bigr)^2=-i/2\). The product
\(P_{\mathrm{spin}}P_R\) is likewise not idempotent, because \(e_0e_1\)
and \(e_1\) anticommute.

A commuting square-\(+1\) pair does exist: \(e_1\) and \(e_0e_2\). Their
product projector
\[
P=\frac{1+e_1}{2}\cdot\frac{1+e_0e_2}{2}
\]
is idempotent and nonzero. The principal left ideal it generates has
real dimension exactly eight. It is the direct sum of the
four-dimensional left ideal \(\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)\,P\) in the Minkowski
subalgebra and a copy of that ideal along the null generator
\(e_4\); the copy is a proper submodule for \(G(3,1,1)\), so the
eight-dimensional ideal is reducible as a PGA-module. The number
eight matches the real dimension of a Dirac spinor \(\mathbb{C}^4\),
but the match is doubling along the null direction, not evidence
that the eight-dimensional ideal is an irreducible Dirac module.
Irreducibility of the Minkowski core as a \(\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)\)-module,
and identification of either ideal with the matrix representation,
are not claimed.

The Dirac matrices of signature \((-,+,+,+)\) are the grade-1
generators \(\gamma^\mu\) of \(\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)\). They obey
\[
\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=2\eta^{\mu\nu},\qquad
\eta=\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1).
\]
In particular \((\gamma^0)^2=-1\), so \(\gamma^0\neq j\). The same
Clifford relations are realised by matrices in \(M_4(\mathbb{C})\)
acting on a Dirac spinor space \(\mathbb{C}^4\). That space has complex
dimension four, hence is not the dual spinor \(\mathbb{C}^2\); as real
vector spaces one has a Weyl splitting
\(\mathbb{C}^4\simeq\mathbb{C}^2\oplus\mathbb{C}^2\). The algebraic
reason for the split is chirality. With
\(\gamma^5=-i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3\) one has
\((\gamma^5)^2=I\) and \(\{\gamma^5,\gamma^\mu\}=0\). The Weyl
projectors
\[
P_\pm=\frac{1\pm\gamma^5}{2}
\]
are complementary idempotents. The upper Weyl block is the
\(+1\) eigenspace of \(\gamma^5\); the lower block is the \(-1\)
eigenspace. The splitting is a dictionary, not an identification of the
dual-sector kinematics with a chiral Dirac component.

The same Clifford product already contains a first-order factor of the
Minkowski wave operator. For a four-momentum \(p\) write
\[
Q(p)=-p_0^2+p_1^2+p_2^2+p_3^2,\qquad
\gamma(p)=\sum_{\mu}p_\mu\gamma^\mu.
\]
The relations of \(\mathrm{Cl}(3,1)\) give \(\gamma(p)^2=Q(p)\) in the
algebra, and the same identity as matrices on \(\mathbb{C}^4\). The mass
shell \(Q(p)=-m^2\) is the vanishing of a quadratic that factors as
\((\gamma(p)-im)(\gamma(p)+im)\). A spinor with
\(\gamma(p)\Psi=im\Psi\) therefore lies on that shell: this is the
Dirac equation in momentum space, not an extra postulate. In the Weyl
representation \(\gamma(p)\) is off-diagonal. Vanishing mass decouples
the two chiral blocks; a nonzero mass mixes them. The two copies of
\(\mathbb{C}^2\) that make up a Dirac spinor are the two chiral halves
of one massive fermion, not two independent particles. Chirality
\(\gamma^5\) sends a solution of mass \(m\) to a solution of mass
\(-m\). Rest-frame solutions exist. A spacetime PDE, covariance under
the full rotor group, and the identification of \(m\) with torsional
lag are not claimed.

\section{Which heights hold}
\label{sec:cons}

The four labels are not yet a logic until one says which heights
\emph{hold}. D4L uses two designated predicates on \(\Jnorm\in[-1,1]\),
not one.

\begin{definition}[Designated values]
\label{def:desig}
\[
\mathrm{HoldsT}(j)\iff j=0,
\qquad
\mathrm{HoldsNotF}(j)\iff j<1.
\]
The first is synchrony (the label \(\lvert T\rangle\)). The second is
non-refutation (every label except \(\lvert F\rangle\)).
\end{definition}

The two predicates answer different questions. Synchrony asks whether
the usual and dual frames are balanced. Non-refutation asks only
whether the configuration has been driven onto the unique all-boost
world. A rotation-dominant configuration is not synchronous, but it is
not refuted either.

The designated point is the information bottom, not the top of the
height order, and the two predicates read the connectives in opposite
directions. Non-refutation of a conjunction is the disjunction of the
non-refutations: a meet reaches the positive wall only when both
heights already sit there. Non-refutation of a disjunction is the
conjunction of the non-refutations: a join reaches that wall as soon
as either argument does. Synchrony is stricter, and it absorbs. The
conjunction of balance with any nonnegative height, the positive wall
included, is balance again; the disjunction of balance with any
nonpositive height is balance again. In particular \(0\wedge 1=0\):
the positive wall does not absorb conjunction.

Formulas are generated by atoms, \(\neg\), \(\wedge\), and \(\vee\),
and are evaluated by the scalar operations already fixed on heights.
An implication on those heights would have to answer both designated
predicates at once. Each predicate has a residual of its own. The
synchrony residual returns the consequent when the antecedent is
balanced, and balance otherwise. The non-refutation residual returns
balance when the antecedent sits on the positive wall, returns that
wall when a non-refuted antecedent implies it, and otherwise returns
the consequent.
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:residuals}
j\to_T k=
\begin{cases}
k & \text{if }j=0,\\
0 & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\qquad
j\to_{\lnot F} k=
\begin{cases}
0 & \text{if }j=1,\\
1 & \text{if }j<1\text{ and }k=1,\\
k & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Thus \(j\to_T k\) is synchronous exactly when a synchronous antecedent
forces a synchronous consequent, and \(j\to_{\lnot F} k\) is
non-refuted exactly when a non-refuted antecedent forces a non-refuted
consequent.

\begin{theorem}[No common residual]
\label{thm:no-common-imp}
No binary operation on \([-1,1]\) is synchronous exactly when a
synchronous antecedent forces a synchronous consequent, and non-refuted
exactly when a non-refuted antecedent forces a non-refuted consequent.
\end{theorem}

Any operation that meets the synchrony clause sits at balance as soon
as the antecedent is off balance, because the conditional is then
vacuous. Any operation that meets the non-refutation clause sits on
the positive wall as soon as the antecedent is off that wall and the
consequent is on it. The clauses meet when a height other than balance
or the wall implies the wall. A boost-dominant witness is
\(\tfrac12\to_T 1=0\) and \(\tfrac12\to_{\lnot F} 1=1\). Synchrony
lies off the wall, so the two readings cannot share a value.

The G\"odel residuum of \(\min\), with lattice top \(1\), preserves the
top of the height order and reads that top as truth. A boost-dominant
height implying synchrony is sent to balance, in agreement with both
residuals. A rotation-dominant height implying synchrony lies below the
consequent, so the residuum returns the wall. The non-refutation
residual returns balance there.

Both residuals send a boost-dominant height implying synchrony to
balance, and every synchrony residual must. Non-refutation leaves that
successful value free, forbidding only the wall. The status algebra
uses the freedom (Section~\ref{sec:regime}). The two residuals return
the same height precisely when the antecedent is balanced, or sits on
the positive wall, or the consequent is balanced.

A valuation assigns each atom a height in \([-1,1]\). Consequence
follows the two predicates:
\(\Gamma\models_T\psi\) when every valuation that synchronises \(\Gamma\)
synchronises \(\psi\); \(\Gamma\models_{\lnot F}\psi\) is the same
clause with non-refutation. The two relations are not the same.
\(P\wedge\neg P\) is non-refuted under every valuation, so the empty
set non-refutes it; it is synchronous only when \(P\) is, so the empty
set does not \(T\)-entail it. Synchrony of an atom does force
synchrony of \(P\vee\neg P\). Non-refutation does not: the negative
wall is not refuted, and the disjunction of that wall with its swap
is the positive wall.

The interval admits more than one two-valued restriction, and the
names \(\lvert T\rangle\) and \(\lvert F\rangle\) are a different pair
from classical true and false. The \emph{named} fragment keeps the
heights \(0\) and \(1\): the labels \(\lvert T\rangle\) and
\(\lvert F\rangle\). The \emph{wall} fragment keeps the heights \(-1\)
and \(+1\): deepest \(\lvert B\rangle\) and \(\lvert F\rangle\).
They behave differently under negation. On the named fragment,
negation fixes \(0\), and it sends \(1\) to \(-1\), which lies outside
\(\{0,1\}\). The named fragment therefore fails to be a Boolean
algebra, and it fails to be closed. On the walls, negation swaps the
two points and fixes neither. The wall fragment is the faithful
classical core, in the sense fixed in Section~\ref{sec:wall}. A
comparison of D4L with classical true and false that cuts to the names
\(\lvert T\rangle\) and \(\lvert F\rangle\) has already left classical
logic.

\section{Classical proof on the walls}
\label{sec:wall}

Read the positive wall as classical truth and the negative wall as
classical falsity. Negation swaps the two points. Conjunction and
disjunction are \(\min\) and \(\max\), and both preserve the walls, so
a formula whose atoms sit on the walls evaluates on the walls. Under
that reading the wall fragment is a Boolean algebra.

\begin{theorem}[Classical tautologies]
\label{thm:wall}
A formula built from atoms, negation, conjunction, and disjunction is a
classical tautology if and only if every assignment of its atoms to the
two walls returns the positive wall.
\end{theorem}

A classical derivation spends most of its length on identities that do
not use this restriction. Section~\ref{sec:conn} already records that
De~Morgan duality and distributivity hold for every real height. Double
negation and absorption are of the same kind. A proof of any of them by
cases on true and false repeats a computation the height has already
performed. The case split records the specialisation of that identity.

Three heights decide which of these identities hold at every real height.

\begin{theorem}[Three heights]
\label{thm:three}
Two formulas built from atoms, negation, conjunction, and disjunction
take the same value under every real assignment if and only if they do
so whenever every atom lies in \(\{-1,0,1\}\).
\end{theorem}

Conjunction and disjunction each return one argument, and negation
returns the swap, so a formula returns the value of one atom or the
swap of one atom. It creates no magnitude of its own. If two formulas
differ at a real assignment, the larger of the absolute values they
return is a positive threshold. Sending every height of absolute value
strictly below that threshold to balance, and every other height to
the wall of its sign, preserves order and commutes with negation,
conjunction, and disjunction. The formulas still differ after every atom has been moved
to \(-1\), \(0\), or \(1\). Every separation that appears at an
intermediate height already appears among these three. The identity is
an identity of functions, so it remains valid inside any surrounding
formula.

Agreement on the walls is a coarser relation.

\begin{theorem}[Walls do not decide a height identity]
\label{thm:walls-coarse}
\(P\vee\neg P\) and \(Q\vee\neg Q\) return the positive wall under
every assignment of the atoms to the two walls. They differ as heights:
when \(P\) sits at the positive wall and \(Q\) at balance, the first
returns \(1\) and the second returns \(0\).
\end{theorem}

A classical proof may exchange the two excluded middles, because both
sit at the positive wall. Off the walls the exchange compares absolute
values, and that comparison is the saturation recorded below. An
equational step checked on \(-1\), \(0\), and \(1\) holds at every real
height.

The walls contribute saturation.
Section~\ref{sec:conn} records \(j\wedge\neg j=-|j|\) and
\(j\vee\neg j=|j|\). Both equal the corresponding wall if and only if
the height is already a wall. Off the walls the comparison
\(-|j|\le|k|\) remains: the conjunction of any height with its negation
lies at or below the disjunction of any height with its negation.
Excluded middle, and the classical form of non-contradiction, are the
statement \(|j|=1\).

Material implication, read as \(\neg j\vee k\), shows the split on a
syllogism. The formula
\[
(P\to Q)\to\bigl((Q\to R)\to(P\to R)\bigr)
\]
returns the positive wall under every wall assignment. It is not an
identity of the height. At total balance, every atom at \(0\), the same
formula returns \(0\). Synchrony holds. The classical reading does not
call that value truth. One computation, two readings, and they disagree
on whether the packaged inference has succeeded. The formula is a wall
computation: it demands the saturation \(|j|=1\). The De Morgan
skeleton underneath it has already been proved on the height. Read as
a rule, with both premises at the positive wall, the syllogism does
not demand that saturation.

The inference that uses the implication is detachment. Write \(j\to k\)
for \(\max(-j,k)\).

\begin{theorem}[Three cuts]
\label{thm:detach}
For all real heights, \(j=1\) and \(j\to k=1\) imply \(k=1\). The
implication lies strictly below the positive wall if and only if \(-j\)
and \(k\) both do. If \(k\le 0\), then \(0\to k=0\).
\end{theorem}

The first cut is classical detachment, and it holds for every real
height. Whenever the antecedent and the implication both sit at the
positive wall, the consequent sits there with them. The second cut is
non-refutation of the implication. It is non-refutation of the negated
antecedent together with non-refutation of the consequent.
Non-refutation of the antecedent itself is the different inequality
\(j<1\). The two inequalities agree on the open interval \((-1,1)\) and
separate on the walls. The witness is the negative wall implying
balance: \(-1\to 0=1\). Both heights lie strictly below the positive
wall, and the implication is that wall. Classical success and the
failure of non-refutation are one value. The third cut is synchrony.
Balance materially implies every nonpositive height, and the
implication is itself balance. Whenever the consequent is strictly
negative, both premises of detachment are synchronous and the
consequent is not. A classical
proof is written in the implication that keeps all three cuts. The
residuals of Section~\ref{sec:cons} restore one cut at a time, and they
agree only on the locus already named there.

\begin{theorem}[Rules and discharge]
\label{thm:rules}
For all real heights, \(j\to k=1\) and \(k=-1\) imply \(j=-1\);
\(\max(j,k)=1\) and \(j=-1\) imply \(k=1\); and \(j\to k=1\) together
with \(k\to m=1\) imply \(j\to m=1\). A height and its negation are
never both equal to \(1\). Every formula returns \(0\) when every atom
does. On the interval, premises force a conclusion onto the positive
wall under every assignment if and only if they do so whenever every
atom lies in \(\{-1,0,1\}\). On the walls, \(\{\phi\}\) forces \(\psi\)
onto the positive wall if and only if \(\phi\to\psi\) returns that wall
under every wall assignment. The atom \(P\) forces \(P\vee\neg P\) onto
the positive wall, while \(P\to(P\vee\neg P)\) returns \(0\) at balance.
Inside the interval, a formula at the positive wall may be weakened by
any disjunct.
\end{theorem}

Modus tollens, disjunctive syllogism, and the syllogism join detachment.
Each one carries the value \(1\) from its premises to its conclusion
at every real height. A proof that has put those premises on the
positive wall may apply the rule while the remaining heights stay off
the walls.
The syllogism formula packages its rule by discharge. On the walls the
packaging is faithful. A single premise moves into the antecedent of a
material implication, and the resulting formula returns the positive
wall under every wall assignment exactly when the rule does. At balance
the packaged formula returns synchrony, as every formula does. The
premises of the rule sit at the positive wall, and balance is a
different height. The one-premise witness is an atom and its excluded
middle. Whenever the atom is the positive wall, so is the disjunction
with its negation. The implication from the atom to that disjunction
returns synchrony at balance.

A height and its negation are never both the positive wall. The
inference from both to a third height preserves that wall, and it
quantifies over an empty set of valuations. Synchrony is the other
designation. There the same pair can hold, and Section~\ref{sec:ex-efq}
records that the inference to an unrelated atom does not.

An inference preserves the positive wall throughout the interval
exactly when it preserves that wall on \(\{-1,0,1\}\). The collapse
used for equational identity, taken at threshold \(1\), sends every
non-wall height to balance and fixes both walls. It commutes with the
connectives and fixes the value \(1\). A premise at the positive wall
remains there, and a conclusion that arrives there after the collapse
was already there. Inside the interval the bound \(|j|\le 1\) lets a
positive wall be weakened by any disjunct: the max of \(1\) with a
height at most \(1\) is \(1\). A height sitting above the wall, taken
as that disjunct, carries the max above \(1\).

\begin{theorem}[Support and the cut]
\label{thm:cut}
For a finite set of premises, positive-wall forcing on the interval
depends only on the atoms that occur in the premises or the conclusion.
The three-height check is then an assignment of that finite set to
\(\{-1,0,1\}\). The forcing splits in two. One half is classical
consequence, the assignment of those atoms to the two walls. The other
half is the case distinction on assignments that send at least one of
them to balance. An atom that appears there is one the premises do not
fix to a wall. Excluded middle satisfies only the classical half, and
the forcing on the interval fails. Detachment, modus tollens,
disjunctive syllogism, and the syllogism rule satisfy both.
\end{theorem}

Discharge of any finite depth agrees, on the walls, with classical
consequence at that depth. At total balance the packaged formula returns
synchrony, whatever the depth. The one-premise witness is an atom and
its excluded middle. The two-premise witness is the syllogism formula.

The three consequence relations are pairwise incomparable. Synchrony
does not pass from \(\{P,\neg P\}\) to an unrelated atom, and neither
does non-refutation. Positive-wall forcing preserves that inference,
because the two premises cannot both equal \(1\). In the other
direction, synchrony of an atom from its excluded middle is not
positive-wall forcing, and non-refutation of a contradiction is neither
synchrony nor positive-wall forcing.

Where the two implications already agree, detachment of the classical
expansion is that agreement. Every pair on an island agrees, and on the
bits of the island the four rules are the classical ones. A basis ray
and the diagonal separate the two implications, and the expansion does
not detach. No map sends a formula into the subspace lattice.

Explosion has the same shape. The emptiness just recorded is why a
classical proof of it is short: the premise does not occur.

\section{What two-valued logic cannot host}
\label{sec:examples}

Section~\ref{sec:wall} factors a classical proof into a height identity,
decided by the three heights \(-1\), \(0\), and \(1\), a wall saturation,
and the three cuts of detachment. The same section records the rules
that preserve the positive wall at every real height, the cut of that
forcing by the atoms that occur, and discharge at every finite depth,
which agrees with classical consequence on the walls. Two-valued algebras still cannot host the
situations below, and D4L still realises them. The difference is fixed
as a pair: emptiness (or vacuity, or collapse to a singleton) on the
fragment, realisation on D4L.

These are changes of semantics, not new theorems of Peano arithmetic.
Unconditional FLT / Beal and a refutation of G\"odel's theorems are
not claimed.

\subsection{A fixed point of negation}
\label{sec:ex-fp}

Classically, \(P\leftrightarrow\neg P\) has no model. On the wall
fragment the same is true: if \(j\in\{-1,+1\}\) then \(j\neq -j\).
In D4L the condition \(j=\neg j\) is equivalent to \(j=0\). The unique
solution is the label \(\lvert T\rangle\), perfect usual--dual
balance, and it is realised both by a constant valuation and by an
amplitude.

The equation is not the G\"odel sentence. It is the self-referential
\emph{equality} \(P=\neg P\), which an algebra whose negation has no
fixed point cannot host, and which D4L seats uniquely at synchrony.

\subsection{Contradiction without explosion}
\label{sec:ex-efq}

Classical two-valued logic has \emph{ex falso quodlibet}: from
\(\{P,\neg P\}\) every \(Q\) follows. On \(\mathrm{Bool}\) the premise
is already impossible, because \(P\) and \(\neg P\) cannot both be
\(\mathtt{true}\). On the walls, \(j\) and \(\neg j\) cannot both be
\(+1\). Two-valued explosion is therefore vacuous: the premises cannot
be designated together.

In D4L they can. Let \(P\) be atom \(0\) and \(Q\) atom \(1\). Then
\[
\{P,\neg P\}\not\models_T Q,\qquad
\{P,\neg P\}\not\models_{\lnot F} Q.
\]
One witness sends \(P\) to \(0\) and \(Q\) to \(1\): the contradictory
pair is synchronous, the unrelated atom is False. A second witness
sends \(P\) to \(-\tfrac12\) (interior \(\lvert B\rangle\)) and \(Q\)
to \(1\): the pair is non-refuted, the unrelated atom is still False.
The conjunction \(j\wedge\neg j\) is always non-refuted. An
inconsistent pair of observations therefore does not force an unrelated
atom onto the positive wall.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{The constraint \(\Jnorm<1\)}{The constraint Jnorm < 1}}
\label{sec:ex-notF}

A sentence forbidden from sitting at the unique all-boost world is
constrained by \(\mathrm{HoldsNotF}\), that is by \(\Jnorm<1\). On the
named fragment this constraint collapses to \(\mathrm{HoldsT}\): the
only remaining point is \(0\). On D4L the same constraint is inhabited
by three labels, \(\lvert T\rangle\), \(\lvert U\rangle\), and
\(\lvert B\rangle\). Non-refutation is not synchrony. The named
fragment cannot see the difference.

\subsection{Complementarity}
\label{sec:ex-comp}

The subspace lattice of \(\mathbb{C}^2\) is not distributive, hence not
a Boolean algebra. It has at most two pairwise orthogonal atoms. The
four D4L labels are not a PVM. The distributive scalar connectives
cannot present that lattice. Each complementary pair determines a
four-element Boolean island (Section~\ref{sec:block}). That island is
not a Boolean valuation of the lattice, and it is not the four names
of the amplitude layer. The two layers remain two
layers.

\section{Where a classical proof survives in the spinor lattice}
\label{sec:block}

The subspace lattice of \(\mathbb{C}^2\) is not Boolean, so a classical
proof about subspaces is not yet a proof in the dual Hilbert layer. The
part that survives has a geometric name.

The implication carried by the orthomodular law is
\[
A\to B \;=\; A^\perp\vee(A\wedge B).
\]
The zero subspace implies every subspace, and no nonzero vector lies in
the zero subspace. Quantum explosion, like the explosion of
Section~\ref{sec:wall}, is an inference from the bottom of the lattice.

\begin{theorem}[Inclusion, splitting, and detachment]
\label{thm:split}
\(A^\perp\vee(A\wedge B)\) equals the whole space if and only if \(A\) is
contained in \(B\). The following are equivalent: \(A\) splits along
\(B\),
\[
A \;=\; (A\wedge B)\vee(A\wedge B^\perp);
\]
the lattice implication equals the classical expansion \(A^\perp\vee B\);
and the expansion detaches,
\[
A\wedge(A^\perp\vee B)\;\subseteq\; B.
\]
\end{theorem}

An inclusion is the first identity. Splitting is the further condition
that compares the lattice implication with the classical expansion. The
lattice implication sits inside that expansion, because \(A\wedge B\)
sits inside \(B\). Equality is the splitting, and detachment of the
expansion is the same condition. The implication the lattice carries
detaches for every pair: a vector that lies in \(A\) and in
\(A^\perp\vee(A\wedge B)\) already lies in \(A\wedge B\), hence in \(B\).
On a splitting pair the expansion is this implication, so the same
vector lies in \(B\). The relation is symmetric: \(A\) splits along
\(B\) exactly when \(B\) splits along \(A\). A pair that splits lies in
a Boolean block, and inside that block the classical expansion is the
implication the lattice already uses.

Orthogonal rays of a computational basis are such a pair. Each is the
orthogonal of the other, the two implications agree, and both equal the
orthogonal line. The agreed value is that line. A ray sits outside the
orthogonal ray, so the implication stops short of the whole space.

A computational ray and the diagonal do not split. Their meet is zero,
and the ray is not orthogonal to the diagonal, so the decomposition is
the zero subspace. The classical expansion returns the whole space. The
implication carried by the lattice returns only the orthogonal line.
The meet of the ray with the expansion is the ray, and the ray does not
lie in the diagonal, so the expansion does not detach. That pair is the
step at which a two-valued proof leaves the lattice.

\begin{theorem}[Rays and their islands]
\label{thm:island}
A one-dimensional subspace splits along another exactly when the two
are equal or orthogonal. A subspace splits along a ray \(\ell\) exactly
when it lies in the island
\[
\{0,\;\ell,\;\ell^\perp,\;\mathbb{C}^2\}.
\]
That set is closed under meet, join, and orthocomplement. On it, meet
distributes over join, and the lattice implication agrees with the
classical expansion. Splitting along \(\ell\) already splits along
\(\ell^\perp\). Islands of distinct complementary pairs meet only at
\(\{0,\mathbb{C}^2\}\). A subspace splits along every subspace exactly
when it is \(0\) or \(\mathbb{C}^2\). Three distinct rays never split
pairwise.
\end{theorem}

Equality is the trivial split. Orthogonality is the split of a
computational basis, and the failure of orthogonality is the refusal
already seen for the diagonal. In this dimension, the subspaces that
split along one ray are the whole island, so a third ray has nowhere
to sit. A classical expansion that has left the island is the expansion
that failed on the diagonal. The four elements are one measurement
context. They are not the four labels of the amplitude layer.

\begin{theorem}[The block and the four labels]
\label{thm:blockfour}
Orthocomplement fixes no subspace. A subspace that splits along two
others distributes over their join. When that subspace is a ray, both
others lie in its island and split along each other. The two atoms of
the island are incomparable under inclusion, so the island does not
embed into the height order. Reading the four elements as bits, with
\((0,0)\) the zero subspace, \((1,0)\) the ray, \((0,1)\) its
orthogonal, and \((1,1)\) the whole space, orthocomplement negates both
bits. Any assignment of heights in \([-1,1]\) to the four bit-pairs
that carries this negation to sign flip misses at least one label. On
the island an element meets its orthocomplement at zero. On the height
a value meets its sign flip at zero only at balance.
\end{theorem}

A distributive step whose distributing subspace is a ray therefore stays
inside one island, and the other two subspaces split along each other.
The four elements are the truth table of one complementary measurement.
Their order has width two. The height order is total, and sign flip
fixes the balanced label. The four names remain the sign and the
saturation of one scalar.

\section{Four turns, one label}
\label{sec:turns}

A graviton, in this reading, is one pure dual step of angle \(2\pi/N\)
about an axis transverse to a computational eigenstate. Aligned
gravitons add that angle. The torsion a configuration expresses is the
admissible foot of the sum. Once the angle has met a right angle, the
foot is the pure dual corner of that axis, and the normalised height
locks at \(-\frac13\).

The rotor that carries the eigenstate continues past the corner. On a
circle divisible by four, four counts stand at the lock: a quarter of
the circle, a half, three quarters, and a full turn.

\begin{theorem}[Four turns, one label]
\label{thm:turns}
Let \(4\mid N\) and \(N\ge 4\). Each of the four counts expresses the
height \(-\frac13\), and the label is \(\lvert B\rangle\). Each of those
heights is non-refuted, and none is synchronous. The half turn occupies
the orthocomplement of the computational ray. The full turn returns to
the ray. Those two rays are the atoms of its island. The quarter turn
and the three-quarter turn occupy two further rays, outside the island,
and along the computational ray the classical expansion does not detach
onto either. The configuration with no graviton expresses the height
\(0\), the label \(\lvert T\rangle\), on the ray of the full turn.
\end{theorem}

The computational device records another number, the deficit of the
Born weight of the original outcome. On the four turns that deficit is
\(\frac12\), \(1\), \(\frac12\), and \(0\). Read in \([-1,1]\), the four
numbers are the labels \(\lvert U\rangle\), \(\lvert F\rangle\),
\(\lvert U\rangle\), and \(\lvert T\rangle\). The half turn is the
positive wall of this reading and the label \(\lvert B\rangle\) of the
height. The full turn is synchrony of this reading and the label
\(\lvert B\rangle\) of the height. The two quarter turns share the
deficit \(\frac12\) and occupy distinct rays. The expressed height, the
device deficit, and the ray are three observables. The integer \(N\)
stays unfixed, and the deficit is not a laboratory rate.

\section{Proof status and Diophantine regimes}
\label{sec:regime}

A DST proof artefact---a core lemma, a live bridge, a refuted
diagnostic, a bookkeeping identity---is not a torsion configuration.
It carries a discrete status that reuses the four names, but the
observable is not \(\Jnorm\). On this carrier, negation \emph{is} a
function of the four labels, and implication is a table, not the
residuum of \(\min\).

Conjunction and disjunction are \(\min\) and \(\max\) of the amplitude
representatives \(\lvert T\rangle\mapsto 0\), \(\lvert U\rangle\mapsto 1/2\),
\(\lvert F\rangle\mapsto 1\), \(\lvert B\rangle\mapsto -1/2\). Label
negation classifies the sign flip of those representatives. The
representative of \(\lvert F\rangle\) is \(1\), and \(-1\) lies in
\(\lvert B\rangle\); the algebra stores that label, whose own
representative is \(-\tfrac12\). In particular \(F\wedge\neg F=B\). A second operation, the establishedness
meet, packages two artefacts along \(F<B<U<T\), so that a proved core
with a live bridge is still live: the meet of \(T\) and \(U\) is \(U\).
Implication is
\[
\begin{aligned}
T\to\phi&=\phi,\\
F\to\phi&=T,\\
U\to T&=U,\\
B\to T&=U.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last two clauses are the point. The implication is refuted exactly
when a non-refuted status implies a refuted one, so the table
residuates non-refutation. An open or conflicted status implying a
theorem stays off \(T\). Every synchrony residual on the height would
have placed the open clause at balance. No Belnap-FOUR
isomorphism is claimed.
Designated predicates on statuses reuse the same two names:
\(\mathrm{HoldsT}_R\) means status \(T\);
\(\mathrm{HoldsNotF}_R\) means status \(\ne F\).

This is the algebra in which DST records what it has proved and what it
has not. Named two-valued logic \(\{T,F\}\) cannot host a live status
\(U\). The wall fragment \(\{B,F\}\) cannot host a proved core \(T\).
D4L realises the assignment core \(T\), diagnostic \(F\), live \(U\),
conjecture \(U\). The core alone does not \(T\)-entail the conjecture.
A window \(T\) together with an ill-posed NoGo \(B\) does not force the
conjecture off \(F\). The implication \(U\to T\) is not designated.

The same algebra hosts a Beal residual atlas: closed slices (including
the Fermat and Darmon--Merel positions, and the signature \((n,n,5)\))
sit at \(T\); refuted diagnostics sit at \(F\); bookkeeping identities
sit at \(B\); live residuals and the classical conjecture sit at \(U\).
The closed slices do not \(T\)-entail classical Beal. Even permutations
of the Darmon--Merel and \((n,n,5)\) signatures stay in the even
two-equal difference residual when the common exponent is not divisible
by three: an even exponent supplies no sign that would carry the
equation onto Darmon--Merel or \((n,n,5)\) form. Divisibility of that
common exponent by three is a separate closure, and \((5,6,6)\) is
again a coprime sum of two cubes. Mihăilescu's consecutive-power theorem closes a
coefficient condition without closing the exponent shape that hosts it.
The status board does not encode the arithmetic assembly of the
residuals. Forcing every named artefact to \(T\) still fails to
\(T\)-entail the conjecture; promoting a single live residual from
\(U\) to \(T\) likewise cannot claim it. The exponent-shape classifier
lands in \(\{T,U,F\}\) and never records bookkeeping \(B\).
A dual-axis Fermat atlas likewise places the additive core, the real
\(L^p\) dichotomy, and the elliptic finite sandwich of \(N_1\) at \(T\),
demoted single-axis modular and balanced-seed diagnostics at \(F\), and
the mixed-motor residual together with classical FLT at \(U\). Closed
Fermat slices do not \(T\)-entail classical FLT.

This is why DST must not treat a single bound, or a refuted diagnostic,
as entailing the seven classical conjectures. The regime layer records
that prohibition. It does not prove any unproved bridge, and it does
not claim unconditional FLT, Beal, or abc.

Additive motors and multiplicative amplitudes occupy distinct seats at
the same label \(\lvert T\rangle\). Pure translation has vanishing
torsion and is vacuum. A balanced massive configuration is not vacuum.
The mixed product \(R\cdot T\) is not identified with
\(\exp(\Omega_{\mathrm{biv}})\) when the factors fail to commute. The
balanced residual class excludes window seeds: a half-window
configuration is not a balanced residual.

On the finite torus the same four names acquire a discrete reading.
When \(4\nmid N\) the sharp discrete bound is strict, so no admissible
point saturates \(\lvert F\rangle\). When \(4\mid N\) both walls are
attained: the all-boost lattice point realises \(\lvert F\rangle\), and
the all-rotation lattice point realises deepest \(\lvert B\rangle\). Pure-boost scaling multiplies \(\Jnorm\) by \(k^2\).
Synchrony is a fixed point. A label \(\lvert U\rangle\) can be driven
onto \(\lvert F\rangle\), or out of the admissible cone, where collapse
is undefined. That is the logical reading of the shared no-go; it is
not new arithmetic.

Vacuum is a fixed point of every admissible scaling. A small
balanced-massive seed stays at \(\lvert T\rangle\) with mass
\(k^2 M\) and may remain admissible; a large balanced seed leaves the
cone. That cone exit is invisible to the signed-height classifier:
\(\Jnorm\) stays \(0\), so the four-state label is still
\(\lvert T\rangle\). Mass and the cone predicate are the observables
that see it. This is why a Beal balanced seed \(\theta=\log 2/m\) can
survive \(m\)-fold scaling inside the cone.

Height and modular winding are two measurements. Real-scale
admissibility and nonzero winding cannot hold together. That
incompatibility is a separation of observables, not a Both-status on
one observable.

\section{A reading of self-reference}
\label{sec:godel}

Let \(G\) be the G\"odel sentence \cite{Godel1931}: ``\(G\) is not
provable in the system.'' In D4L, provability is read as a drive toward synchrony
\(\Jnorm\to 0\), i.e.\ toward \(\lvert T\rangle\). The honest constraint
on a sentence forbidden from sitting at the unique all-boost world is
exactly \(\mathrm{HoldsNotF}\) of Section~\ref{sec:ex-notF}:
\[
\Jnorm(G)<1.
\]
That constraint leaves \(G\) in \(\lvert T\rangle\), \(\lvert U\rangle\),
or \(\lvert B\rangle\) as labels, or at \(\lvert T\rangle\) if one
further demands synchrony. Conjunction does not explode
(Section~\ref{sec:ex-efq}).

This is a change of semantics, not a proof that G\"odel's theorems fail
in classical arithmetic. Completeness talk over the negative-torsion
sector is likewise a reading of admissible rotors, not a completion of
the Hilbert programme inside first-order Peano arithmetic. The content
of Section~\ref{sec:examples} is fixed; the reading in this section is
not a new incompleteness theorem.
