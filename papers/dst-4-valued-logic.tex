Four Truth Values from the Smallest Admissible Torus \(N=4\)}

\begin{abstract}
We introduce D4L (Dual Spacetime 4-Valued Logic) by starting from the smallest
discrete dual-spacetime model in which the four logical states are actually
attained. Dual Spacetime Theory encodes every massive configuration by six
rapidity ticks on a torus \((\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^6\). The compactification
\(N=4\) is the first value for which the anti-synchronisation cutoff
\(\pi/2\) is itself a lattice point. After discarding non-admissible ticks,
only \(3^3=27\) configurations remain. Their normalized torsion
\(\Jnorm\) takes the seven values
\(-1,-2/3,-1/3,0,1/3,2/3,+1\), which partition into the four D4L states
\(\lvert T\rangle\), \(\lvert U\rangle\), \(\lvert F\rangle\), \(\lvert B\rangle\).

Truth values are therefore not an extra semantic layer: they are the sign and
saturation of a height already present in the algebra. The continuous bound
\(|\Jnorm|\le 1\) on admissible configurations, and the fact that \(N=4\)
attains both extremes, are machine-checked in Lean~4. The remainder of the
note reads the G\"odel sentence against this four-state lattice.
\end{abstract}

\section{How to read this note}

A first-time reader need not know Dual Spacetime Theory, Clifford algebras, or
paraconsistent logic in advance. The only geometric picture required is this:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item Every configuration has \emph{three spatial axes}.
  \item On each axis there are two angles: a usual-sector boost \(\alpha_a\)
        and a dual-sector rotation \(\beta_a\).
  \item Those six angles are not continuous at the outset. They sit on a
        finite clock with \(N\) hours, \(N=4\) being the smallest clock that
        already contains the physically allowed maximum \(\pi/2\).
\end{itemize}

Section~\ref{sec:n4} works entirely with this clock. The four truth values
appear as a grouping of the \(27\) allowed settings. Later sections translate
the same grouping into the continuous language of the companion papers
\cite{DST2025,Diophantine2026} and then interpret self-reference.

\section{The smallest model: \texorpdfstring{\(N=4\)}{N=4}}
\label{sec:n4}

\subsection{Six ticks on a four-hour clock}

Discretize the six rapidities by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:n4-embed}
\alpha_a = \frac{2\pi n_a}{4} = \frac{\pi n_a}{2},\qquad
\beta_a  = \frac{2\pi m_a}{4} = \frac{\pi m_a}{2},
\qquad a=1,2,3,
\end{equation}
with integers \(n_a,m_a\in\{0,1,2,3\}\). The full torus therefore has
\(4^6=4096\) points. Most of them are \emph{not} used as logical states.

\begin{definition}[Admissible configuration]
\label{def:admissible}
A configuration is \emph{admissible} when, on every axis,
\[
\alpha_a\ge 0,\qquad \beta_a\ge 0,\qquad \alpha_a+\beta_a\le \frac{\pi}{2}.
\]
Equivalently, in the integer labels of \eqref{eq:n4-embed},
\[
n_a+m_a\le 1.
\]
\end{definition}

The cutoff \(\pi/2\) is the anti-synchronisation bound of the dual map: usual
and dual rapidities on one axis are not allowed to add past a right angle.
For \(N=4\) that cutoff is exactly one tick, which is why \(N=4\) is the
natural first model. For \(N=3\) the same cutoff is not a lattice point, and
the extremes \(\Jnorm=\pm 1\) are never attained \cite{Diophantine2026}.

\subsection{Three allowed ticks per axis}

The inequality \(n_a+m_a\le 1\) leaves only three pairs per axis:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cccccl}
\toprule
\((n_a,m_a)\) & \((\alpha_a,\beta_a)\) & \(\alpha_a^2-\beta_a^2\) & sign \(e_a\) & name \\
\midrule
\((0,0)\) & \((0,0)\) & \(0\) & \(0\) & rest \\
\((1,0)\) & \((\pi/2,\,0)\) & \(+(\pi/2)^2\) & \(+1\) & pure boost \\
\((0,1)\) & \((0,\,\pi/2)\) & \(-(\pi/2)^2\) & \(-1\) & pure rotation \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Ticks with \(n_a\ge 2\) or \(m_a\ge 2\), and the mixed tick \((1,1)\), lie
outside the admissible cone. Three independent axes therefore give
\[
3^3 = 27
\]
admissible worlds, and nothing else.

\subsection{Height of a configuration}

The raw torsional scalar of Dual Spacetime Theory is the elementary quadratic
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:J-raw}
J = \frac12\sum_{a=1}^3\bigl(\alpha_a^2-\beta_a^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
On an admissible configuration one has \(|J|\le\Jbound\), with equality only
when every axis is the same extremal tick. The \emph{normalized} height
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Jnorm}
\Jnorm := \frac{8}{3\pi^2}\,J
\end{equation}
rescales that ceiling to \(1\). Substituting the \(N=4\) ticks
\eqref{eq:n4-embed} collapses both formulae to an integer count:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:n4-height}
J = \Jbound\cdot\frac{e_1+e_2+e_3}{3},\qquad
\Jnorm = \frac{e_1+e_2+e_3}{3},\qquad
e_a\in\{-1,0,+1\}.
\end{equation}
Thus \(\Jnorm\) on the \(N=4\) lattice is just the average of three votes,
each vote being ``boost'', ``rest'', or ``rotation''.

\begin{example}[Worked values]
\label{ex:n4-work}
The all-rest vote \((0,0,0)\) gives \(\Jnorm=0\).
The all-boost vote \((+1,+1,+1)\) gives \(\Jnorm=+1\) and raw height \(J=\Jbound\).
The all-rotation vote \((-1,-1,-1)\) gives \(\Jnorm=-1\).
A single boost \((+1,0,0)\) gives \(\Jnorm=1/3\).
\end{example}

The same two extremes are the \(N=4\) instances of the general lattice points
that Lean records as \texttt{pureHyperbolicDiscrete} and
\texttt{pureEllipticDiscrete} \cite{Diophantine2026}.

\subsection{The seven attainable heights}

Because \(e_1+e_2+e_3\) ranges through \(\{-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3\}\), the
admissible \(N=4\) torus realises exactly seven heights. Their multiplicities
are the number of ways to write each sum with three votes in \(\{-1,0,+1\}\):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{crrl}
\toprule
\(\Jnorm\) & \(\#\) worlds & \(J\) & D4L state \\
\midrule
\(+1\)     & \(1\)  & \(+\Jbound\) & \(\lvert F\rangle\) False \\
\(+2/3\)   & \(3\)  & \(+\frac23\Jbound\) & \(\lvert U\rangle\) Undetermined \\
\(+1/3\)   & \(6\)  & \(+\frac13\Jbound\) & \(\lvert U\rangle\) Undetermined \\
\(0\)      & \(7\)  & \(0\) & \(\lvert T\rangle\) True \\
\(-1/3\)   & \(6\)  & \(-\frac13\Jbound\) & \(\lvert B\rangle\) Both \\
\(-2/3\)   & \(3\)  & \(-\frac23\Jbound\) & \(\lvert B\rangle\) Both \\
\(-1\)     & \(1\)  & \(-\Jbound\) & \(\lvert B\rangle\) Both (deepest) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The seven cardinalities sum to \(1+3+6+7+6+3+1=27\). No admissible
\(N=4\) point produces any other value, and none violates \(|\Jnorm|\le 1\).

\subsection{Four logical states as a partition}

\begin{definition}[D4L states on \(N=4\)]
\label{def:d4l-n4}
Group the twenty-seven worlds by the sign and saturation of \(\Jnorm\):
\begin{align*}
\lvert T\rangle &\colon \Jnorm = 0
  &&\text{\(7\) worlds: perfect usual--dual balance,}\\
\lvert U\rangle &\colon 0 < \Jnorm < 1
  &&\text{\(9\) worlds: boost-dominant, not saturated,}\\
\lvert F\rangle &\colon \Jnorm = +1
  &&\text{\(1\) world: all three axes pure boost,}\\
\lvert B\rangle &\colon -1\le \Jnorm < 0
  &&\text{\(10\) worlds: rotation-dominant, including the unique}\\
&&& \text{all-rotation point \(\Jnorm=-1\).}
\end{align*}
\end{definition}

Two asymmetries are already visible and should not be papered over.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item The positive extreme is a singleton named False; the negative extreme
        is kept inside Both. That is a logical convention, not a gap in the
        lattice: both extremes exist and are unique.
  \item Mixed extrema --- one axis boost, another rotation --- never reach
        \(|\Jnorm|=1\). For instance \((+1,+1,-1)\) has \(\Jnorm=1/3\) and
        sits in \(\lvert U\rangle\). Saturation requires \emph{every} axis
        to vote the same way.
\end{enumerate}

This is the whole discrete semantics. The next section only says the same
thing in the continuous notation used by the gravitational papers.

\section{From the \texorpdfstring{\(N=4\)}{N=4} clock to the continuous bound}
\label{sec:continuous}

\begin{definition}[Torsion scalar]
\label{def:J}
For a rotor pair \(R=R_{\rm usual}R_{\rm dual}\) set
\(\Omega=R_{\rm usual}^\dagger R_{\rm dual}\) and write \(\Omega_{\rm biv}\)
for a principal logarithm. The parameter form used throughout this note is
\eqref{eq:J-raw}, equivalently
\[
J=\frac12\sum_{a=1}^3(\alpha_a^2-\beta_a^2),
\]
together with the normalization \eqref{eq:Jnorm}. The combination
\(\frac1{16}B(\Omega_{\rm biv},\Omega_{\rm biv})\) appearing in older drafts
differs from \eqref{eq:J-raw} by a constant factor under the standard
generator expansion; boundedness statements here always refer to \(J\) and
\(\Jnorm\) \cite{Diophantine2026}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Torsion bound]
\label{thm:torsion-bound}
On the admissible cone of Definition~\ref{def:admissible},
\[
|J|\le\Jbound,\qquad |\Jnorm|\le 1.
\]
Equality \(|\Jnorm|=1\) holds if and only if every axis is pure boost, or
every axis is pure rotation. The image of \(\Jnorm\) on the admissible cone
is the whole interval \([-1,1]\). The bound is false if one keeps only the
principal-branch condition \(|\alpha_a+\beta_a|\le\pi/2\) and allows negative
rapidities.
\end{theorem}

For a general compactification \(N\) the discrete bound is sharper
\cite{Diophantine2026}:
\[
|\Jnorm|\le\Bigl(\frac{4\lfloor N/4\rfloor}{N}\Bigr)^2.
\]
When \(4\mid N\) --- in particular when \(N=4\) --- the right-hand side is
\(1\) and both signs are attained. When \(4\nmid N\) every admissible point
satisfies the strict inequality \(|\Jnorm|<1\), so the states
\(\lvert F\rangle\) and deepest \(\lvert B\rangle\) are missing. That is the
precise sense in which \(N=4\) is the smallest complete logical clock.

\begin{definition}[Truth value]
\label{def:truth}
The truth value of a proposition \(p\) is
\(v(p):=\Jnorm(R_p)\in[-1,1]\), where \(R_p\) is a rotor (or a finite
ensemble of rotors) encoding the proof data of \(p\). On the \(N=4\)
clock this is exactly the average \eqref{eq:n4-height}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Logical operations]
\[
\neg p = -v(p),\qquad
p\wedge q = \min\bigl(v(p),v(q)\bigr),\qquad
p\vee q = \max\bigl(v(p),v(q)\bigr).
\]
Negation is the usual--dual swap \((\alpha_a,\beta_a)\mapsto(\beta_a,\alpha_a)\),
which sends \(J\mapsto -J\) and therefore \(\Jnorm\mapsto-\Jnorm\). In
particular it exchanges \(\lvert F\rangle\) with deepest \(\lvert B\rangle\)
and fixes \(\lvert T\rangle\).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
An earlier draft wrote \(\neg p=1-p\) after identifying truth values with a
raw scalar already forced into \([0,1]\). That convention collides with the
signed interval \([-1,1]\) of \(\Jnorm\). The dual-swap negation above is the
one induced by the algebra.
\end{remark}

\section{The G\"odel sentence on the four-state lattice}

Let \(G\) be the G\"odel sentence: ``\(G\) is not provable in the system.''
Classically this is a paradox. In D4L we read provability as a drive toward
synchrony \(\Jnorm\to 0\), i.e.\ toward \(\lvert T\rangle\).

\begin{theorem}[G\"odel reading in D4L]
\label{thm:godel-eq}
If one pretends that ``not provable'' is an affine complement inside a
one-sided interval, one is led to a midpoint \(\Jnorm(G)=1/2\). That point
is \emph{not} an \(N=4\) lattice value; the nearest undetermined ticks are
\(\Jnorm=1/3\) and \(\Jnorm=2/3\), both in \(\lvert U\rangle\). Under the
full signed dynamics the honest constraint is only
\[
\Jnorm(G)<1
\]
(``not the unique all-boost world \(\lvert F\rangle\)'').
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Three attractors, one forbidden wall]
\label{thm:attractors}
A minimal flow compatible with the Killing sign asymmetry is
\[
\frac{d\Jnorm}{dt}=-\sin(\pi\,\Jnorm).
\]
Its rest points on \([-1,1]\) are \(\Jnorm=0\) (\(\lvert T\rangle\),
classically true) and the endpoints \(\Jnorm=\pm 1\). The open interval
\((0,1)\) is undetermined and, under this flow, unstable toward
\(\lvert T\rangle\). The half-interval \([-1,0)\) is rotation-dominant and
attracting toward deepest \(\lvert B\rangle\). The wall \(\Jnorm=+1\) is
admissible as a lattice point but is not an interior attractor of the
negative-torsion basin.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The quadratic \eqref{eq:J-raw} receives a positive contribution from each
boost tick and a negative contribution from each rotation tick. Self-reference
that is forbidden from sitting at \(\lvert F\rangle\) is driven toward the
rotation side until compactness stops it at \(\Jnorm=-1\). On the \(N=4\)
clock this is exactly the unique all-rotation world. The flow itself is a
modelling choice, not a Lean theorem; the census of the twenty-seven worlds
and the bound \(|\Jnorm|\le 1\) are.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
Read in D4L, the sentence \(G\) is not required to choose between ``true''
and ``false''. It is allowed to occupy \(\lvert B\rangle\): both, in the
precise sense of a rotation-dominant admissible height.
\end{corollary}

\section{Completeness talk, with the lattice in view}

\begin{theorem}[Interpretation over the negative-torsion sector]
\label{thm:hilbert}
Let \(\mathcal{S}\) be a formal system containing Peano arithmetic, and
interpret sentences of \(\mathcal{S}\) as admissible rotors. In the sector
\(\Jnorm<0\) every such sentence receives one of the ten \(N=4\) labels
that make up \(\lvert B\rangle\), or a continuous interpolation thereof for
larger \(N\). Conjunction does not explode: two negative heights remain
negative. This is a semantics for \(\mathcal{S}\), not a proof that the
classical Hilbert programme is completed inside first-order Peano arithmetic.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Self-referential sentences are excluded from \(\lvert F\rangle\) by
\cref{thm:godel-eq} and therefore land in \(\lvert U\rangle\) or
\(\lvert B\rangle\). The signed negation of \cref{sec:continuous} sends a
negative height to a positive one and back, so \(p\wedge\neg p\) is the
minimum of a height and its opposite, hence non-positive, and never forces
every sentence to \(\lvert F\rangle\). Decidability on \(N=4\) is literal
finiteness: twenty-seven worlds.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Larger \(N\) with \(4\mid N\) refine the same four bins by inserting more
interior heights, still bounded by \(|\Jnorm|\le 1\). The four names do not
multiply. That is why the logic is four-valued rather than \(N\)-valued.
\end{remark}

\section{What \texorpdfstring{\(N=4\)}{N=4} does and does not show}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item It \emph{does} exhibit four named states as a partition of a finite,
        completely listed admissible set.
  \item It \emph{does} attain both walls \(\Jnorm=\pm 1\), which no
        \(N\not\equiv 0\pmod{4}\) can do.
  \item It \emph{does} make negation geometric: swap boost ticks with
        rotation ticks.
  \item It does \emph{not}, by itself, prove G\"odel's theorems false in
        classical arithmetic. The D4L reading is a change of semantics.
  \item It does \emph{not} identify raw \(J\) with a value in \([-1,1]\).
        The interval \([-1,1]\) belongs to \(\Jnorm\); the raw ceiling is
        \(\Jbound\).
\end{itemize}

The continuum hypothesis, consciousness, and large-language-model
hallucinations are not consequences of the \(N=4\) census. They remain
speculative glosses on the same signed height.
